#### Ideas: NOAA CPO FY2027 SI/ML Innovation

**Key call rules and evaluation criteria.** Up to 15 awards, most up to $250,000 per year for 1–3 years, no cost share. A letter of intent (at most 2 pages) is strongly encouraged and due by email by 11:59 p.m. PT on **2026-11-17**; CPO answers within four weeks. Full applications are due **2027-02-09**; the earliest start date is 2027-09-01 ([NOFO](https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/df29db0f-809b-40ad-9cbe-c7954b9e5cfd/attachments/2aa9ad30-0dde-4761-956c-9637c045a2c6/Foa_Content_of_NOAA-OAR-CPO-2027-33392.pdf)). Scoring: technical/scientific merit 50%, importance/relevance 25% (including the PI's data-sharing record and plan), qualifications 15%, costs 10%; proposals below 3.0 of 5 are not considered. Duplication with projects "funded or considered for funding by NOAA/Federal agencies" is a selection factor, and double dipping is not allowed. Every priority area requires domain-interpretable SI/ML results, NOAA data, public SI/ML-ready data, and NOAA partner engagement backed by letters of support. **PA3a** asks teams to "identify extreme heat thresholds for hazards to people and property, and model the complex interactions unleashed by extreme heat across U.S. systems and infrastructure", with examples such as "soil moisture data, in combination with water scarcity, power outages, health and air quality indicators, and infrastructure stressors"; PA3 proposals must use NOAA data and "engage with NIHHIS partner agencies." **PA3b** (forecast-of-opportunity windows) must explain physical sources of predictability and test NOAA forecast systems. **PA1** requires 2–10 year prediction or emulators of NOAA models such as SPEAR; **PA2** requires socio-economic methods. An eRA Commons account is needed (2–4 weeks for first-time registration). PA3a is the only area where Hao Lab can credibly lead. **Recommendation: one proposal, N1, with N2 and N3 folded in as aims.**

##### N1. Interpretable SI/ML thresholds for heat-driven cascades in the Tennessee Valley energy-water system — Pursue (lead)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao (PI; heatwave hydrology, flash drought, soil moisture, data-center water). Shi Jin (from Spring 2027; ML pipeline and data engineering: PyTorch, LSTM, YOLO/EfficientNet, a 350K-record ETL and retrieval pipeline, infrared + LiDAR fusion for substation monitoring). Yifan Gao (geospatial analysis, load-growth scenarios). Mingzhou Jin (UT ISE/ISSE, listed collaborator; possible decision-support advisor, role unconfirmed). **Needed, all unconfirmed:** a power-systems or grid-data co-investigator (none in the talent pool); a NOAA collaborator (e.g., an NWS forecast office serving Tennessee or the Southeast Regional Climate Center); a NIHHIS partner-agency contact; a TDEC letter of support (TDEC is named in the 104b grant title, but no letter is documented); TVA or utility data access; optionally a heat-health partner. The ORNL RiTHyMs and UT ReForM river-temperature groups would be natural data partners; they are not in the talent pool.

**Research question and approach.** At what combinations of air temperature, soil-moisture/flash-drought state, river temperature, and electric load do heat impacts begin to cascade (thermoelectric derates and curtailments → load peaks and outages → water-supply stress)? How well does NOAA heat information signal these thresholds in advance, and how does projected data-center load growth shift them? Interpretable ML (gradient boosting with SHAP; monotone or threshold-detecting models) learns joint thresholds for three outcomes, benchmarked against logistic and segmented regression. Inputs: NOAA nClimGrid, URMA/RTMA and NWS heat products; satellite soil moisture; USGS stream temperature; EIA-923/860 plant data; EIA/DOE outage records. Existing river-temperature forecasts (RiTHyMs or ReForM outputs) or simple physics enter as inputs rather than being rebuilt. Deliverables: a public SI/ML-ready threshold dataset, a NOAA Learning Journeys notebook, and threshold briefings co-produced with TDEC and the NIHHIS partner. To keep hazards to people visible, include thresholds at which heat-driven outages cut off cooling for residents.

**Closest existing projects and papers, and the gap.**
- River-temperature ML for TVA exists: ORNL's RiTHyMs, an LSTM model (about 1.1 °C mean absolute error) being applied to TVA-managed rivers ([ORNL news](https://www.ornl.gov/news/ai-model-delivers-river-temperature-insights-strengthening-us-energy-security); [HPCwire 2026-01-15](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/01/15/ornls-sean-turner-on-using-ai-to-bridge-river-models-power-grid-operations/)), and UT Knoxville's hybrid ReForM forecast for TVA ([UT CEE](https://cee.utk.edu/tce-group-develops-novel-forecasting-model-to-predict-river-temperature/)). Both known only from search snippets (bot check on the pages); scope unverified.
- National interpretable-ML outage vulnerability exists but does not identify heat thresholds: Ma, Li, Omitaomu and Mostafavi (Applied Energy, 2025), a county-level Power System Vulnerability Index from about 179 million outage records, 2014–2023 ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/appene/v397y2025ics0306261925010906.html); [ORNL record](https://www.ornl.gov/publications/establishing-nationwide-power-system-vulnerability-index-across-us-counties-using-interpretable-machine-learning-2/)).
- Quoted-phrase NSF Award API searches for "power outage" + "heat", "extreme heat" + "threshold", "thermoelectric" + "water temperature", and "data center" + "extreme heat" returned no awards ([example query](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22data%20center%22%20%22extreme%20heat%22)).
- External evidence that the hazard is real here: Browns Ferry's 90 °F discharge limit; heat-related derates and an August 2007 unit shutdown ([Climate Central](https://climatecentral.org/news/in-tennessee-heat-waves-frustrate-nuclear-power); [Inside Climate News 2011](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/20110414/southeast-extreme-heat-growing-concern-nuclear-power-operators); [APR 2007](https://www.apr.org/2007-08-17/heat-and-drought-shut-down-tva-reactor); [NRC document](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1828/ML18283B708.pdf)). Sources date from 2007–2011; current permit terms not verified.
- **Gap:** prior work forecasts one link (river temperature) or ranks general outage vulnerability. No source checked identifies joint heat, soil-moisture, cooling-water, load, and outage thresholds at which cascades begin, triggered by NOAA heat information, with data-center load growth as a new stressor.

**Novelty.** Real, provided the project uses RiTHyMs/ReForM outputs instead of competing with them: threshold identification and interaction modeling across a coupled chain, which is what PA3a asks for.

**Why us.**
- Funded regional energy-water decision support (PI): USGS 104b "Decision Support for TDEC to Reduce Freshwater Withdrawals and Thermal Discharges from Data Centers and Small Modular Reactors in Tennessee" ($84,000, from 01/2027); UT ISSE "The Local Footprint of AI Data Centers: Noise, Water, and Land-Market Tradeoffs in Tennessee" ($60,000, from 08/2026).
- Heat and soil-moisture science: heatwave effects on CONUS hydrology ([J. Hydrol. 637, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2024.131368)); flash-drought WUE and recovery ([Agric. For. Meteorol. 378, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2025.110982)); soil-moisture controls review ([npj Clim. Atmos. Sci. 8, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41612-024-00888-8)).
- Power-grid interaction: Mao, Hao et al., "Wildfire–Power Grid Interactions" ([RSER 232, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2026.116817)).
- Energy-water screening: K. Jin and Y. Hao, "Regional screening of electricity-related water consumption for projected data-center load growth" (Water Resources and Industry, under review).
- Students' Tennessee Valley siting papers in progress (Shi Jin: tariff- and moratorium-aware siting; Yifan Gao: robust siting under interconnection-queue uncertainty).
- AI workforce: PI teaches IE 591 AI for Engineer Managers and IE 465/565 Applied Data Science.
- Why others have not: river-temperature groups focus on operational forecasting; outage-vulnerability groups work nationally without hydro-climate drivers.

**Fundability.** Matches PA3a's threshold-and-interaction language almost line by line; NOAA data is the trigger; the public dataset and notebook meet data and education requirements; about $200–250K per year for 3 years funds two students, PI summer salary, and a small partner subaward. **Weakest criterion: technical merit (50%)** — the talent pool shows no ML-for-prediction publications, so a co-investigator with a power-systems or ML-for-climate record and a clear benchmark design are needed to clear the 3.0 floor.

**Risks.** (1) LOI due in 17 days with no NOAA, NIHHIS, or utility partner confirmed. (2) UT's eRA Commons registration and the PI's account unverified. (3) Outage data (OE-417, EAGLE-I) may be too coarse for county-scale thresholds. (4) Reviewers may read it as energy-sector work rather than heat hazards to people. (5) Overlap with the 104b grant and 2026-10-21 ideas C2, K1, E1, E2, U1/U2 must be partitioned (see below). (6) RiTHyMs/ReForM may cover more than the snippets suggest.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Send the LOI by 2026-11-17 under PA3a to the PA3 program contacts listed in the NOFO (NIHHIS, MAPP, AdSci). Before the full application: confirm RiTHyMs/ReForM scope, recruit a power-systems co-investigator, secure a NOAA collaborator and NIHHIS-partner engagement, and request a TDEC letter. Treat the 104b grant as preliminary work and data access, not a deliverable.

##### N2. Compound heat and flash-drought thresholds for Southeastern agriculture and rural water supply — Revise (module of N1)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao, Yifan Gao (geospatial). Needed, unconfirmed: an agricultural or extension partner, crop/livestock outcome data, small-water-system data.

**Approach.** Apply N1's threshold engine to a second sector: at what soil-moisture-conditioned heat levels do humid Southeastern crops and small water systems cross into loss, using NOAA heat products?

**Closest existing work and gap.** Flash-drought AI is already funded: NSF 2616278/2616279 (UCLA and Ohio State, from 2026-08-15; $502,520 + $390,000), an interpretable multi-task deep-learning flash-drought predictor using soil moisture and heat ([NSF API 2616278](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2616278.json?printFields=id,title,abstractText)); also EAGER 2435046, CAREER 2144293 (Auburn), 2414601, 2303457/2303458, and 2406295/2445027 ([NSF API "flash drought"](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22flash%20drought%22)). Compound heat-drought events outweigh single events for maize yield (Bogenreuther et al. 2025; region not verified) ([PDF](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/8863/1/1-s2.0-S0168192325004551-main.pdf)); a Carolinas/Georgia random-forest study linked drought, not heat, to yields ([EGU24-2908](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-2908.html)). The remaining gap is narrow: soil-moisture-conditioned heat-impact thresholds for humid Southeastern agriculture and rural water built from NOAA heat information.

**Why us.** PI is Co-PI on the FFAR grant "A multi-source remote sensing-based framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods under climate change" ($434,038, from 04/2024); flash-drought and ET papers ([AFM 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2025.110982); [AFM 2019 BMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2019.107759)); ongoing Xi–Hao–Leib–M. Jin irrigation paper under flash drought.

**Risks.** Overlaps the FFAR grant, R1/R5 in [2026-10-25.md](2026-10-25.md), and K3 in [2026-10-21.md](2026-10-21.md); no agricultural outcome data or partner confirmed.

**Verdict: Revise.** Fold into N1 as the agriculture and rural-water module (PA3a names "health, built infrastructure, and agriculture"). Do not submit separately.

##### N3. Forecasts of opportunity for U.S. extreme heat preconditioned by soil-moisture deficits — Revise (aim of N1, or standalone only with an S2S co-PI)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao, Shi Jin. Needed, unconfirmed: an S2S or ML-forecasting co-PI and a NOAA PSL, CPC, or GFDL collaborator.

**Approach.** Interpretable ML identifies windows when soil-moisture and flash-drought states make U.S. (Southeast and Central) heat predictable at weeks 2–4, then tests whether NOAA systems (GEFS, CFSv2, SPEAR seasonal) capture them, as PA3b requires.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Only three NSF awards mention "forecasts of opportunity" ([NSF API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22forecasts%20of%20opportunity%22)); the closest is 2616256/2616257 on stratosphere-troposphere coupling ([NSF API 2616257](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2616257.json?printFields=id,title,abstractText)). NSF 2435262/2435263 studies soil moisture in observed heatwaves as process science, not ML forecasts of opportunity. Published ML forecast-of-opportunity work centers on tropical sources ([NOAA S2S webinar](https://www.weather.gov/media/climateservices/Arcodia-NOAA-S2S-APS-Webinar.pdf); [DOE EESM record](https://eesm.science.energy.gov/publications/assessing-decadal-variability-subseasonal-forecasts-opportunity-using-explainable-ai)) or Europe ([van Straaten et al.](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_28103_1/component/file_28111/28103oa.pdf); [Weirich-Benet et al., WCE 2024](https://doaj.org/article/5d720f87d154453294b912e23d052a58)); soil-moisture and evaporative-demand forecasts of opportunity have been shown for weeks 3–6 ([NSF PAR 10458479](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10458479)). No U.S. study was found that diagnoses soil-moisture-driven heat forecast-of-opportunity windows with ML and verifies them in NOAA systems.

**Why us (partial).** Heatwave, flash-drought, and soil-moisture papers (above) supply the physical interpretation; PI was topic lead of the RUBISCO Soil Moisture Working Group mini-workshops. The talent pool shows no S2S forecasting or verification publications.

**Risks.** Technical-merit credibility; overlap with B3 in [2026-10-21.md](2026-10-21.md) (E3SM-RRM predictability).

**Verdict: Revise.** Make it a small N1 aim (when do heat plus flash-drought threshold exceedances become predictable at weeks 2–4?). Standalone only with a confirmed S2S/ML co-PI and a NOAA lab collaborator. Keep NOAA systems here and E3SM in B3.

##### N4. How small and mid-size utilities and water systems use SI/ML tools with NOAA heat and drought information (PA2) — Drop

PA2 requires socio-economic methods and strongly encourages NOAA staff collaboration. Large-utility AI adoption is already surveyed (fewer than a quarter of 49 large U.S. drinking-water utilities used AI: [ASCE JWRMD](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/JWRMD5.WRENG-5870); [BYU summary](https://cce.byu.edu/how-do-drinking-water-utilities-use-artificial-intelligence)); aiWATERS studied utilities of all sizes ([VTechWorks](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/1eb809d6-947b-4e08-b91e-039380737f95)). The talent pool has no social scientist, survey record, or utility partner, so Hao Lab would only be a technical add-on. Keep at most a light stakeholder-interview task inside N1, run with a partner.

##### N5. SI/ML emulator of land and ecosystem drought response for multi-annual prediction (PA1b) — Drop

PA1b asks for emulators of NOAA models such as SPEAR at 2–10 year leads. GFDL and Ai2 lead this area (ACE and ACE2 learn variability from subseasonal to decadal scales: [ACE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02074); [ACE2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11268); [SPEAR](https://aos.princeton.edu/news/spear-next-generation-gfdl-modeling-system-seasonal-multidecadal-prediction-and-projection)). The PI's model experience is E3SM, not SPEAR, and the lab has no emulator or multi-annual prediction publications. It would also duplicate B2 in [2026-10-21.md](2026-10-21.md).

### Which call should host which idea

CPO's LOI reply is the go/no-go point; if discouraged, the threshold work can move to CMMI-EBE (C2) or the CAREER plan (K1), not to a second NOAA submission.
