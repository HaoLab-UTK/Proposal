#### Past winners: ISSE Seed Grants

ISSE seeds go to small UT Knoxville teams, from a PI alone to a PI with three
co-PIs from other departments (mostly the Tickle College of Engineering, often
CEE), on applied, Tennessee-relevant topics that map onto the RFP bullets.

regional resilience: the PI's data center footprint seed and Haochen Li's
flood forecast with Jon Hathaway, Kelsey Ellis and Nicholas Shanahan
([ISSE seed page](https://isse.utk.edu/opportunities-for-faculty-students-partners/seed-grants/)).

Carolina at about 10 m resolution, updated about every six hours, for
transportation agencies (about 3,000 bridges and culverts) and regional
planning organizations; the team's post-Helene analysis estimated that about
40% of flooded areas lay outside FEMA flood-map coverage
([ISSE news](https://isse.utk.edu/new-flood-forecasting-seed-project-targets-gaps-in-southern-appalachian-flood-risk-information/)).
Its engine is a GPU/TPU hydrodynamic solver for the roughly 19,980 km²
watershed, supported by UT ORIED's AI Tennessee Initiative
([UT CEE news](https://cee.utk.edu/li-develops-flood-hazard-forecasting-system)).
Earlier seeds cover PFAS and microplastics, hydrogen and CO2 storage,
waste-to-value, urban heat ("Beat the Heat", Kelsey Ellis), exposome
monitoring, micromobility batteries and green-infrastructure inspection
([ISSE 2023-24 Annual Report](https://isse.utk.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2024/10/2023-24-THEC-report-FINAL-web.pdf), pp. 9-10;
[ISSE 2025-2026 Annual Report](https://isse.utk.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/11/ISSE-2025-2026-Annual-Report-compressed-2.pdf), p. 27).

flexible load affects transmission reliability and built multi-timescale grid

##### IS1. When rivers run low and hot: compound flash drought-heatwave stress on Tennessee Valley energy-water systems — Pursue

**Call.** UT Knoxville ISSE 2027 Seed Grants; RFP topics "modeling
sustainability and resilience under environmental stressors" and "extreme
hydrologic events ... for resilient energy-water systems".

**Team.** PI Yuefeng Hao (ISE). Co-PI Joshua S. Fu (Chancellor's Professor,
CEE; joint appointment in ORNL's Computational Earth Sciences Group) for
regional climate and extremes projections and climate impacts on energy
infrastructure ([CEE profile](https://cee.utk.edu/people/joshua-s-fu/)).
Co-PI Emine Fidan (Biosystems Engineering and Soil Science, UTIA; Hao as the
UTK PI meets the UTIA rule) for water availability and sectoral demand; a past
ISSE seed PI whose 2026 showcase was "Evaluating Water Availability and
Sectoral Demand for Sustainable Allocation"
([2025-2026 report](https://isse.utk.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/11/ISSE-2025-2026-Annual-Report-compressed-2.pdf), p. 35).
Students: Yifan Gao (IS10 text task) and, from Spring 2027, Shi Jin. Partners
to invite: the PI's ORNL E3SM collaborators (Jiafu Mao, Xiaoying Shi, Yaoping
Wang), ORNL's regional-resilience initiative and TVA (river temperature, flow
and derate records, which need a data request); John Schwartz (TNWRRC) as
advisor. Nobody has been contacted; no partner has agreed to anything.

**Research question and approach.** Do compound flash drought-heatwave events
in the Tennessee Valley give a detectable land-surface signal (ET deficit,
soil-moisture drawdown, terrestrial-water-storage anomaly) weeks before the
river low-flow and high-temperature conditions that constrain river-cooled
generation, and how will their frequency change under E3SM-RRM and downscaled
projections? (1) An event catalog from multi-source remote sensing and drought
indices, merged with triple collocation. (2) Lagged links from those
indicators to USGS flow and temperature at intakes and to known TVA
curtailment periods. (3) Projected event frequency from Fu's downscaled
climate and ORNL's E3SM-RRM. (4) A screening map of which corridors with
announced large-load growth face the highest compound risk, reported as a
map, not as a siting decision. IS12 (reservoir "whiplash") can be an aim if
TVA data are available.

**Closest existing projects and papers, and the gap.** NSF Award API searches
for "flash drought" AND power, "thermoelectric" AND drought AND heat, "large
load" AND "extreme weather" and "Tennessee Valley" AND resilience returned no
awards ([example query](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22data%20center%22%20AND%20flood)).
The closest NSF award, [2009726](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2009726)
(UNC, 2020), links drought and electricity systems to air-pollution exposure,
not to cooling-water constraints. TVA cut output at Browns Ferry in 2007, 2010
and 2011 because river temperatures were too high
([GAO-23-105375](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105375), search summary),
and TVA's adaptation plan names drought as a risk to river-cooled generation
([TVA 2022 CAP](https://www.sustainability.gov/pdfs/tva-2022-cap.pdf), search
summary): the hazard is documented, but not a land-surface early-warning
signal. ORNL's Southeast Resilience Accelerator works on heat, drought and
flood risk to Southeast energy infrastructure (search summaries of
[ORNL Regional Resilience](https://www.ornl.gov/regional-resilience/); page
403), so the gap claim against it is provisional. **Gap:** in the sources
checked, flash-drought onset science has not been tied to energy-water
operations in this basin. The thermoelectric water-vulnerability literature
was not re-checked.

**Novelty.** It moves flash-drought and heatwave hydrology from ecosystems
and agriculture to the thermal and low-flow limits of an energy system and
offers ORNL's resilience work a land-surface early-warning layer.

**Why us.** First-author papers on heatwave effects on CONUS hydrology
(*J. Hydrol.* 637, 2024) and flash-drought WUE and recovery (*AFM* 378, 2026);
first-author Priestley-Taylor and BMA ET algorithms (*AFM* 279, 2019);
co-author of triple-collocation drought-index integration (*SERRA* 36, 2022)
and the terrestrial-water-storage drivers paper (*J. Hydrol.* 635, 2024); Co-PI on the FFAR multi-source remote sensing flash
drought and flood tool; E3SM-RRM co-authorship and the DOE/ORNL grant (PI)
with ORNL modelers; the submitted paper on electricity-related water
consumption for projected data center load growth
([PI CV](../talent-pool/resources/hao-lab/Yuefeng_Hao_CV.md);
[hao-lab.md](../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)).

**Fundability.** Mission fit with RFP topics 2 and 3; originality on energy
reliability and water; feasible in one year with existing data and ORNL
outputs; collaboration across ISE, CEE, UTIA, ORNL and TVA. Two external
targets: (1) DOE Office of Science BER through the FY2027 continuation
solicitation DE-FOA-0003665, tracked idea **B1**
([summaries/2026-10.md](summaries/2026-10.md)); the per-topic award size is not
stated, so the team sets the target amount after talking to the program
manager; submit before 2027-04-01 for FY2027 or use the seed results for
FY2028; odds are modest given the FY2027 budget request. (2) USGS Powell
Center working group, tracked idea **PC1** ([2026-11-22.md](2026-11-22.md));
the FY2028 call closes 2027-01-31, before the seed starts, so the FY2029 call
fits the seed timeline; the award is travel for up to 15 participants plus an
optional Fellow up to $100K. A fallback multi-investigator target is NSF BIO
**BE1** only if recast. CAREER **K1** cannot be a named target: it is
single-investigator, and ISSE requires multi-investigator follow-on proposals.

**Risks.** (a) Overlap with the current Hao seed, which names heat-wave
electricity demand and water: keep IS1 free of siting, noise, land markets and
the web tool, and say so in the narrative. (b) Overlap with the 104b TDEC grant
(from 2027-01): no facility withdrawal or thermal-discharge decision support.
(c) Overlap with the DOE Office of Science project the ISSE report lists (PI
Qiang He, co-PI Hao; "Water Availability and Flood Resilience in
Energy-Supporting Watersheds"): if active, frame IS1 as atmospheric and
land-surface compound-event science, not surface-groundwater modeling, and
list it as related support. (d) TVA data access is not secured. (e) Fourteen
days for CVs and signatures. (f) Six months of overlap with the current seed:
separate effort, and distinct tasks for Gao and Keating. (g) The open question
whether a current awardee may be PI again; if ISSE says no, move IS1 to Joshua
S. Fu as PI with Hao as co-PI.

**Verdict: Pursue,** as the single proposal with Hao as PI, after ISSE
confirms that a current seed holder may lead.

##### IS2. Will the grid hold? Transmission reliability of AI large-load growth in the Tennessee Valley under heat, drought and flood extremes — Pursue

**Call.** UT Knoxville ISSE 2027 Seed Grants; RFP topic "technologies related
to energy dominance and resilience" plus energy-water extremes.

**Team.** PI Kai Sun (Professor, EECS; CURENT member; EPRI project manager for
grid operations and planning 2007-2012; power system stability, control and
optimization; [EECS profile](https://eecs.utk.edu/people/kai-sun/)), who led
the ISSE hydrogen-transmission seed. Co-PI Yuefeng Hao (hydro-climate stress
scenarios and siting inputs). Optional co-PI: Leon M. Tolbert or Hector A.
Pulgar (both CURENT; registry). Students: Shi Jin (tariff- and
moratorium-aware siting model gives candidate load locations) and Yifan Gao
(queue-uncertainty model). Partners to invite: TVA; the ORNL Next-Generation
Data Centers Institute / GRID-C; CURENT; TDEC Office of Energy Programs. Kai
Sun has not been asked; IS2 needs Kai Sun's agreement to serve as PI. Nobody
has agreed to anything.

**Research question and approach.** How much AI data center load can the
TVA-region transmission system host at plausible sites while staying secure
during compound heat-wave demand peaks, derated river-cooled generation, and
voltage disturbances that make data center loads trip to backup power at
once? (1) Candidate load clusters from the students' siting models plus
announced projects. (2) Hydro-climate stress cases from the PI's heatwave and
flash-drought definitions (concurrent demand, derating and outage cases).
(3) Steady-state and dynamic studies on a synthetic or CURENT test-bed model
of the region, using Kai Sun's multi-timescale simulation methods. (4) Metrics:
hosting limits, ride-through and flexibility needs. IS7 (computer-vision asset
inventory) can be a data task.

**Closest existing projects and papers, and the gap.** NSF
[2610262](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2610262) (Lehigh,
2026, $518,881) studies cyber-physical stability of hyperscale data centers in
inverter-dominated grids, with no hydro-climate driver. EPCL large-load awards
[2603293](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2603293) and
[2541996](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2541996) cover
colocation energy management and flexible data centers in unit commitment.
NERC's incident review of the July 2024 Northern Virginia event (about 1,500 MW
of data center load lost at once after a 230 kV fault) is event analysis, not
a hydro-climate study
([NERC Incident Review](https://nerc.com/pa/rrm/ea/Documents/Incident_Review_Large_Load_Loss.pdf),
seen through search summaries; [APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/nerc-incident-review-examines-risks-challenges-tied-integration-large-loads)).
NSF Award API searches for "data center" AND flood returned no relevant award.
**Gap:** none of these combines load placement with compound hydro-climate
stress on a river-cooled fleet, the same gap tracked EPCL idea **P1** claims
([2026-11-22.md](2026-11-22.md)).

**Novelty.** Load location becomes a decision-relevant input to reliability
studies under hydro-climate stress; grid-side data center studies usually omit
hydro-climate stress, and hydro-climate studies usually omit grid dynamics.

**Why us.** The *RSER* 232 (2026) review of wildfire-power grid interactions
(with Fangxing Fran Li, Mingzhou Jin and Jiafu Mao); the heatwave and
flash-drought papers; two funded data center grants (ISSE footprint; 104b
TDEC); the submitted electricity-related water paper; Shi Jin's Tennessee
Valley siting model and substation digital-twin inspection experience; Yifan
Gao's queue-uncertainty siting model
([hao-lab.md](../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)). Kai Sun's grid record comes
from the EECS profile and ISSE reports, not from the talent pool.

**Fundability.** Mission fit with energy resilience and energy-water
extremes; timely given NERC and FERC attention to large loads; feasible with
test-bed models and existing load lists; collaboration across EECS/CURENT,
ISE, TVA and ORNL. A non-awardee PI avoids the reapplication question. Two
external targets: (1) NSF ECCS EPCL (NSF 26-514, rolling), tracked idea
**P1**; recent EPCL large-load awards ran $200K-$585K, so about $500K over
three years is a team planning figure, not a sponsor figure. (2) NSF CMMI
core (NSF 26-515, rolling; 225-275 awards, $100M total) using the held idea
**C2** framing (data center-grid-water system-of-systems resilience), subject
to the partition rule in [summaries/2026-10.md](summaries/2026-10.md): if
**P1** or CAREER **K1** is pending, C2 cannot carry the same core. A TVA- or
EPRI-sponsored study is a possible third route, not checked.

**Risks.** (a) P1 and K1 partitioning ([2026-11-22.md](2026-11-22.md)).
(b) TVA's real network data are restricted, so a synthetic grid may be needed
(not checked). (c) The current Hao seed also names heat-wave electricity
demand: IS2 must be about transmission reliability, not where data centers
should go. (d) Kai Sun's interest and availability are unknown. (e) The 14-day
timeline.
