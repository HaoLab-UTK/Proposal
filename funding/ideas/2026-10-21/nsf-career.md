# NSF CAREER (NSF 22-586): Theme Screening for Dr. Yuefeng Hao (UT Knoxville ISE)

Current as of 2026-10-22. PI and team facts come only from `talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md` (cited as [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)). NSF award facts come from the NSF Award API (`api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/<id>.json`). Each award links to its public NSF page, `https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=<id>`.

Method note: the NSF Award API `keyword` parameter OR-matches words and sorts by date, so `CAREER "data center"` returns unrelated awards. I pulled every award since 2019 that matches each quoted phrase, then filtered locally for titles starting with "CAREER" and for the phrase appearing in the title or abstract. Phrases searched: "data center(s)", "flash drought(s)", "energy-water nexus", "water-energy nexus", "compound extremes", "facility location", "small modular reactor", "carbon-aware", "site selection", "siting", "hyperscale", "drought early warning", "interconnection queue", "heatwave", "agricultural drought". I also ran a regex over about 880 pulled awards to find any project that ties data centers to water, siting, location, or community. Of the API sources, only the NSF Award Search covers NSF. I did not search NIH, DOE, or USDA databases.

### Takeaway

The earlier Research Assistant Professor appointment does not disqualify him. ### Cited Findings
- **Eligibility.** Proposers must meet all of the following by the annual deadline: hold a doctoral degree in an NSF-supported field; do research in an NSF-supported area; hold at least a 50% tenure-track (or tenure-track-equivalent) position as an assistant professor (or equivalent title); be untenured; and not have received a CAREER award before. — [NSF 22-586 solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation)
- **Tenure-track equivalency.** A position counts only if all three hold: (1) a continuing appointment expected to last the five years of the grant; (2) substantial research and educational responsibilities; (3) a project that relates to the employee's career goals and job responsibilities and to the department's mission. For non-tenure-track faculty, the Departmental Letter must "clearly and convincingly" show equivalency. Associate Professors and adjunct faculty are not eligible, and "NO EXEMPTIONS ... WILL BE GRANTED." — [NSF 22-586](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation)
- **Proposal limits.** A PI may submit one CAREER proposal per annual competition and "may not participate in more than three CAREER competitions." Proposals withdrawn or returned without review do not count. No co-PIs are allowed. — [NSF 22-586](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation)
- **Prior positions and time since degree.** The FAQ says "Previous employment in a tenured position does not affect eligibility" and "CAREER eligibility is not limited by time from degree or years in a tenure track appointment." The Department Head certifies eligibility through the Departmental Letter. — [CAREER FAQ](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/information/faq-faculty-early-career-development-career-program)
- **Minimum award size.** The minimum is $400,000 over 5 years, except BIO, ENG, and OPP, which expect at least $500,000. So the minimum is $500K for ENG and $400K for GEO, apart from OPP. Voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited. — [NSF 22-586](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation)
- **Deadline.** The next full-proposal deadline is 2027-07-28, the fourth Wednesday in July, by 5 pm local time. NSF expects about 500 awards per year. The program page was last updated 2026-09-10. — [CAREER program page](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program)
- **Proposal requirements.** The proposal needs an integrated research and education plan with assessment strategies. A Departmental Letter is required; without it the proposal is returned without review. Letters of collaboration must use the single-sentence format, and no appendices are allowed. The solicitation also allows an additional activity such as "entrepreneurship, industry partnerships, or policy". — [NSF 22-586](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation)

A CAREER FAQ update was posted 2026-07-08. — [CAREER FAQ](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/information/faq-faculty-early-career-development-career-program); [Update 94708](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/updates/94708)

## Theme 1: Water-energy-climate-aware siting of AI computing infrastructure under extremes

### Takeaway
This is the strongest theme and should be pursued in revised form, merged with Theme 4. No NSF CAREER award covers siting or capacity planning of AI data centers. Every existing data-center CAREER award deals with operations: power-system integration, thermal management inside the data center, or carbon-aware computing. The gap is real: no project plans data centers and their co-located power (including SMRs) against hydro-climate extremes (flash drought, heatwaves, low flows that limit cooling water and thermal discharge). The PI's hydrology and extremes record combined with his two PI data-center grants gives a credible answer to "why this PI".

### Inferences
- **The gap.**
  - Existing CAREER awards optimize how data centers operate (power flexibility, thermal management, carbon).
  - Existing water work schedules workloads against water stress.
  - Existing siting work is either screening indices (PESGM) or the PI's own students' work on power-side uncertainty (queues, tariffs, moratoria).
  - Missing piece: no funded project treats hydro-climate extremes as the uncertainty in a long-horizon siting and capacity decision. Examples are compound heat-drought events that reduce cooling-water availability and tighten thermal-discharge limits for both data centers and co-located SMRs. Extreme scenarios drawn from Earth-system projections would be built into robust or distributionally robust siting models.
- **Novelty claim (sharpened).** Foundational methods for siting interdependent compute-power-water infrastructure under nonstationary, spatially correlated hydro-climate extremes. Components:
  - extreme-event scenario generation from projections (E3SM, ORNL);
  - correlated-failure-aware robust and stochastic siting, building on the Gao and Jin papers;
  - footprint constraints for withdrawals and thermal discharge, building on the USGS 104b grant.

  Correlated outages across shared river basins and grids are named as a gap even in the industry literature, which is a hook for the "spatially correlated" claim.
- **Why this PI.** Data-center siting groups mostly come from power systems or computer science, and drought groups mostly come from geoscience. This PI is the rare case who has published on flash drought and heatwaves and is also PI on data-center water and thermal-discharge decision-support grants in a single basin (Tennessee Valley), with an ORNL Earth-system collaboration. Every claim must stay within these listed facts.
- **Division fit.** The best home is CMMI/EBE: infrastructure systems that deliver water and energy services under "hazards, extreme events, and cascading effects", with analytical, computational, or AI methods. CBET/EWRE is a water-management-centred alternative. Do not target CISE/CSR or ECCS/EPCL; that is where the operational CAREER awards already sit.
- **Education plan.** Build a "siting under extremes" data-science studio in IE 465/565 that uses the lab's public Tennessee datasets, AI-for-infrastructure decision cases in IE 591, and statistics-of-extremes modules in IE 200. Optionally add a policy activity: TDEC decision-support outputs from the 104b grant, which the solicitation explicitly allows as an "additional activity such as ... policy".
- **Risks.**
  - Reviewers may read it as an applied Tennessee case study, so the foundational methods contribution must lead.
  - There is overlap with the PI's own USGS 104b and ISSE grants. The CAREER must not duplicate them; position those grants as preliminary results and data sources.
  - Fast-moving competition: three or more data-center/grid projects started in 2026 alone.
  - Optimization credibility: the PI's training is in hydrology, so the optimization track record is mostly the students' in-progress papers. Getting at least one optimization paper published or submitted before July 2027 matters.

## Theme 2: Decision support for flash drought and compound extremes (detection to operational decisions)

Revise this, or keep it only as a backup. Flash drought is now a crowded NSF topic, with two CAREER awards (Auburn 2022, Rice 2026), a 2026 CAIG AI project, and a CHIRRP EAGER that already covers prediction, decision-scale forecasts, dashboards, and community tools. A CAREER from an ISE faculty member would be differentiated only if the contribution is decision science: optimizing irrigation, water allocation, or infrastructure operations under probabilistic flash-drought forecasts, framed in ENG. - **Detection and prediction are saturated at NSF.** Tian, Vergopolan, CAIG, and CHIRRP already cover them. DroughtRx already claims "decision-relevant scales" and a dashboard. A GEO CAREER on flash-drought detection would face direct comparison and weak novelty.

## Theme 4: Optimization under uncertainty for energy-water infrastructure transitions (data centers, SMRs), with workforce education

### Takeaway
Merge this into Theme 1; do not pursue it as written. On its own it is too generic and lacks a scientific hook that separates it from decades of energy-water-nexus and stochastic capacity-expansion work, including the Sanders and Stillwell CAREER awards. Its methods core (robust and stochastic optimization, ML-assisted optimization) and its workforce-education angle are the right engine for Theme 1, where the hydro-climate-extremes uncertainty supplies the novelty.

### Inferences
**A. Pursue (lead). "Siting AI computing infrastructure under hydro-climate extremes: robust planning of coupled compute-power-cooling-water systems." Merges Themes 1, 4, and 3. Home: ENG CMMI/EBE.**
- **Research thrusts.**
  - (i) Generate extreme scenarios (compound heat-drought, low-flow) for siting horizons from observations, remote sensing, and E3SM-class projections, with an ORNL collaboration letter.
  - (ii) Build correlated-hazard robust or distributionally robust siting and capacity models that include withdrawal and thermal-discharge constraints for data centers and co-located SMRs, extending the Gao and Jin siting models.
  - (iii) Develop ML- and NLP-assisted extraction of moving policy constraints (moratoria, tariffs, permits) as decision-dependent uncertainty.
- **Education.** IE 465/565 studio, IE 591 cases, IE 200 extreme-statistics modules. Policy activity through TDEC decision support.
- **Why this PI.** Flash-drought and heatwave record, PI of the USGS 104b and ISSE data-center grants, two student siting papers, and the ORNL E3SM/CIVE link.
- **Changes needed.** Lead with the foundational method, not Tennessee. Publish at least one siting optimization paper before July 2027. Distinguish the CAREER clearly from the 104b and ISSE scopes.

**B. Pursue as alternate framing of A. "Stress-adjusted water accounting and planning for the AI compute buildout under drought." Home: ENG CBET/EWRE.**
- **Focus.** Water management rather than siting: basin-level, drought-conditioned water footprints of compute and power demand, linked to the PI's submitted paper on water use tied to data-center electricity demand.
- **Differentiation.** Must go beyond SCARF and "Balancing Bits and Drops" (operational, static stress factors) by making water stress dynamic under flash drought, using the PI's ET and soil-moisture expertise.
- **Choosing between A and B.** Pick one after talking to program officers.

- **Status.** Pursue only if A and B are judged out of scope. It needs strong separation from DroughtRx (2544077), Tian (2144293), CAIG (2616279), and the PI's own FFAR Co-PI tool.
