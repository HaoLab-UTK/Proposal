### Inferences
- **Event qualification is strong.** Tennessee went from about 1% D1 (late August) to 64% D1+ and the first D4 (October 20) in eight weeks. This is a flash drought, and it maps directly onto the PI's published flash drought expertise.
- **45-day timing.** The conservative anchor is the 2026-09-29 USDM map (D2+ jumped to 33.75%; D3 in Moore County, confirmed by NWS): 9/29 + 45 days = **2026-11-13**. A later anchor is the first D4 on the 2026-10-20 map: + 45 days = **2026-12-04**. The drought is still intensifying, so a "prolonged event" statement is credible. Recommendation: submit by **2026-11-13** and anchor on the D3/D4 escalation, citing USDM weekly data plus the NWS statements. The Grants.gov listing closes 2026-12-31.
- **Geography.** The coordinator's candidate region (southern Middle TN) is now only part of the story. The most severe area is West TN (Lauderdale, Tipton, Fayette, Hardeman, Chester, McNairy, Haywood). A proposal scoped to "Middle and West Tennessee" is better supported by the data than one scoped to Moore and Lincoln Counties only.
- **Spring and fall droughts compound for livestock.** A spring drought followed by a fall flash drought in the same year hits forage-livestock operations twice: reduced spring growth and hay, then lost fall stockpiled forage, all before the winter hay-feeding period. This is a concrete "critical and urgent" stakeholder need under emphasis b.1 (feed and water for livestock).
- **One application per PD.** Hao can lead only one A1712 application. The other ideas must be merged into it, led by a different PD, or redirected (for example, to A1701 CARE).

**Cover crop and practice efficacy literature**
- Deines et al., *Global Change Biology* (2023): satellite field-level analysis of more than 90,000 Corn Belt fields (2019–2020). Fields with 3+ years of cover crops showed average maize yield losses of 5.5% and soybean losses of 3.5%, with larger losses under drier spring conditions. — [PNNL record](https://data.pnnl.gov/group/nodes/publication/33662); [Stanford news](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2022/11/cover-crops-can-lower-yields)
- 2012 drought SARE/CTIC farmer survey: about 9.6% higher corn yields and 11.6% higher soybean yields after cover crops. These are self-reported. — [SARE](https://www.sare.org/publications/cover-crop-economics/an-in-depth-look-at-management-situations-where-cover-crops-pay-off-faster/when-soil-moisture-is-at-a-deficit-or-irrigation-is-needed)
- Field soil moisture evidence is mixed (a significant effect at only 1 of 3 sites). — [Practical Farmers of Iowa](https://practicalfarmers.org/research/cover-crop-effect-on-soil-water)
- A NASA-affiliated student project used NDVI to map cover crops in NW Alabama and reported higher soil water retention. — [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002857) (search snippet)

**Drone forage biomass literature**
- UAV structure-from-motion and vegetation-index models estimate pasture biomass. The SfM model is weak at low biomass, which is exactly the drought condition. — [PMC7833225](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7833225/)
- Kentucky alfalfa and tall fescue height-biomass work used ground-vehicle sensing. — [UKnowledge IGC 2023](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/igc/XXV_IGC_2023/Sustainability/8) (search snippet)

His work covers irrigation scheduling software, soil water sensors, and weather networks for soybean, corn, and cotton in TN. — [UT FEWSUS bio](https://fewsus.utk.edu/brian-leib) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- **Gap 1: farm-level forage and stock-water decisions.** The NASA/UAH/ETSU project serves monitors and USDM authors. FFAR serves row-crop irrigation in East TN. Grass-Cast does not cover the humid Southeast. Nothing found translates real-time ET and soil moisture flash drought signals into county or pasture-level forage deficits and hay or destocking guidance for Tennessee cattle producers during the 2026 event.
- **Gap 2: practice efficacy in the humid Southeast.** Satellite practice-efficacy evidence is Corn Belt and yield-based (Deines et al.). An ET- and water-use-efficiency-based assessment of drought damage and recovery in the humid Southeast during a documented flash drought would be new, and it matches the PI's published flash drought recovery and WUE work.
- **Gap 3: drones are only a support tool.** Drone biomass estimation is already established in the literature and is weakest at low biomass, so a drone-only idea has little novelty. It is useful as ground truth or calibration for satellite estimates.
- **Overlap risk.** If the FFAR team (Schwartz, Leib) also submits an A1712 for this drought, institutional coordination is needed. NIFA "encourages" coordination when one institution submits several applications.

### Gaps
- NIFA Data Gateway, CRIS/REEIS, and USAspending were not queried. The NIFA site was unreachable and the Data Gateway is interactive. An award-level check for prior A1712 drought projects in TN, KY, AL, and MO is still open.
- Topics of the Idaho and Florida A1712 awards are unknown, so similarity to our ideas cannot be ruled out.
- I did not find whether UT Extension already runs a 2026 fall drought forage program (for example, Beef & Forage Center drought webinars). This needs direct contact.

### Idea 1 (revised, merges candidates 1 and 2): "Flash Drought Forage and Stock-Water Rapid Response for Middle and West Tennessee cattle producers" — **Pursue (conditional on an extension partner)**
- **Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PD); Mingzhou Jin (decision support, collaborator listed in the talent pool; role unconfirmed); Yifan Gao (geospatial pipeline); Shi Jin (dashboard and ETL from Spring 2027).
- **Needed partner (unconfirmed):** a UT Extension forage or beef specialist, for example the UT Beef & Forage Center (whose director is listed in the May 2026 newsletter), plus county agents in the D3/D4 counties. Possibly the Tennessee Climate Office for event communication.
- **Question:** How large are pasture and forage deficits, and stock-pond and water stress, by county and pasture during the 2026 fall flash drought? What hay-purchase, destocking, and stockpiling decisions reduce losses before winter feeding?
- **Approach:**
  - Adapt the FFAR multi-source flash drought framework to grazing lands: ET, soil moisture, and vegetation indices give a weekly pasture-deficit index versus normal.
  - Translate it into county hay-balance estimates using the UT W 1222 planning benchmarks.
  - Deliver it through extension meetings, a web dashboard, and county agent briefings.
  - Measure adoption with pre/post producer surveys, decisions taken (hay sourcing, early weaning, culling, stockpile use), dashboard use, and agent trainings.
- **Within 90 days of award:** a first county-level deficit map and bulletin, plus agent training.
- **Gap and novelty:** Real but modest. The data products are mostly existing; the novelty is the farm-level forage and water translation for the humid Southeast during the event. Grass-Cast does not cover the Southeast, the UAH/ETSU NASA project serves monitoring rather than forage decisions, and FFAR covers row-crop irrigation.
- **Why us:** Hao's published flash drought, ET, and recovery methods (listed above); Co-PI on the FFAR flash drought decision-support framework at UT; UT ISE decision-support collaborators (M. Jin).
- **Fundability:** Fits emphasis a.3 (connected-sensor and AI decision tools) and b.1 (livestock feed and water security). It is integrated (extension plus applied research), can deliver within 90 days, and can measure adoption. $300K fits about 1 PD summer plus a student plus an extension partner sub-budget plus meeting costs (estimate).
- **Risks:**
  1. No extension specialist on the team. Reviewers weigh "immediate implementation" heavily, so a confirmed UT Extension Co-PD is near-mandatory.
  2. The 45-day clock: target 2026-11-13.
  3. FFAR tool readiness for pasture is unverified, since FFAR targets row crops. Clarify with PD Schwartz what can be reused (data rights).
  4. Overlap with any UTIA A1712 submission for the same drought.
  5. Fall rains may end the drought before the award, but the hay-shortage effects persist through winter.
- **Verdict:** **Pursue** as Hao's single A1712 application, if a UT Extension Co-PD confirms within about one week.

### Idea 2 (revised candidate 3): "Did soil-health practices limit 2026 flash drought damage? ET-based efficacy assessment and extension guidance" — **Revise: make it Objective 2 of Idea 1, or have a different PD lead it**
- **Team:** Hao. Partner needed (unconfirmed): a UT soil-health or cover-crop extension specialist, plus NRCS TN practice data.
- **Approach:** Compare satellite ET, water-use efficiency, and greenness drawdown and recovery between fields and pastures with and without soil-health practices (cover crops, no-till; for pastures, stockpiled or rotational grazing). Use the PI's flash drought WUE and recovery methods. Producer field days in spring 2027 would show the recovery results.
- **Gap and novelty:** Deines et al. (Corn Belt, yield-based) show cover crops can *reduce* yields in dry springs. No humid-Southeast, ET- and recovery-based efficacy evidence from a documented flash drought was found. That gap is real.
- **Why us:** Hao's published work on drought recovery and WUE (AFM 2026; J. Hydrol. 2023); prior cover-crop and drought proposal text (not funded, so it cannot be cited as an asset).
- **Fundability:** Emphasis a.1 is a direct match. However, ground truth on which fields use which practices depends on NRCS or extension data access within 12 months, and the NOFO points "recovery" work toward A1701.
- **Risks:** Practice-location data access; confounders; timing (most of the fall drought falls after the row-crop growing season). The 12-month window may be too short to show recovery.
- **Verdict:** **Revise.** Fold it into Idea 1 as an efficacy objective limited to pasture practices (stockpiling, rotational grazing), or submit it under A1701 CARE.
