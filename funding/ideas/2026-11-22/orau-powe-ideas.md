## Key Question 1: Past winners, typical framing, UT Knoxville record, topic area to use

### Takeaway
The Powe program funds about 36 single-PI seed projects a year from roughly 170 applications (about 21% success in FY2026), in every science field. Winners frame the project as a focused, one-year step that produces preliminary data for a named larger grant. ORAU's winner lists give names and institutions but no fields or titles, so a field count is not possible from official pages. Among the FY2026 winners whose fields could be verified, the closest engineering/environment winner is an environmental-engineering assistant professor working on AI modeling for sustainable energy systems. ORAU has sponsored single "specialty" awards before (GDIT supply chain innovation and AREA augmented reality in FY2024, Oakland University in FY2025, Temple University in FY2026). The FY2027 special topic "Critical Mineral Constraints on SMR Deployment Capacity in the U.S." matches the lab's SMR co-location thread, but ORAU publishes only its title. The strongest Hao Lab idea should use that special topic, with "Engineering and Applied Science" as the fallback.

**SMR critical-minerals special topic: what exists**
- ORAU gives the title only (see above).
- Rhodium Group and Rovjok (2026-08-13) estimated mineral needs for a U.S. Gen 3+ build-out (AP1000 and BWRX-300). Their findings:
  - Aggregate supply is sufficient except for uranium.
  - There are chokepoints in specific forms and niche minerals, especially fuel-rod zirconium, because China dominates mid-stage processing.
  - The reactors need "at least 18" minerals on the USGS critical list.
  - A doubled fleet needs about 3,300 t of zirconium.
  - Hafnium and zirconium would be about 3.2% and 3.1% of U.S. annual demand.
  - Zirconium would take 8.1% of ex-China production.
  - The outlook becomes "more nuanced" if Gen 4 SMRs are pursued at scale.

  — [Rhodium report PDF](https://rhg.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Critical-Mineral-Chokepoints-Could-Threaten-the-US-Nuclear-Energy-Renaissance.pdf); [Rhodium web note](https://rhg.com/research/critical-minerals-nuclear-us/)
- The Payne Institute (Colorado School of Mines) argues that factory-built SMRs "multiply the impact of midstream bottlenecks in zirconium, hafnium, nuclear-grade steels and advanced electronics". The piece is framed for Canada and is qualitative. — [Payne Institute](https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/nuclear-and-smr-non-fuel-critical-minerals-supply-chain-an-emerging-fourth-value-chain/)
- USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, zirconium and hafnium:
  - Import sources of zirconium ores and concentrates in 2021-24 were South Africa 48%, Australia 35% and Senegal 15%.
  - Global mine production of zirconium concentrates fell 12%.
  - The leading exporters of unwrought hafnium were China and Germany.
  - Hafnium is used in nuclear control rods.

  — [USGS MCS 2026 Zr-Hf](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-zirconium-hafnium.pdf)
- HALEU supply risk: Carnegie Endowment (2026-10) on whether fuel can be delivered in time, and National Academies 2025 workshop proceedings noting the lack of a reliable U.S. HALEU supply chain. Both were seen only as search summaries. — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/10/can-nuclear-fuel-be-delivered-in-time-to-power-advanced-nuclear-reactors); [NASEM 29142](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29142/chapter/4)

### Inferences
- **Typical winning framing.** The project is single-PI, one year and narrowly scoped. It names the larger proposal it seeds ("preliminary data for larger external grants"), uses graduate students, and has a credible record in the same topic. The verified stories (Hibshman, Cojocaru, Park) all follow this pattern. With two reviewers who are discipline experts, the two pages must read as a crisp research plan, not a program vision.
- **Why the winners were likely chosen.** Each is a clear, testable question that fits $10K. The applicant has a visible publication record on it, often with lab infrastructure already in place. Each has a plausible follow-on route (NSF, NIH or DOE). Mentoring and teaching ties are mentioned in some letters (Lu Li). The 21% rate is high for a seed program, so a well-matched, cleanly written proposal has real odds.
- **Special topics.** ORAU has a record of single specialty awards funded by a partner (GDIT supply chain, AREA AR, Oakland, Temple). The two FY2027 special topics likely work the same way: perhaps one award each, judged by a smaller set of reviewers. That likely means fewer competitors but fewer slots. ORAU does not state this; ask poweawards@orau.org or the UT Councilor.
- **Topic area for the strongest idea.** "Critical Mineral Constraints on SMR Deployment Capacity in the U.S." is the best match. The lab already has a funded USGS 104b grant on data centers and SMRs and two student siting papers. A mineral-constrained deployment-capacity model is a systems-engineering contribution that the materials- and policy-oriented Rhodium/Payne work does not provide. Fallback: "Engineering and Applied Science" for the water/heat stress-test idea (PW2 below).
- **UT Knoxville.** UT Knoxville has one recent winner (FY2026) and an internal two-slot competition. The internal deadline may already have passed: Pitt's was 2026-10-21, and other schools set November-December dates. Contacting the UT ORAU Councilor's office this week is the first action.

### Full evaluation PW1 (lead): Mineral-constrained SMR deployment capacity for AI data center load growth

- **Title (working):** "How many SMRs, where, and when? A mineral-constrained deployment-capacity model for co-locating small modular reactors with AI data centers."
- **Call / area:** ORAU Powe FY2027, special topic "Critical Mineral Constraints on SMR Deployment Capacity in the U.S." ([ORAU](https://www.orau.org/partnerships/grant-programs/powe/)).
- **Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI, sole applicant). Yifan Gao (PhD student; robust optimization and infrastructure planning under uncertainty; NLP information extraction) is paid from the award. Shi Jin (PhD student from Spring 2027; tariff- and moratorium-aware siting model for the Tennessee Valley) contributes the site layer. Letters or data support, not co-PIs, could come from the people in `orau-powe-collaborators.md` (for example ORNL OR-SAGE staff and UT Nuclear Engineering faculty). None has been contacted.
- **Research question:** Given midstream supply limits on nuclear-grade zirconium, hafnium, nickel-chromium-molybdenum alloys and HALEU, how much SMR capacity can the U.S. deploy by 2035, and which candidate data center co-location sites should receive constrained module deliveries first? Which mineral's shadow price limits deployment, and how robust is the answer across supply scenarios?
- **Approach (12 months, $10K):**
  1. Months 1-3, mineral intensity and supply scenarios. Compile per-module mineral intensities and supply scenarios from public sources: Rhodium/Rovjok intensities for the BWRX-300 and AP1000, USGS MCS 2026 trade and production data, and HALEU projections. Gao's NLP extraction builds a source-linked supply table (PW10).
  2. Months 3-8, constrained deployment model. Formulate a multi-period, scenario-based deployment model in which module deliveries are capped by the mineral supply chains. Gao's comparison of robust optimization approaches under interconnection-queue uncertainty is reused here. Constrained modules are allocated to candidate sites screened with published OR-SAGE criteria and Jin's Tennessee Valley siting inputs, to meet projected data center loads.
  3. Months 8-12, results. Report deployable GW, unmet data center load, mineral shadow prices and regret across scenarios. Write one journal paper and prepare the larger proposal.
  4. Budget sketch: graduate summer support for Gao (most of the $10K), and travel to present (for example INFORMS or ANS). No indirect costs are allowed.
- **Closest existing work and the gap:**
  - [Rhodium/Rovjok 2026](https://rhg.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Critical-Mineral-Chokepoints-Could-Threaten-the-US-Nuclear-Energy-Renaissance.pdf) quantifies national mineral requirements and chokepoints for Gen 3+ builds. It has no time-phased deployment optimization, no siting, and no link to data center loads. It also notes that SMRs at scale make the outlook "more nuanced", which leaves the SMR case open.
