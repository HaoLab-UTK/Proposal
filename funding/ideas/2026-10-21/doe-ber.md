## Q0. Is an off-panel BER Earth-system application realistic in FY2027, and what must it look like?

### Takeaway
Yes, it is formally allowed but narrow. The only realistic Earth-system pitch is one that matches the FY2027 request's single surviving line, Earth and Environmental Systems Modeling (EESM): AI-enhanced, regionally refined E3SM and multisector modeling of **water availability for energy expansion** on **weeks-to-years** timescales in **target regions**. Ecosystem vulnerability, ET process studies, and boreal ecology framed as stand-alone science are what the request says is being "completed." Talk to BER before writing. The Tennessee Valley, with its data center and SMR build-out, fits the request's "target regions where energy expansion is envisioned" language.

## Q1. Idea 1: CIVE v1.0 extended to compound flash drought plus heatwave extremes in the Southeastern US with E3SM-RRM (with ORNL)

### Takeaway
The science is credible and a genuine Hao Lab strength. As written, though, it is an ecosystem-vulnerability study, and that is the topic the FY2027 request is winding down. Revise it: keep the compound flash-drought and heat physics in E3SM-RRM over the Southeast, but make the outcome the predictability of **water availability for energy infrastructure** in the Tennessee Valley. Then merge it into Idea 5.

### Cited Findings
- E3SMv2's North American RRM (NARRM) runs atmosphere and land at about 25-100 km over North America with 0.125° river routing. It was built "to inform the US energy sector about changes in water availability, wildfires, heat waves, and sea-level rise," and costs about 10-20% of a uniform high-resolution run. — [Tang et al., GMD 2023](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3953/2023/)
- Searches found no E3SM-RRM study focused on Southeastern US flash drought or heatwave compound extremes. NARRM land-atmosphere coupling papers emphasize the Southern Great Plains (search result; full text not read). — [ResearchGate listing of Tang et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365333700_The_Fully_Coupled_Regionally_Refined_Model_of_E3SM_Version_2_Overview_of_the_Atmosphere_Land_and_River)
- A Hao, Mao et al. paper evaluated ecosystem water-use efficiency and recovery during flash droughts at the Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux site against ELMv2. It found that ELM produced an artificially prolonged GPP recovery time and that soil-moisture-only stress misses rapid hydraulic recovery. — [Hao et al., Agric. For. Meteorol. 378:110982 (2026), ORNL record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/evaluating-ecosystem-water-use-efficiency-and-recovery-dynamics-d/); [OSTI 3020938](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/3020938-evaluating-ecosystem-water-use-efficiency-recovery-dynamics-during-flash-droughts-insights-from-observations-model-simulations)
- Hao Lab assets: an ORNL collaboration on the E3SM regionally refined configuration and the CIVE v1.0 framework; PI on the DOE/ORNL grant "Quantification of Boreal and Temperate Ecosystem Vulnerabilities and Their Model Uncertainties" (2024-01, 5 yr, $230K); and collaborators Mao, Shi, Wang, Ricciuto, Thornton, and Hoffman at ORNL. — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)
- The FY2027 request says ESS "completes most research on... boreal ecology." — [FY2027 BER CJ](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fy-2027-biological-and-environmental-research-budget-request)

### Inferences
- The gap is real but narrow: E3SM-RRM has not been applied to Southeastern compound flash drought plus heat. What is new is the region and the compound-event framing, not the model.
- **Why us:** CIVE plus E3SM-RRM experience with ORNL, published ELMv2 flash-drought evaluation, and flash-drought and heatwave expertise ([hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)). This combination is hard for another university group to match.
- **Main risks:** (a) Framed as ecosystem vulnerability, it reads as ESS work that BER is closing out. (b) ORNL collaborators could run the experiment inside their own lab programs, so the university's added value must be spelled out. (c) CIVE v1.0 could not be found publicly; if it is unpublished, reviewers cannot assess it.
- **Verdict: Revise.** Keep it as the Earth-system work package of the merged lead idea (Final Idea A). Do not submit it alone to BER. A stand-alone ecosystem version fits NSF GEO Core better.

### Gaps
- I could not find any public CIVE v1.0 publication or code. Confirm with the PI whether it is published, in review, or internal to ORNL.
- I could not verify whether ORNL already has funded Southeastern RRM extreme-event runs, for example in the E3SM project's regional work.

## Q2. Idea 2: Benchmark and improve E3SM land model ET partitioning with Bayesian Model Averaging of ET products and AmeriFlux sites under drought

### Takeaway
There is a documented scientific gap, since ESMs misrepresent the transpiration/ET (T/ET) ratio and ELM has only a few site evaluations. But it is a process benchmarking task, which is the kind of observational and process work BER says it is completing. It is too small and too generic to win an off-panel BER award alone. Fold it into the lead idea as a validation component, or send it to NSF GEO Core.

### Cited Findings
- ESMs underestimate T/ET, which is "attributable to inaccurate representation of canopy light use, interception loss and root water uptake." Constraining models with observations strongly increases the modeled T/ET. — [Lian et al., Nature Climate Change 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-018-0207-9)
- T/E partitioning errors explain the modeled summertime warm bias in the central US. — [Nature Communications 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-27938-6)
- ELM-specific ET work exists:
  - ELMv0 at a temperate forest site (Missouri Ozark) underestimated the impact of the 2012 drought. — [GMD 12:1601 (2019)](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/12/1601/2019/)
  - ELM evaluation and improvement for an Amazonian peatland. — [Yuan et al. 2023, AFM / USFS PDF](https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2023/nrs_2023_yuan_001.pdf)
  - ELM deforestation effects on ET, where calibration shifted ET toward more transpiration. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334182585_Improving_Representation_of_Deforestation_Effects_on_Evapotranspiration_in_the_E3SM_Land_Model)- Searches found no dedicated, multi-site, drought-conditioned T/ET benchmark of ELM. This is a search result, not proof of absence. — same searches as above
- Hao Lab assets: ET estimation and partitioning (Priestley-Taylor, complementary relationship, Bayesian Model Averaging), AmeriFlux and flux tower data, and MODIS remote sensing. — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** a drought-conditioned ELM T/ET benchmark across many sites, using a BMA ET ensemble with uncertainty. The novelty is moderate: BMA ET products and ILAMB-style benchmarking already exist, and Hao's own BMA expertise is the differentiator.
- **BER fit is weak.** The CJ prioritizes AI-enhanced prediction for energy expansion, not land process benchmarking. ET does, however, drive the water availability that energy facilities depend on, so it works as a supporting component.
- **Main risks:** it reads as incremental; ILAMB already benchmarks ET; and it overlaps with work ORNL's ELM team could do in-house.
- **Verdict: Drop as a stand-alone BER idea. Merge it as a validation work package into Final Idea A.** Alternatively, pursue it alone at NSF GEO Core.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether ILAMB already includes a T/ET partitioning benchmark.

## Q3. Idea 3: AI emulator of E3SM land/ecosystem response to drought for fast vulnerability and recovery projections

### Takeaway
This is the closest match to the FY2027 language ("AI-enhanced E3SM," "surrogate components," "huge ensembles," the Genesis Mission). Generic ELM surrogates already exist, though, and E3SM has its own flagship AI emulator. Novelty has to come from a drought-specific, recovery-aware surrogate that is corrected with the known ELM recovery bias and **used for decision-making**: thousands of drought scenarios feeding water-availability risk into infrastructure siting. Hao Lab's AI emulation capacity is thin on paper.

### Cited Findings
- Existing ELM surrogates and ML work:
  - An ML emulator of ELM methane biogeochemistry for sensitivity analysis, cutting run time from 6 CPU hours to 0.72 ms. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376235013_Machine_Learning_Driven_Sensitivity_Analysis_of_E3SM_Land_Model_Parameters_for_Wetland_Methane_Emissions)
  - ML GPP-SIF surrogate calibration of ELM photosynthesis. — [Chen et al., JAMES 2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022MS003135)
  - An ML surrogate for groundwater table depth calibration. — [E3SM news](https://e3sm.org/machine-learning-helps-improve-groundwater-representation-in-earth-system-models/)
  - A DNN wildfire surrogate in ELM. — [GMD 2022](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1899/2022/)
- E3SM released "an AI emulator that operates at 100X the speed of previous capabilities." — [FY2027 BER CJ](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fy-2027-biological-and-environmental-research-budget-request)
- I found no drought- or recovery-specific ELM emulator in searches.
- Conflicting evidence on the direction of ELM's recovery bias:
  - Hao et al. found ELMv2 produces an artificially prolonged GPP recovery after flash drought. — [ORNL record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/evaluating-ecosystem-water-use-efficiency-and-recovery-dynamics-d/)
  - Multi-model studies find that land models make drought legacy effects about 4x too short and 4x too weak, meaning recovery is too fast. — [Kolus et al., Sci. Rep. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-39373-1)
  - These differ by drought type (flash versus prolonged) and by variable, so the bias depends on the regime. That is itself a scientific hook.
- Including drought legacy effects improves modeled carbon and water fluxes. — [Biogeosciences 23:5461 (2026)](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/23/5461/2026/)
- Hao Lab assets: Shi Jin lists interests in "AI for understanding the physical world," "AI for environment (Earth system models, decision support)," and diffusion-model workflows. Yifan Gao works on ML-assisted optimization. The PI has ELM evaluation experience with ORNL. — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** a regime-aware (flash versus prolonged drought) ELM drought-and-recovery surrogate, calibrated to fix the documented recovery-timing bias with AmeriFlux and WUE observations. Its purpose would be to run large ensembles feeding water and ecosystem risk into energy-siting decisions.
- **Why us:** only partial. Hao Lab has no published emulator work. Shi Jin's ESM interest is stated as an interest, not a track record, and he starts in Spring 2027. The emulator must be co-developed with ORNL (Ricciuto has a surrogate and UQ background, but that is outside hao-lab.md, so do not claim it).
- **Main risks:** ORNL or E3SM can build this in-house; reviewers will ask why a university should do it; and there are no preliminary results.
- **Verdict: Revise.** Make it the AI component of Final Idea A, or keep it as a smaller stand-alone (Final Idea B) only if ORNL co-PIs lead the emulator architecture and Hao Lab leads the observational recovery constraints and decision use.

### Gaps
- Whether an ELM drought emulator is already in progress inside E3SM or ORNL's TES SFA could not be verified. Ask the ORNL collaborators.

## Q5. Idea 5: Land and water impacts of rapid large-load energy infrastructure (data centers, SMRs) in Earth system modeling

### Takeaway
This is the strongest fit and the most differentiated idea. The CJ's "energy expansion," "earth-energy interactions," "multi-sector dynamics," and "water availability... target regions" language, together with the NOFO's "human system processes," make it viable, **provided it stays science-led**: it must improve predictability of the earth-water system that large loads depend on and stress, not be a pure siting or optimization study. PNNL's IM3 already does data center energy-water work with GCAM-USA, but at state or HUC2 scale, with a Western Interconnection grid focus. That work does not use E3SM land hydrology or compound droughts in the Southeast. This leaves a real gap, and Hao Lab already runs a data center plus SMR water grant in Tennessee.

### Cited Findings
- The NOFO's BER mission includes "energy-relevant... human system processes." — [NOFO p. 53](https://science.osti.gov/-/media/grants/pdf/foas/2027/DE-FOA-0003665-Part-1.pdf)
- The request says the system "will be applied to specific target regions where energy expansion is envisioned and are particularly sensitive to variable environmental conditions such as water availability." EESM couples "exascale class and multi-sector dynamics models." — [FY2027 BER CJ](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fy-2027-biological-and-environmental-research-budget-request)
- Existing BER-funded data center work (PNNL/IM3/JGCRI):

  - Projected US data center locations to 2035 with the CERF-Data Centers model, tied to GCAM-USA IM3 Phase 2. — [OSTI 3020186](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/3020186); [OSTI 2571680](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2571680)
  - Grid stress in the Western Interconnection under data center growth. — [OSTI 2589826](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2589826)

  - IM3 Phase 2 GCAM-USA water availability is resolved by HUC2 basin under RCP4.5/8.5. — [OSTI 2584365](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2584365)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** no existing work links (i) E3SM/ELM projections of compound flash drought and heat stress on sub-basin water availability and cooling-water temperature with (ii) siting and capacity of large loads (data centers, SMRs) in a fast-growing Southeastern energy region. IM3 covers the West, at state or HUC2 scale, using GCAM's water module rather than ESM land hydrology extremes. The novelty is real, but it must be presented as coupling an ESM with human-system decisions, not as optimization.
- **"Human system processes" is enough to make it in scope.** It is competitive only if the hypothesis is about earth-system predictability, for example: "compound flash drought-heat events in the Tennessee Valley reduce summer cooling-water availability on weeks-to-months scales that current siting assumptions, based on annual or HUC2 averages, miss."
- **Why us:** no other team pairs an active data center and SMR water decision-support grant for the Tennessee regulator (TDEC) with E3SM-RRM and flash-drought ELM experience at ORNL, plus two PhD students already building Tennessee Valley siting models. This is the strongest "why us" in the set.
- **Main risks:**
  - (a) BER may see it as applied energy or decision work (better suited to DOE EERE/OE or NSF) unless the earth-system science leads.
  - (b) Overlap with IM3: reviewers from PNNL may ask why not IM3. Answer: a different region, ESM-based hydrology extremes, and sub-annual timing. Consider inviting IM3 as a data partner (an option, not yet a fact).
  - (c) Small EESM budget.
  - (d) The PhD students start 2026 Fall and 2027 Spring.

## Final shortlist (3 ideas, ranked)

### Takeaway
Lead with **A**, an energy-expansion water-availability study in the Tennessee Valley that couples E3SM-RRM compound extremes with large-load siting. It has the best BER fit, a real gap, and the strongest "why us." **B** is a revised AI-surrogate option that depends on ORNL leading the emulator. **C** is a minimal stand-alone fallback built from Idea 1's science, reframed toward energy-relevant water availability. Drop Idea 4 and stand-alone Idea 2 for BER.
