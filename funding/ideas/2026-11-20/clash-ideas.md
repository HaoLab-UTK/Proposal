## Q1. Call facts and what reviewers will score

### Takeaway
This is a small seed grant: $20K-$40K including indirect costs, 12 months from 2027-05-01, 5-page description, due 2027-01-15. Reviewers score relevance to the cascade most heavily. A proposal outside Alaska or Puerto Rico must link two or more processes or observatories.

### Cited Findings
- Proposals are due January 15, 2027 (11:59 PM PST). The portal opened November 15, 2026. Awards are announced early to mid April 2027, and projects run May 1, 2027 to April 30, 2028 — [2026-27 RFP (Updated Oct 30 2026), program page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- 5-9 awards of $20,000-$40,000, including indirect costs. Up to 12 months. No subcontracts or subawards and no participant support costs. Computers are unallowable. No carry-over and no no-cost extensions — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Eligibility: the PI must be at a U.S. institution eligible for NSF funding and eligible to be an NSF PI there. "Early-career scientists and investigators are strongly encouraged to apply" — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- 2026-27 priorities: (1) cross-observatory inquiry and modeling to test cascade hypotheses, favoring "cross-observatory hypothesis testing with existing or new data" or work in Puerto Rico or Alaska, with partner and community-engaged research encouraged; (2) modeling the cascade (triggers, hillslope-channel coupling, runout, sediment transport, flooding), with CSDMS/BMI/pyMT/Landlab integration and ML/AI "especially welcome"; (3) remote sensing and AI at scale (InSAR, scaling observatory measurements to regional products, FAIR AI-ready datasets and benchmarks), which must state its contribution to the strategic plan; (4) event-driven investigations and new hazard types, which must fit a hazard cascade or its "long tail" — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Relevance questions reviewers ask: what part of the cascade "we don't understand today" the work addresses; whether it links "two or more processes or observatories, if the focus is not on Alaska or Puerto Rico"; what it contributes to CLaSH cascade model and data objectives; and, for sites outside CLaSH geographies, whether the site choice is justified — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Other review criteria: Intellectual Merit, Broader Impacts (training, outreach, data sharing), Innovation & Collaboration (cross-disciplinary work or new partnerships) and Feasibility. Existing partners named: EarthScope, NHERI RAPID, DesignSafe and OpenTopography. Letters of collaboration are optional — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Data rule: all data must be uploaded to a public repository at closeout (an embargo of up to 1 year is allowed) and follow FAIR principles and the NSF EAR data policy — [2026-27 RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Changes from 2025-26: that round's priorities were (1) site-specific work at observatories, (2) modeling of weather-related forcing, and (3) ML and AI-ready datasets. It had a December 8, 2025 release, a February 4, 2026 deadline and April 6, 2026 notification — [2025-26 page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2025-26/); [2025-26 RFP PDF](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2026-FINAL_1_22_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2026-27 call newly asks for cross-observatory hypothesis testing and favors Puerto Rico and Alaska. An Appalachia-only proposal is therefore at a disadvantage unless it explicitly links Appalachia with Puerto Rico or another observatory, or links two cascade processes.
- At $40K all-in with no subawards, a realistic budget is one graduate student for part of the year, a little PI summer salary, data and publication costs, and travel to the Annual Meeting or a field visit. The UT indirect rate does not appear in the talent-pool files. Confirm it with the UT sponsored-programs office before building the budget.
- Because a partner cannot receive a subaward, a collaborating geomorphologist at another university can only be named as an unfunded collaborator with a letter.

### Gaps
- The 2026-27 FAQ document was not downloaded. It may say whether co-PIs at other institutions or multiple submissions per PI are allowed.
- No published success rate or applicant count was found.

## Q3. The Appalachia observatory and strategic-plan goals

### Takeaway
The Appalachia observatory is in Kentucky, with the Kentucky Geological Survey as local partner. It focuses on the runout → river → flooding part of the cascade, using the 2022 eastern Kentucky event and debris fans. Helene (2024) adds a southern Appalachian Blue Ridge event that reached Tennessee. Soil moisture is explicitly named in the strategic plan as a trigger condition, but the plan does not say Appalachia has a hydro-climate preconditioning lead. That is the clearest opening for Hao Lab.

### Cited Findings
- Observatory selection criteria were existing robust datasets, "modular" cascade frameworks extendable by AI/ML, and local partners: the California Geological Survey, the **Kentucky Geological Survey**, UPR Río Piedras/Mayagüez, and tribal communities in Alaska — [Hazard Observatories](https://www.geoclash.org/2299-2/)
- "In KY, we will focus on how runout magnitude and grain size influence fluvial systems and flooding … observing how grain size controls the sediment response of the 2022 event … as well as the long-term morphological structure of debris fans" — [Hazard Observatories](https://www.geoclash.org/2299-2/)
- Puerto Rico and Southern California focus on how the critical zone and topography control landslide occurrence, size and timing. Puerto Rico adds ecological controls and feedbacks. Alaska (Haines Highway, Klukwan / Chilkat Indian Village) focuses on debris-flow initiation and entrainment under changing atmospheric rivers — [Hazard Observatories](https://www.geoclash.org/2299-2/)
- Observations planned at all observatories: landslide size, runout length and volume; grain size; critical zone properties; and "analysis of precipitation histories and downscaling of future projections" — [Hazard Observatories](https://www.geoclash.org/2299-2/)
- MA2 tasks in the strategic plan: (2) "Constrain the hydro-meteorological conditions of rainfall-triggered slope failures (e.g., soil moisture properties and dynamics) … define rainfall-triggered slope failure thresholds" (initial focus Puerto Rico and Southern California); (4) "Analyze runout–river coupling through object detection using deep learning and image segmentation of lidar DEM data for debris fans" (Appalachia/Kentucky); (5) characterize storm types by site, such as "convective storms in KY" and tropical cyclones in Puerto Rico; (7) "Synthesize Hazard Observatory data within a regional context (e.g., statewide datasets) or across sites" — [Strategic Plan, MA2](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CLaSH-1.0-Five-Year-Strategic-Plan.pdf)
- The four principal science questions: IM.1 does trigger magnitude set the cascade trajectory, and are there tipping points? IM.2 how do past events or preconditioning factors influence future threats? IM.3 how do process linkages change magnitude and frequency? IM.4 how long do cascades last? Box 1 lists preconditioning on timescales of "years (wildfire, permafrost degradation, wet–dry cycles, land use change)" and notes that "Loss and regrowth of vegetation changes slope stability" — [Strategic Plan, 2030 Goals and Box 1](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CLaSH-1.0-Five-Year-Strategic-Plan.pdf)
- MA4 adopts CSDMS BMI, a model-data integration pipeline, model intercomparison for precipitation-induced landslide triggering, and linked cascade models run on SimCenter computing — [Strategic Plan, MA4](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CLaSH-1.0-Five-Year-Strategic-Plan.pdf); [Modeling Collaboratory](https://www.geoclash.org/2350-2/)
- Helene: a Tier 2 CLaSH response (University of Kentucky / KGS) collected drone and terrestrial lidar, geophysics of flood deposits and samples in North Carolina and Tennessee over 60 days. Helene floods exceeded historic floods by 50%. About 2,200 landslides were triggered, and "there was little work estimating the run-out distance of debris flows in the region" — [CLaSH case studies](https://www.geoclash.org/case-studies/)
- The USGS inventory counts 2,217 Helene-triggered landslides — [USGS OFR 2025-1028](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20251028/full) (search summary). GSA Today (January 2026) links the landsliding to antecedent rain in the days before Helene, with a 24-hour threshold of about 125 mm on forested slopes. 55% of mapped landslides damaged infrastructure or property — [GSA Today, Jan 2026](https://www.geosociety.org/gsa-today/january-2026/insights-into-widespread-landsliding-in-southern-appalachia-from-hurricane) (search summary)
- Kentucky partners: KGS researchers (Dortch, Crawford, Thigpen, Swallom, Koch) link the NSF EPSCoR CLIMBS project with CLaSH. Swallom co-leads Appalachian flood modeling — [UKNow](https://uknow.uky.edu/research/climbs-researchers-go-global-finding-hazard-solutions-through-nsf-s-clash-initiative)
- Event response by now covers Mindanao (June 2026, about 6,600-7,000 mapped landslides), the Venezuela earthquakes (June 2026), Nepal (August 2026 Trishuli debris flood), the LA fires (2025, Tier 3) and Hilary (2023). CLaSH aimed to post online data inventories in fall 2026 — [event page](https://sites.google.com/umich.edu/clasheventpage/home); [event-based research](https://www.geoclash.org/event-based-research/); [2026 Annual Meeting](https://www.geoclash.org/2026-annual-meeting/)

### Inferences
- The Kentucky observatory works downstream in the cascade (runout → river → flood). The Puerto Rico and Southern California observatories work upstream (critical zone → initiation, soil-moisture thresholds). No observatory description names a lead for evapotranspiration or drought-driven wet-dry preconditioning. That gap matches Box 1 and IM.2, and it is Hao Lab's strongest science.
- Helene reached East Tennessee, and the GSA Southeastern Section 2025 program includes a Helene East Tennessee landslide abstract ([GSA SE 2025](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2025SE/webprogram/Paper408243.html), search summary). So a UT Knoxville team can credibly add a Tennessee and southern Blue Ridge piece to the Appalachia work.

- The geographic boundaries of the Appalachia observatory were not found: it may be Kentucky-only or may include the Helene region. Ask the observatory leads.

## Q5. Full evaluations of the strongest ideas

### CL1. Does flash drought set up or suppress the landslide cascade? Wet-dry preconditioning of rainfall-triggered landslides in Appalachia and Puerto Rico — Pursue

- **Call fit:** Priority 1 (cross-observatory hypothesis testing with existing data, including Puerto Rico), with Priority 3 (scaling remote-sensing products; a FAIR antecedent-wetness dataset, CL2) and a light Priority 2 hook (BMI-ready threshold function, CL5).
- **Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI; ET, soil moisture, flash drought); Yifan Gao (data pipeline, ML screening of predictors); Shi Jin (from Spring 2027; image and time-series ML, data QC). ORNL collaborators (Mao, Shi, Wang) as unfunded advisors on soil-moisture modeling. No subawards are allowed.
- **Research question:** Box 1 of the CLaSH strategic plan names "wet–dry cycles" as preconditioning. Does the antecedent hydro-climate state 1-90 days before an extreme storm shift the rainfall needed to trigger landslides, and does the sign differ between temperate Blue Ridge and Cumberland Plateau soils and tropical Puerto Rican soils? The state includes flash-drought onset, ET deficit and root-zone soil-moisture anomaly. Hypotheses: H1, short drought-wetting cycles raise infiltration through cracks and preferential flow and lower thresholds in clay-rich regolith. H2, deep root-zone drying adds storage capacity and raises thresholds. H3, the net effect depends on soil and critical-zone class, which links to the Puerto Rico and Southern California critical-zone work.
- **Approach (12 months):** (1) Compile landslide inventories for Helene (USGS, 2,217 landslides), the eastern Kentucky 2022 event (KGS, via the observatory) and Maria (>40,000 landslides). (2) Build daily ET and root-zone soil-moisture histories (the PI's PT soil-water-index and BMA ET methods, SMAP and reanalysis), with flash-drought indices. (3) Fit antecedent-conditioned rainfall threshold and logistic models per observatory, and compare across sites (cross-observatory test). (4) Release the antecedent-wetness cube and model code as a FAIR dataset, with a BMI-wrappable threshold function for the Modeling Collaboratory.
- **Closest existing work and the gap:**
  - Bessette-Kirton et al. (2019) linked Maria landslide distribution to antecedent soil moisture 11-13% above average — [GSA Today](https://www.geosociety.org/gsatoday/science/G383A/article.htm) (search summary). That is one event, wet-anomaly only, with no drought history.
  - GSA Today (January 2026) ties Helene landsliding to antecedent rain in the days before the storm — [GSA Today 2026](https://www.geosociety.org/gsa-today/january-2026/insights-into-widespread-landsliding-in-southern-appalachia-from-hurricane) (search summary). That covers days, not the weeks-to-months drought history.
  - Rahimikhameneh et al. (NHESS 2026) add normalized SMAP soil moisture to highway landslide thresholds — [NHESS 26, 3163](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/26/3163/2026/nhess-26-3163-2026.pdf) (search summary). They do not cover Helene or Puerto Rico, and do not test drought preconditioning or work across sites.
  - Aringoli et al. (2024, Italy) identify a preceding drought as a factor in triggering thresholds — [Aringoli 2024](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/bitstream/11581/489603/1/1059_GFDQ_Aringoli_2024_.compressed.pdf) (search summary). That is a single Mediterranean region.
  - Deijns et al. (EGU25) find preconditioning rainfall central for East African landslides and flash floods — [EGU25-10781](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-10781.html) (search summary).
  - Thomas, Mirus & Smith (2020) show Puerto Rican hillslopes "aren't always wet": seasonal drainage varies with soil-hydraulic properties — [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/hillslopes-humid-tropical-climates-arent-always-wet-implications-hydrologic-response) (search summary). This supports the hypothesis that dry phases matter even in the tropics.
  - NSF awards: RAPID 2629285 (UNC Charlotte) "Capturing Perishable Source-to-Impact Debris Flow Data from Hurricane Helene" covers runout field data, not antecedent hydro-climate — [NSF 2629285](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2629285). NSF API keyword searches for drought + landslide and evapotranspiration + landslide returned no award on drought preconditioning of landslides (searched 2026-11-20).
  - CLaSH internal work: Puerto Rico regional mechanistic triggering (Kassem, UC Berkeley) and MA2 task 2 ("soil moisture properties and dynamics … thresholds", initial focus Puerto Rico and Southern California) — [Annual Meeting](https://www.geoclash.org/2026-annual-meeting/); [Strategic Plan](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CLaSH-1.0-Five-Year-Strategic-Plan.pdf). The gap is a drought and ET history component tested across observatories, including Appalachia. This must be framed as complementing MA2 task 2, not duplicating it.
- **Novelty:** it moves from "wet antecedent conditions" (days) to a full wet-dry history (weeks to months, with flash-drought onset and ET deficit) as a preconditioning variable. It is tested as a hypothesis across temperate and tropical observatories, with a reusable FAIR antecedent-wetness product.
- **Why us (talent-pool facts only):**
  - The PI developed a soil-water-index Priestley-Taylor ET algorithm (Agric. For. Meteorol. 2019) and combined complementary-relationship ET models with BMA (Agric. For. Meteorol. 2019).
  - The PI published triple-collocation drought-index integration (SERRA 2022), drought recovery-time comparisons (SERRA 2022), flash-drought WUE and recovery from observations and models (Agric. For. Meteorol. 2026), and heatwave effects on CONUS hydrology for 2003-2022 (J. Hydrol. 2024). He led the review "Soil moisture controls over carbon sequestration and GHG emissions" (npj Clim. Atmos. Sci. 2025).
  - The PI is Co-PI on the FFAR "multi-source remote sensing-based framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods" ($434K, 2024-04), and Topic Lead of the RUBISCO Soil Moisture Working Group mini-workshops (2023-2025).
  - ORNL E3SM collaborators are available for modeling support.
  - This is hydro-climate expertise CLaSH names as wanted ("climatologists") and does not yet have at the observatories.
- **Fit to review criteria and past-winner patterns:**
  - Intellectual Merit: answers IM.2 and Box 1 directly.

### CL3. How long does a landslide cascade last? Satellite ecohydrologic recovery of landslide and debris-flow scars across three event ages — Pursue (alternative lead)

- **Call fit:** Priority 1 (cross-observatory, existing data, Puerto Rico included) and Priority 4 (the "long tail of site response"). It answers IM.4 ("How long do cascading hazards last?").
- **Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI; drought recovery-time and ecosystem recovery methods); Shi Jin (segmentation of scars and change detection from imagery, multi-sensor registration); Yifan Gao (data pipeline, statistics).
- **Research question:** After landslides and debris flows strip vegetation, how long do greenness, ET and water-use efficiency take to recover, and what controls that? Candidate controls: climate, soil and critical-zone class, scar size, post-event drought. Recovery is expected to be faster in tropical Puerto Rico (Maria 2017, Fiona 2022) than in the Kentucky (2022) and Helene (2024) scars. Does slow ET recovery predict reactivation? Kassem reported that some Maria slopes reactivated during Fiona — [April 2026 newsletter](https://mailchi.mp/geoclash/clash-newsletter-2026-april).
- **Approach:** map scars from existing inventories; build Landsat/Sentinel-2/MODIS NDVI and ET time series on scars and paired undisturbed controls; estimate recovery time with the PI's drought-recovery-time framework; relate recovery time to climate, soil, scar geometry and later rainfall; test whether slow recovery predicts reactivation (Puerto Rico Maria → Fiona). Deliverable: a FAIR "scar recovery" dataset across observatories.
- **Closest existing work and the gap:** CLaSH names "Loss and regrowth of vegetation changes slope stability" (Box 1) and IM.4 but lists no recovery-tracking task at the observatories — [Strategic Plan](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CLaSH-1.0-Five-Year-Strategic-Plan.pdf). Event inventories (USGS Helene; Bessette-Kirton Maria) map where landslides happened, not how they recover. Landslide4Sense and similar benchmarks detect new landslides, not recovery trajectories — [Landslide4Sense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00515). A targeted literature search for post-landslide vegetation recovery from satellites was **not** done in this session (gap; must be done before claiming novelty, because recovery studies exist for earthquake landslides such as Wenchuan and Gorkha).
- **Novelty:** recovery is measured with ecohydrologic metrics (ET, WUE) as well as greenness, compared across temperate and tropical observatories, and linked to reactivation in a later storm.
- **Why us:** the PI's papers on recovery of ecosystem carbon and water fluxes after drought (J. Hydrol. 2023), drought recovery time with two drought indices (SERRA 2022), and flash-drought WUE and recovery (Agric. For. Meteorol. 2026); "Flash flourishing" vegetation drivers (npj 2026, co-author); ET partitioning (J. Hydrol. 2021, co-author). Shi Jin: YOLO/EfficientNet detection and classification, multi-sensor registration (infrared + LiDAR SLAM).
- **Fit:** Relevance is strong (IM.4, long tail, cross-observatory). Innovation: new ecohydrology lens. Feasibility: all satellite data, so 12 months is realistic. It matches the remote-sensing + AI pattern.
- **Missing expertise:** a geomorphologist or forest ecologist for vegetation-root reinforcement (a Puerto Rico ecologist type fits, since UPR's Restrepo works on plant-microbe-rock weathering in landslide terrain — [Annual Meeting](https://www.geoclash.org/2026-annual-meeting/)); an observatory inventory partner.
- **Risks:** novelty is unproven until the literature check is done; small scars (<30 m) are below Landsat/MODIS resolution, so Sentinel-2 or Planet may be needed (budget); Helene scars are only about 2.5 years old by 2027, so trajectories are short.
- **Verdict: Pursue as the alternative to CL1. Do not submit both as separate proposals** (see Q6). Choose CL1 if the literature check finds existing satellite recovery studies of landslide scars; choose CL3 if the gap holds and a Puerto Rico ecology partner signs on.
