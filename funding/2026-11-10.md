#### Ideas: SERDP FY2028 Core — Data Fusion for Natural Infrastructure Monitoring

**What the SON rewards.** The SON asks for general, rigorous, validated statistical data fusion for natural infrastructure on DoW lands, across five challenges: (1) integrate emerging with legacy monitoring, "including use of contemporary observations to improve inferences from legacy monitoring records"; (2) combine multiple emerging technologies; (3) reconcile spatial and temporal scales; (4) spatiotemporal fusion; (5) characterize and propagate error and uncertainty ([SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)). Every proposal must "evaluate the performance, efficiency, and limitations of data-fusion approaches relative to single-source approaches, including differences in accuracy, precision, spatial coverage, uncertainty, cost, and labor requirements", and investigators are expected to "work closely with natural resource or installation range management communities in project design and execution." The named variables lean toward ecology (habitat, species occurrence and abundance), but "environmental variables", "hazard assessments" and "mission relevant environmental conditions" are also in scope, so a drought-stress or fuel-condition product must be framed as a condition variable managers use. Under the FY28 BAA, Relevance is pass/fail and "Technical Merit is significantly more important than Personnel, which is more important than Cost"; technical merit rewards hypothesis-driven tasks with milestones and go/no-go points; an optional "Existing Support" appendix lists overlapping funded work; each pre-proposal answers one SON ([FY28 BAA instructions](https://sepub-prod-0001-124733793621-us-gov-west-1.s3.us-gov-west-1.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2026-11/FY28%20SERDP%20CORE%20BAA%20Proposal%20Instructions.pdf?VersionId=gMdAcNjcRrnB_qAuB0cLvKnKMCL85XMY)). Pre-proposal (5 pages) due **2027-01-07, 2:00 PM ET** in SEMS. **Standard** proposals run 2-5 years; **Limited Scope** proposals are up to **$350,000 for about 1 year**, and the SON invites them for "computationally efficient algorithms, and efficient approaches for working with natural infrastructure datasets of large size and high dimensionality." **Bottom line:** send one pre-proposal to this SON — S1 (Standard, with S2 as its decision aim) if an installation partner and a domain co-investigator are confirmed by about 2026-12-15, otherwise S4 (Limited Scope). The largest weakness: the talent pool shows no DoW installation contact, ecologist, or fire scientist.

##### S1. Uncertainty-aware fusion of legacy and emerging water-stress observations for continuous ecosystem-condition monitoring on Southeastern installations — Pursue (lead, Standard)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao (PI: ET modeling, Bayesian Model Averaging (BMA), drought, remote sensing, engineering statistics); Yifan Gao (geospatial analysis; scenario and robust-optimization decision layer); Shi Jin (data pipelines and ETL, ML, computer-vision cloud and quality screening). Possible advisors, involvement unconfirmed: ORNL collaborators listed in [hao-lab.md](../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md). To recruit, all unconfirmed: a DoW installation natural resources or range partner, an ecologist or forest scientist, and optionally a spatial statistician.

**Research question and approach.** Can hierarchical Bayesian fusion beat every single source, over 2000-present, on accuracy, 30 m to 1 km coverage, and calibrated uncertainty of installation drought-stress and vegetation-condition metrics, and can contemporary high-resolution observations retroactively sharpen the legacy record (SON challenge 1)? Inputs: legacy MODIS-era ET, LST and vegetation indices; VIIRS, ECOSTRESS, HLS, and SMAP; flux towers; installation field records. Tasks: T1 source error characterization (triple and extended collocation; flux-tower validation); T2 hierarchical Bayesian/BMA spatiotemporal fusion with explicit sensor-transition terms (MODIS→VIIRS, ECOSTRESS gaps, HLS cadence); T3 back-casting the legacy record; T4 the required fusion-vs-single-source benchmark (accuracy, precision, coverage, uncertainty, compute cost, field-labor equivalents); T5 a manager-facing condition and uncertainty product co-designed with installation staff. Go/no-go after T1–T2: does fusion beat the best single source on held-out towers or field data?

**Closest existing projects and papers, and the gap.** BMA-ET fuses 30 ET datasets globally against FLUXNET2015 ([ESSD 17, 6445, 2025](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6445/2025/)); multi-product ET fusion has been tested against 462 flux towers ([Xu et al., AFM 2025](https://lee-lab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-05/xu_afm_2025.pdf)); triple-collocation ET fusion is published ([Atmosphere 15, 1410](https://mdpi.com/2073-4433/15/12/1410)). PML-V2.2 handles MODIS/VIIRS/AVHRR continuity only at 500 m or coarser ([ESSD preprint](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2026-94/essd-2026-94-manuscript-version2.pdf)). ECOSTRESS STARS fuses VIIRS with HLS at 30 m but LP DAAC flags lower-quality periods and VIIRS-dominated artifacts when HLS is sparse ([NASA Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-eco-l3g-jet-002)). Within SERDP, the nearest precedent is a one-year Interior Alaska multi-sensor project with data through 2017 (page now "Page Not Found"; search snippet only; [project 358c7356](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/358c7356-da06-46b9-b58d-653849375eb4)); RC25-0189 (LANL, vegetation stress uncertainty) overlaps in wording but its scope could not be verified (snippet only). **Gap:** no funded SERDP project was found that, for Southeastern installations, (a) fuses legacy and emerging ET, soil-moisture, and vegetation records with flux towers, (b) uses explicit error models rather than plain averaging, (c) propagates uncertainty to a decision metric, and (d) reports cost and labor against single-source products.

**Novelty.** Real but methodologically incremental: sensor-transition and back-casting terms, uncertainty carried to a manager-facing metric, and the cost/labor benchmark. Do not pitch it as "BMA of ET extended".

**Why us.** PI's multi-source ET fusion record: generalized complementary relationship models with BMA over China (*AFM* 279, 2019); BMA of hydrological drivers of ET over Northeast Asia (*J. Hydrol.* 607, 2022); co-author, "Integration of multiple drought indices using a triple collocation approach" (*SERRA* 36, 2022); co-author, Simple Taylor Skill Fusion merged ET (ISRS 2018); soil-water-index Priestley-Taylor ET (*AFM* 279, 2019); ET partitioning across biomes (*J. Hydrol.* 603, 2021). U.S. observations: flash-drought WUE and recovery (*AFM* 378, 2026), Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux talk (AGU 2024), CONUS heatwave hydrology (*J. Hydrol.* 637, 2024). Co-PI on the FFAR grant "A multi-source remote sensing-based framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods under climate change" ($434,038, from 04/2024). Lead author of CIVE v1.0 (*Ecological Indicators*, under second revision). Teaches IE 200 Engineering Statistics and IE 465/565 Applied Data Science. Shi Jin built a 350K-paper ETL and retrieval platform with ingestion-time schema validation. Note: the PI's BMA papers cover East Asia, not U.S. installations.

**Fundability.** Hits SON challenges 1, 3, 4, and 5; uses public earth-observation data; builds the mandatory benchmark in as T4; the hypothesis-driven tasks and go/no-go match the BAA's technical-merit language. Weak on installation engagement and Personnel until an installation partner and an ecologist co-investigator are confirmed. Fits a 3-year Standard proposal; typical award size not stated.

**Risks.** (1) No DoW installation partner or access to installation field records — the biggest risk to Relevance and Personnel. (2) Reviewers may see drought/ET as peripheral to species and habitat; tie metrics to at-risk species habitat condition, training-land condition, and burn windows. (3) Overlap with the FFAR grant and October idea G1 (see below) must be declared in Existing Support. (4) Flux-tower density near candidate installations not checked.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Frame on legacy-to-emerging continuity and uncertainty; name 2-3 Southeastern installations only once a partner confirms; add an ecologist or fire/forest co-investigator; prepare a one-page AmeriFlux pilot (fused vs. single-source) as supporting data.

##### S2. Flash drought to fuel-condition fusion for prescribed-burn windows and wildfire risk on Southeastern training lands — Revise (Aim 3 of S1)

**Team.** Hao (flash drought, soil moisture), Gao (burn-window decision layer), Jin (ML). Possible collaborators, unconfirmed: Jiafu Mao, Xiaoying Shi, Mingzhou Jin (co-authors on the wildfire review). Missing: a fire or fuels scientist and an installation fire-management partner.

**Approach.** Fuse SMAP and in-situ soil moisture, ET/LST flash-drought indicators, Sentinel-1 SAR and HLS vegetation water signals, and public fuel-moisture data from SERDP-facilitated burn campaigns ([Fort Stewart 2022](https://wifire-data.sdsc.edu/dataset/2022_fortstewart2022prescribedburncampaignfuelmoisturedata); [Eglin 2023](https://wifire-data.sdsc.edu/dataset/2023_fuelmoisturecontentdatafromthe2023departmentofdefensewildlandfirescienceinitiativeegli)) into calibrated live fuel moisture content (LFMC) and drought-stress maps with uncertainty; test whether they improve burn-window and fire-risk decisions over single-source indices.

**Closest work and gap.** LFMC remote sensing is mostly western U.S.: Sentinel-1 + Landsat-8 physics-assisted RNN ([Konings Lab](https://koningslab.stanford.edu/research/wildfire-behavior)); MODIS-only CONUS deep-learning projections ([Miller et al. 2023, IJWF](https://www.publish.csiro.au/wf/Fulltext/WF22188)); a 2025 foundation-model preprint ([arXiv 2506.20132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20132v2)). SERDP NH24-9046 collects burn-day fuel and weather data but builds no satellite fusion product ([NH24-9046](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/ace90b68-342a-4c56-8aec-f5dd4cf061db)). 2026 Southern Area advisories reported D3-D4 drought and reduced live fuel moisture driving fire behavior ([SACC 2026-05-14](https://gacc.nifc.gov/sacc/resources/predictive/Fuels_and_Fire_Behavior_Advisory_Southeastern_Coastal_Plain_051426.pdf)). No study fusing Southeast flash drought with LFMC was found (a search result, not proof).

**Why us.** Flash-drought papers (*AFM* 378, 2026; *J. Hydrol.* 624, 2023); soil moisture review (*npj CAS* 8, 2025); co-author, "Wildfire–Power Grid Interactions" (*RSER* 232, 2026) and the submitted review "Living with Fire in the Humid Southeastern US" (*Earth-Science Reviews*, under review). No fire-behavior or field-fuels record in the talent pool.

**Verdict: Revise.** Make it S1's decision-application aim. Split out only with a fire-science co-PI and an installation fire-program contact, and never send both to this SON.

##### S3. UAS + LiDAR + satellite computer-vision fusion for habitat and vegetation structure — Drop

Already well funded in SERDP: RC24-8162 terrestrial laser scanning in pine ecosystems ([RC24-8162](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/01612720-0b87-4cb5-8503-eb904fc67862)); RC-201667 multi-sensor airborne mapping ([RC-201667](https://serdp-estcp.mil:443/projects/details/8afe3241-365b-4fd7-a93c-bb141491ce8b)); RC19-5218 and RC18-5187 UAS at Marine Corps installations ([RC19-5218](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/9dd0dd09-5acd-4e88-a1c7-52451272a96d); [RC18-5187](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/49ff1f14-56a2-4fe9-bf10-84c46f34386b)); the literature is crowded too ([Remote Sensing 18, 1796](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01796/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01796.pdf)). Shi Jin's infrared + LiDAR and YOLO/EfficientNet work is industrial (substation digital twin), and the talent pool lists no ecology, forestry, or LiDAR publications. Salvage only as a covariate and quality-screening task inside S1.

##### S4. Scalable spatiotemporal fusion with calibrated uncertainty for large natural-infrastructure datasets (Limited Scope, up to $350K, about 1 year) — Revise (fallback to S1)

**Team.** Hao (PI; statistics and fusion), Jin (ML, pipelines), Gao (ML-assisted optimization, a stated interest).

**Approach.** An ML emulator of the S1 hierarchical Bayesian fusion that reproduces the posterior at a fraction of the compute, with conformal or ensemble calibration checked against triple-collocation error estimates, tested on 30 m multi-decade stacks for one or two installations against single-source products.

**Gap and fit.** No calibrated-uncertainty ML emulator of multi-sensor ET or condition fusion for installations was found (search not exhaustive). Matches the SON's explicit Limited Scope invitation and the "minimal supporting data" situation of a new Assistant Professor.

**Risks.** A pure-methods framing can fail the pass/fail Relevance screen unless tied to one installation variable and one user.

**Verdict: Revise.** Do not submit with S1. Decision rule: by about 2026-12-15, if an installation partner and an ecology or fire co-investigator are confirmed, submit S1 (with S2 as an aim); otherwise submit S4, tied to one variable (drought stress or fuel condition) and public data, as proof of concept for an FY2029 S1.
