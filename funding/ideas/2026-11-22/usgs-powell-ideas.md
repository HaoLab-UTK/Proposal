Notes for the 2026-11-22 scan. Researcher: deep-research subagent. Pages were downloaded with curl (full browser user-agent) and their text was extracted. "Why us" claims below use only facts in `talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md` and `talent-pool/resources/hao-lab/*.md`. No partner has been contacted, and no one has agreed to anything.

Call facts that matter for screening come from the [Proposal Submission Instructions](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/powell-ctr/proposal-submission-instructions) and the [Proposal Guidance PDF, released 2026-10-02](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf):

- **Review criteria (nine):** (1) compelling and urgent; (2) methods; (3) plan of action; (4) outcomes and results; (5) expertise; (6) justification for coming to the Powell Center ("Could the work be done elsewhere?"); (7) data availability; (8) model and data management; (9) unique contribution. Source: [Guidance PDF](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf), p. 10.
- **Tips (NCEAS rules of thumb):** proposals must be question-driven, not descriptive. They must explain why the work can only be done at the Powell Center, "with or without potential NSF co-funding". They should avoid jargon and say whether each participant has agreed. Source: [Guidance PDF](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf), p. 11.
- **Money:** the Center pays travel for up to 15 participants for two week-long meetings, page charges for one open-access paper, and **up to $100,000 over two years for a Fellow, including indirect costs**. Fellow money moves through CESU agreements to universities, and a successful group that asks for a Fellow at its own institution applies through a Grants.gov Announcement of Opportunity. No formal budget is needed at submission. Source: [Guidance PDF](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf), pp. 4-5. **Correction to the brief:** "no money to the lab" holds for PI salary, overhead and supplies. However, a Fellow (for example a postdoc) can be housed at UT through the CESU route.
- **Not supported:** new data collection or field research; projects "that should be funded by another entity or can be conducted elsewhere"; overhead or home-institution funds; supplies; field travel. Source: [Submission Instructions](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/powell-ctr/proposal-submission-instructions).
- **Advice:** the Center "strongly encourage[s]" contacting Interim Director Leslie Hsu before submitting. Unsuccessful proposals may be revised and resubmitted up to three times. Reviews usually come back within about six weeks. Source: [Guidance PDF](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf), pp. 9-10.
- **Other requirements:** the participant table must name a **USGS FSP Liaison** for Fundamental Science Practices, and each group has a data and computing liaison. Source: [Submission Instructions](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/powell-ctr/proposal-submission-instructions); [Guidance PDF](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/media/files/Powell%20Center%20Proposal%20Guidance%202026-10-02.pdf), p. 5.

## 1. Past winners: Powell working groups on drought, ET, water use, extremes, energy-water, wildfire and hazards

### Takeaway
The Powell Center funds a few groups each year: 6 of 22 proposals in FY25, 2 of 22 in FY26 and 4 of 16 in FY27. Winners pose one sharp, question-driven synthesis at national to global scale. They pool existing multi-network data into a new harmonized database and deliver a data release, synthesis papers and a framework a manager can use. Teams have 3-4 PIs that mix a USGS mission-area scientist with university and other-agency leads, and they are often co-sponsored by NSF (CZNet), Moore or NCEAS. Topics close to Hao Lab already won: an ET synthesis (2023), a U.S. water-use reanalysis (2019), multi-year drought (2020), ecological stability under heatwaves and droughts (2024), a fire-scar network (2022) and an energy-water precedent (hydraulic fracturing, 2012). New ideas must therefore avoid repeating these and state what they add.

### Inferences
- **Common patterns**
  - (a) One question tested across many existing networks (100+ drought experiment sites, flux towers, fire-scar trees, soil-moisture networks, county water-use records), not one region.
  - (b) A new harmonized database is a central product (GFID, CH4 flux database, soil-moisture database "ready for AI", fire-scar network), plus synthesis papers.
  - (c) A management hook: well permitting, land management, hazard models.
  - (d) 3-4 PIs. Most winners have at least one USGS PI from the mission area that owns the topic: Water Mission Area for water use and floods, Science Centers for ecology, CASC or Fort Collins for fire. Several are all-USGS.
  - (e) Frequent co-sponsorship (NSF CZNet, NSF, Moore, NCEAS), which strengthens the "why the Powell Center" case.
  - (f) ML appears as a tool inside a synthesis (flood impacts, soil moisture). It is never the headline.
- **Why they were likely selected:** each addressed a gap that no single lab could close because the data sat in separate communities. Each could show the data already existed (criterion 7), and each delivered something a USGS program or manager would use (criteria 1, 4, 9).
- **Implications for Hao Lab**
  - An ET-intercomparison idea is pre-empted by the 2023 ET group. A generic drought-ecosystem idea is crowded by the 2020 and 2024 groups.
  - Water use is a USGS-owned topic with a recent Powell group (2019), but that group did not cover data centers, which are not a USGS water-use category. That gap fits the "unique niche" criterion best.
  - ORNL scientists have been Powell PIs (Herndon, FY25), so Hao Lab's ORNL co-authors could credibly join as participants.

### Gaps

## 2. Candidate ideas (Hao Lab co-led with a USGS PI), screening, and full evaluations

### Takeaway
Ten short ideas were screened. Strongest is **PW1**: a national synthesis of data center and thermoelectric water use (withdrawal, consumption, thermal discharge) under drought and heat, led with a USGS water-use scientist. It fills a gap no Powell group or USGS product covers, uses existing USGS models, and draws on Hao Lab's two funded data center water grants. **PW2**, flash drought onset speed versus ecosystem water-carbon recovery across flux networks, is a strong second but needs sharper separation from the 2020 and 2024 Powell drought and stability groups and from tracked ideas G1, I1 and CL1. **PW3**, compound drought, heat and wildfire in the humid Southeast, is a Revise: it needs a fire-science USGS PI. The ET intercomparison idea is a Drop because the 2023 ET group already covers it.

### Cited Findings (evidence used in screening)

**Data center and thermoelectric water (PW1)**
- USGS thermoelectric work:
  - USGS has a physics-based thermoelectric water-use model with monthly estimates for **1,341 plants for 2008-2020**. Benchmark paper: Harris, Diehl, Gorman Sanisaca, Galanter, et al. 2025, *Environmental Modelling & Software* 185:106265 ([USGS pubs](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70262012)).
  - Contributing offices include the **Lower Mississippi-Gulf Water Science Center** ([USGS pubs](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70262012)).
  - Data releases: plant-month reanalysis [doi:10.5066/P9ZE2FVM](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9ZE2FVM) and condenser duty [doi:10.5066/P9XG876W](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9XG876W), both used in the 2025 National Water Availability Assessment ([NWAA model README](https://water.usgs.gov/nwaa-data/data/water-use/wu-thermoelectric/wu-thermoelectric_README.html)).
- The USGS industrial water-use release for 2020 (by county, HUC12 and month; dated 2026-04-22; [doi:10.5066/P14WTLVD](https://www.usgs.gov/data/industrial-water-use-2020-county-huc12-month-and-year-conterminous-united-states)) defines industry as NAICS 31-33 (manufacturing), self-supplied only. GPP falls below normal within 16 days and reaches its minimum within 24 days for more than 50% of events. Water use efficiency rises in forests and falls in cropland and savanna during recovery, and GPP recovery lags soil-moisture recovery ([HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/5579/2020/)).

**Fire (PW3)**

- The Gatlinburg fires burned more than 11,000 ha and killed 14 people ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5956230)). A NIDIS-funded 2016 Southeast drought and wildfire assessment exists ([drought.gov](https://drought.gov/drought-research/2016-southeast-drought-and-wildfire-assessment)).

### Full evaluation PW1: Water for the AI build-out (Pursue)

- **Title:** "Water for the AI build-out: a national synthesis of data center and power-sector water withdrawal, consumption and thermal discharge under drought and heat." Short title: "AI Water Nexus".
- **Call:** USGS Powell Center working group, due 2027-01-31. Keywords: water; energy and minerals; climate and land use change.
- **Team (up to four PIs; none contacted):**
  - USGS PI (required): a USGS water-use scientist. Best candidate is Melissa A. Harris (LMG WSC, Nashville), who led the automated thermoelectric model and co-leads the national industrial water-use team.
  - Co-PI: Yuefeng Hao (UT).
  - Co-PI: a data center water-footprint academic, for example Landon Marston (Virginia Tech; past Powell water-use PI).
  - Optional fourth PI from a national lab, for example Arman Shehabi (LBNL).
  - Participants (up to 15 total): Yifan Gao and Shi Jin (siting, NLP and ETL); ORNL Earth system modelers from the registry (Jiafu Mao); Mingzhou Jin (UT optimization); a utility or state water agency participant; a social-science or policy participant.
  - Fellow: a postdoc at UT through the $100K Fellow route, which is the only money to UT.
- **Synthesis question:** Where and when does the growth of AI data centers, counting direct cooling water plus water at the power plants that supply them, push water withdrawals, consumption and thermal discharges past what drought-year and heatwave-year streamflow and river temperatures can absorb?
- **Sub-questions:**
  - (a) How large is data center water use at HUC12 and monthly scale, a category USGS water-use models do not yet estimate separately?
  - (b) How much of it is indirect, through thermoelectric plants?
  - (c) Which basins move from low to high stress in drought and heat years?
- **Approach (existing data only):**
  1. Build a harmonized national facility layer from the inventories behind Siddik et al. 2021 and the LBNL 2024/2025 reports. Add text-mined public permits and utility filings (Gao's NLP; Jin's ETL platform).
  2. Link each facility to the generators that serve it and to the USGS thermoelectric plant-month reanalysis (2008-2020) and condenser-duty data. Update to recent years with the automated model code.
  3. Place direct use within USGS public-supply HUC12 estimates where utilities serve the facility.
  4. Stress-test against USGS streamflow and water-temperature records and the NWAA water budgets for the driest and hottest years, using the PI's flash-drought and heatwave event definitions.
  5. Use ML gap-filling and uncertainty bounds where facility water data are missing.
  6. Products: a USGS data release of the data center water layer; a synthesis paper; and a screening framework that state agencies could use in permitting, following the pattern of the FY27 lakes-and-well-permitting group.
- **Named existing datasets:**
  - USGS thermoelectric reanalysis [doi:10.5066/P9ZE2FVM](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9ZE2FVM) and condenser duty [doi:10.5066/P9XG876W](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9XG876W).
  - USGS industrial water use 2020 [doi:10.5066/P14WTLVD](https://www.usgs.gov/data/industrial-water-use-2020-county-huc12-month-and-year-conterminous-united-states).
  - USGS public-supply reanalysis 2000-2020 ([USGS data](https://www.usgs.gov/data/public-supply-water-use-reanalysis-2000-2020-period-huc12-month-and-year-conterminous-united)).
  - NWAA Data Companion ([README](https://water.usgs.gov/nwaa-data/data/water-use/wu-thermoelectric/wu-thermoelectric_README.html)).
  - LBNL 2024 report ([PDF](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/us_data_center_energy_usage_report_lbnl-2001637.pdf)) and Siddik et al. 2021 ([LBNL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/environmental-footprint-data-centers)).
  - EIA plant data and USGS NWIS streamflow and temperature are named in the idea but were not opened in this check.
- **Closest existing projects and the gap:**
  - Powell "Reanalyzing and Predicting U.S. Water Use" (2019; [page](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/john-wesley-powell-center-for-analysis-and-synthesis/science/reanalyzing-and-predicting-us)). It fused economic and water-use data by county and watershed, before the AI load surge, and with no data center category. Gap: a sector that did not exist at that scale in 2019.
  - USGS thermoelectric model ([Harris et al. 2025](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70262012)) covers plants, not the loads that drive them, and ends in 2020.
  - USGS industrial model covers NAICS 31-33 only (from a search summary of its metadata).
  - Siddik et al. 2021 ([ERL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/environmental-footprint-data-centers)) is an annual, pre-2021 footprint with no drought-year stress test.
  - Han et al. 2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02705)) covers peak demand on public water systems, with no basin hydrology or thermal discharge.
  - LBNL 2024 is a national total. Critics note definition and boundary problems ([Pacific Institute](https://pacinst.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Issue-Brief_AI-Data-Centers-2026_Final.pdf); [ITIF](https://www2.itif.org/2026-data-centers-water.pdf)).
  - Powell "Hydraulic Fracturing and Water Resources" (2012; [page](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/john-wesley-powell-center-for-analysis-and-synthesis/science/hydraulic-fracturing-and-water)) is the precedent: a new energy sector's water effects synthesized nationally.
  - **Gap:** no national, basin-scale, monthly synthesis joins data center direct and indirect water with drought and heat supply limits and thermal discharge, and no USGS water-use category covers it.
- **Novelty:** the first national data center water-use layer consistent with USGS water-use models. It also introduces a drought- and heat-conditioned stress test that turns a contested annual national number into basin-month risk.
- **Why us (talent pool facts only):**
  - The PI holds the funded USGS 104b TDEC grant on freshwater withdrawals and thermal discharges from data centers and SMRs in Tennessee, and the funded UT ISSE grant on the local water footprint of AI data centers.
  - The PI has a submitted paper on electricity-related water consumption for projected data center load growth.
  - The PI's flash-drought and heatwave hydrology papers (AFM 2026; J. Hydrol. 2024) supply the drought and heat stress definitions.
  - Students Yifan Gao (NLP extraction from policy documents; geospatial siting) and Shi Jin (350K-record ETL and retrieval platform; Tennessee Valley siting under tariffs and moratoria) cover facility-layer building.
  - The PI has led a working-group workshop: Topic Lead of the DOE RUBISCO Soil Moisture Working Group mini-workshop, 2023-2025.
  - Not a "why us" claim: no USGS, LBNL or Virginia Tech person has agreed to join.
- **Fundability against the nine criteria:**
  - (1) Compelling and urgent: strong. AI load growth is current, and national numbers conflict.
  - (2-3) Methods and plan: sound if the facility layer is scoped carefully.
  - (4) Outcomes: data release plus framework.
  - (5) Expertise: strong only with the USGS water-use PI and a footprint academic on board.
  - (6) Why Powell: the data sit in USGS water-use models, LBNL inventories, utility and state filings and academic footprints, and no single group holds them all, which matches the 2019 water-use group's pattern.
  - (7) Data availability: the main risk, because facility water data are partly proprietary. Use public inventories and model-based estimates with uncertainty.
  - (8) Data management: USGS data release via the USGS PI.
  - (9) Unique contribution: high.
- **Risks:**
  - (a) A USGS PI must agree. Without one the proposal is ineligible, so contact Harris or another water-use scientist and Leslie Hsu in early December.
  - (b) It could read as "should be funded by another entity" (DOE or NSF). Answer: synthesis of existing USGS and LBNL data that requires convening.
  - (c) Overlap with tracked ideas: 104g U1 (Tennessee estimation), CAREER K1/K2, EWRE E1, BER B1, and especially the funded 104b TDEC project. Keep Powell national and existing-data-only; list all in current and pending support. The Powell Center funds only travel and a Fellow, so budget overlap is small, but scientific overlap must be explained.
  - (d) Facility locations and water data may be commercially sensitive. Aggregate to HUC12 and county.
  - (e) No money for PI time.
  - (f) An early-career PI. Mitigate with experienced co-PIs, including the past Powell PI.
- **Verdict: Pursue.** Lead with the USGS water-use PI. Ask Leslie Hsu whether a data center water synthesis fits the USGS mission and whether co-sponsorship (for example DOE or LBNL) is welcome.

### Full evaluation PW2: Does onset speed set recovery? (Pursue as second; sharpen)

- **Title:** "Fast in, slow out? A continental synthesis of flash drought onset speed and ecosystem water-carbon recovery." Short title: "Flash Recovery".
- **Call:** same. Keywords: ecosystems; water; natural hazards; climate and land use change.
- **Team (none contacted):**
  - USGS PI: Jennifer M. Cartwright (SE CASC Science Coordinator, Nashville; forest drought impacts).
  - Second USGS option: Brent T. Aulenbach (SAWSC; flash drought, AET and wildfire at Panola Mountain).
  - Alternative USGS PI with a Powell record: Gabriel Senay (EROS; SSEBop; past Powell ET PI).
  - Co-PI: Yuefeng Hao.
  - Third co-PI: a flash-drought or flux community lead, for example Jason Otkin (UW-Madison) or Di Tian (Auburn).
  - Participants: ORNL modelers from the registry (Jiafu Mao, Yaoping Wang, Daniel Ricciuto); flux-site scientists from the registry (Lianhong Gu, Jeffrey Wood); Shi Jin (data pipeline).
  - Fellow: a postdoc at UT.
- **Synthesis question:** Across North American biomes, do droughts that develop fast (flash droughts) leave longer or shorter ecosystem water-carbon recovery than slow droughts of equal depth? Does climate history or ecosystem type explain the difference?
- **Approach (existing data only):**
  1. Detect events consistently from soil moisture and ET anomalies, using SSEBop, MODIS and soil-moisture networks.
  2. Pair each event with flux tower GPP, ET and water use efficiency (AmeriFlux/FLUXNET) and with satellite greenness.
  3. Estimate recovery times with the PI's methods (drought recovery-time and index comparison; triple collocation).
  4. Use hierarchical statistical models and ML to separate onset speed from severity, timing and biome.
  5. Products: a public event-recovery database and a manager-oriented indicator of "recovery debt".
- **Closest existing work and the gap:**
  - Zhang and Yuan 2020 ([HESS](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/5579/2020/)): 29 FLUXNET sites, no fast-versus-slow comparison, FLUXNET2015 era.
  - The PI's own single-site AFM 2026 paper (Missouri Ozark).
  - AmeriFlux 22-tower drought and heat synthesis (seen only in search results).
  - Powell multi-year drought group ([2020](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/john-wesley-powell-center-for-analysis-and-synthesis/science/a-global-synthesis-multi-year)): imposed experiments, multi-year, not rapid onset.
  - Powell ecological stability group ([2024](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/john-wesley-powell-center-for-analysis-and-synthesis/science/quantifying-drivers-ecological)): diversity times climate history, mostly biological community data.
  - NSF CAIG 2616279/2616278 (2026): AI forecasting of flash-drought development and propagation, not recovery.
  - NSF 2303458/2303457: land-atmosphere feedbacks.
  - **Gap:** no synthesis has tested onset speed against recovery across networks. Confidence is moderate; the literature search was not exhaustive.
- **Novelty:** turns flash drought from a forecasting topic into a recovery-legacy question, with a reusable event database.
- **Why us (talent pool facts only):**
  - PI papers on flash drought water use efficiency and recovery (AFM 2026), drought recovery of carbon and water fluxes (J. Hydrol. 2023), flash drought ecosystem effects (2023), drought-index recovery time (SERRA 2022), triple collocation (2022) and ET methods (PT, CR plus BMA, partitioning).
  - FFAR flash drought tool (Co-PI).
  - ORNL co-authors.
  - RUBISCO working-group topic lead.
- **Fundability:**
  - Strong on criteria 7 and 2: open data, clear methods.
  - Moderate on criterion 9: reviewers may see it as adjacent to the 2020 and 2024 Powell groups, so the proposal must say what those groups did not do.
  - Criterion 1: urgency comes from Southeast events and management (SE CASC).
- **Risks:**
  - The USGS PI must agree.
  - Overlap with tracked NSF GEO **G1** (flash drought recovery thresholds in humid Southeastern ecosystems), 104(b) **I1/I2** and CLaSH **CL1/CL3**. Powell would be the continental synthesis, while G1 is process science at sites. If both are funded, the event database must be shared and declared in current and pending support.
  - A second Powell proposal with the same lead PI in the same year may be unwise. Ask Leslie Hsu.
- **Verdict: Pursue as the alternate,** or hold for the FY2029 round if PW1 goes in. Sharpen the fast-versus-slow framing and name the SE CASC user need.
