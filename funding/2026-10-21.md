AI/ML is "a central requirement" ([NOFO p.5](https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/2c5fcf26-2264-42ae-9bc9-60f591fd770c/attachments/1931b7a5-8a2e-4007-a158-cbf31be312cd/Foa_Content_of_G27AS00164_100226.pdf)). The most important external fact is a USGS Water Mission Area project on data center water use. **Recommendation: submit one merged proposal, "Observe–Estimate–Forecast: an AI framework for data center water use in Tennessee."** Aim 1 is the observing layer (U3 + U4), Aim 2 is validated estimation (U1), and Aim 3 is forecasting under extremes and policy (U2).

##### U1. Tennessee data center water use: hybrid physics-ML estimation validated against independent records — Pursue (merged lead)

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI), Yifan Gao, Shi Jin; Mingzhou Jin as a possible collaborator (UT ISE/ISSE, listed in hao-lab.md; participation not confirmed).

**Research question and approach.** Can Scope 1 water use (direct use from public supply and self-supply) of Tennessee data centers be estimated at the facility or county level with calibrated uncertainty, from public and utility data? Can Scope 2 (thermoelectric water tied to their electricity) be attributed on top of that? The model chain is a hybrid physics-ML estimator: IT load → cooling technology → water-use effectiveness, plus grid mix → plant-level water intensity. It produces probabilistic outputs and is validated on 3–5 Tennessee facilities with utility or permit data obtained through letters of support.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Siddik, Shehabi and Marston (2021) gave the first spatially detailed US water footprint of data centers ([LBNL](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/bibcite/reference/34978)). The Ren group works at national and peak-capacity scale ([arXiv 2304.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03271v4); [arXiv 2603.02705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02705)). Minnesota Sea Grant funds a facility-level real-time estimation dashboard for Minnesota ([MN Sea Grant](https://seagrant.umn.edu/research/ai-water-impact)). The USGS project above covers Scope 1 only. None found does ML-based, uncertainty-quantified, validated Scope 1+2 estimation for one balancing authority (TVA).

**Novelty.** Methods are incremental, but geography and validation are strong. The project also directly fills the Scope 2 gap that USGS's own project leaves open.

**Why us.** The PI holds the ISSE grant on the local water footprint of AI data centers in Tennessee and the USGS 104b TDEC grant on data center and SMR withdrawals. The PI also has a submitted paper, "Regional screening of electricity-related water consumption for projected data-center load growth" (with Kaylee Jin, *Water Resources and Industry*, under review), which is the direct precursor for Scope 2. Gao's and Jin's ongoing Tennessee Valley siting papers supply the demand side.

**Fundability.** It hits "estimating, validating, forecasting", the AI/ML requirement and "validating results against independent or ground truth data" almost verbatim. Training points (10) are covered by naming Gao and Jin as federally supported students.

**Risks.** Ground-truth access (utility billing at MLGW, KUB and others) is unconfirmed. Whether Tennessee withdrawal registration data are accessible was not verified. $120K over 3 years is a small budget. The program screens for overlap with the 104b project, so the proposal must say plainly that 104b produces a TDEC decision tool while 104g produces validated estimation methods.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Lead with Scope 1 from public supply, as the NOFO frames it, and present Scope 2 as the "associated" component that complements the USGS project.

##### U2. Seasonal, heatwave and flash-drought forecasts of data center water demand under siting-policy scenarios — Pursue (Aim 3 of U1)

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao, Yifan Gao, Shi Jin.

**Research question and approach.** How do weather-driven peak cooling demand and flash-drought or heatwave stress on source water combine with policy-aware build-out (tariffs, moratoria, interconnection queues) to shape future demand on Tennessee public supply?

**Closest existing work and gap.** "Small Bottle, Big Pipe" projects national peak-day capacity needs of 697–1,451 MGD through 2030 ([arXiv 2603.02705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02705)). "AI Data Centers and the Water Use Feedback Loop" finds burdens of 0.2%–134% of host capacity across ten sites ([arXiv 2606.21760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21760)). An ACS study finds about 25% of 9,347 data centers face water stress at least one month a year ([ACS](https://www.acs.org/pressroom/presspacs/2026/october/many-data-centers-are-located-where-water-resources-are-limited.html)). None couples policy-aware siting scenarios with process-based flash-drought and heatwave hydrology at local supply scale.

**Novelty.** Real. It joins three things no one has joined.

**Why us.** The PI's papers on heatwave effects on CONUS hydrology (*J. Hydrol.* 2024) and on flash-drought ecosystem effects and recovery. Shi Jin's "Siting Under Moving Rules" (tariff- and moratorium-aware siting in the Tennessee Valley) and Yifan Gao's robust siting under interconnection-queue uncertainty.

**Fundability.** It answers "forecast future scenarios" and "seasonal variability", and supports EO14239 local preparedness.

**Risks.** Forecasts cannot be validated within the award, and on its own the idea reads as planning. That is why it is an aim of U1, not a standalone proposal.

**Verdict: Pursue as Aim 3 of U1.**

##### U3. Computer-vision detection of data center cooling technology — Revise (Aim 1 data layer)

**Team:** Shi Jin, Yuefeng Hao.

**Approach and change.** The original idea, quantifying evaporative loss from Landsat, ECOSTRESS or MODIS thermal and ET signals, is physically weak. Cooling towers are building-scale. ECOSTRESS is 70 m, ET algorithms are calibrated for vegetation, and its industrial use so far is detecting once-through thermal plumes ([ECOSTRESS poster](https://ecostress.jpl.nasa.gov/downloads/other/ECOSTRESS_aquatic_applications_poster.pdf)). The revised version classifies cooling technology (wet towers, dry or adiabatic coolers, chillers) from high-resolution imagery, following WRI's satellite method for power plants. That method reached 90% precision on cooling type and 69% on water demand across 200 US plants ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/research/methodology-estimate-water-demand-thermal-power-plants-data-scarce-regions-using-satellite)). Thermal anomalies serve only as signals of operating status.

**Gap.** No satellite or aerial detection of data center cooling type was found.

**Why us.** Shi Jin's YOLO/EfficientNet inspection models and SLAM-based infrared + LiDAR registration for a substation digital twin; the PI's MODIS and multi-source remote sensing work. Shi Jin starts in Spring 2027, so this aim should be scheduled from year 1, semester 2.

**Verdict: Revise.** Use it as the "observing" input to U1, not as a standalone proposal.

However, the USGS project already runs NLP over the literature and utility filings ([Zintellect](https://www.zintellect.com/Opportunity/Details/DOI-USGS-2026-33)), and corporate disclosures are voluntary and not comparable ([AIhub/The Conversation](https://aihub.org/2025/09/24/data-centers-consume-massive-amounts-of-water-companies-rarely-tell-the-public-exactly-how-much)).

**Why us.** Gao's undergraduate NLP and information-extraction work and the planned source-linked dataset of data center requirements; Jin's 350K-paper ETL and retrieval platform.

**Verdict: Revise.** Offer the Tennessee dataset to the USGS team as a complement. This strengthens the "integration with USGS science" element instead of competing with USGS.

##### B1. Water availability for Tennessee Valley energy expansion under compound flash drought-heat extremes: AI-enhanced E3SM-RRM coupled to large-load siting — Pursue

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI); ORNL collaborators listed in hao-lab.md (Jiafu Mao, Xiaoying Shi, Yaoping Wang, Daniel M. Ricciuto, Peter E. Thornton, Forrest M. Hoffman; participation and funding mechanism to be confirmed); Yifan Gao and Shi Jin for the large-load use case.

**Research question and approach.** Do compound flash drought-heat events in the Tennessee Valley cut summer cooling-water availability on weeks-to-months timescales in ways that siting assumptions based on annual or HUC2 averages miss? There are four work packages: (1) E3SM-RRM/ELM compound-extreme runs over the Southeast; (2) ET, T/ET and WUE recovery constraints from AmeriFlux and BMA-merged ET products; (3) a drought-regime surrogate for large ensembles (B2); and (4) sub-basin, seasonal water availability and cooling-water stress as inputs to data center and SMR siting.

**Closest existing work and gap.** PNNL's BER-funded IM3/GCAM-USA work covers data center water at state or HUC2 scale, focused on the West ([OSTI 2998851](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2998851); [OSTI 2589826](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2589826)). Alston, Love and Haider model data center energy and water for Michigan ([arXiv 2609.25437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25437)). E3SMv2's North American RRM was built "to inform the US energy sector about changes in water availability... heat waves" ([Tang et al., GMD 2023](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3953/2023/)), but no Southeastern compound flash drought-heat application was found. None found couples ESM drought projections to large-load siting.

**Novelty.** Real, provided the hypothesis is about Earth-system predictability and optimization is the use case.

**Why us.** The PI is a co-author on the submitted overview of the E3SMv3 regionally refined configuration (*JAMES*, under review). The PI is lead author of CIVE v1.0 (*Ecological Indicators*, second revision) and PI of the DOE/ORNL vulnerability grant ($229,835, 5 years). The PI is also PI of the 104b TDEC data center + SMR water grant and the ISSE footprint grant, and leads two students who are building Tennessee Valley siting models. No other team found holds all of these.

**Fundability.** It mirrors the budget request's "target regions," "water availability for energy," "multi-sector dynamics" and "AI surrogate components." Program policy factors include "PI not previously supported by SC"; whether a lab subcontract counts as SC support is unknown.

**Risks.** BER may see the project as applied energy work unless the science leads. Reviewers will ask why not IM3; the answer is a different region, ESM extremes and sub-annual timing. The EESM budget is small. Both students are new.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Send a one-page concept to a BER EESM program manager first, keep the narrative to 15 pages or fewer, and submit before 2027-04-01.

##### B2. Regime-aware AI surrogate of E3SM land drought response and recovery — Revise

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao, Shi Jin, Yifan Gao, with ORNL collaborators.

**Approach and gap.** E3SM land-model surrogates already exist for methane, GPP-SIF, groundwater and fire ([Chen et al., JAMES 2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022MS003135); [GMD 2022](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1899/2022/)), and E3SM has released a flagship emulator 100 times faster than earlier capabilities ([FY2027 BER request](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fy-2027-biological-and-environmental-research-budget-request)). No drought- or recovery-specific emulator was found. The documented biases point in opposite directions. ORNL's publication record of the PI's flash-drought paper describes ELMv2 as prolonging GPP recovery after flash drought ([ORNL record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/evaluating-ecosystem-water-use-efficiency-and-recovery-dynamics-d/); external description, not a talent-pool fact). Multi-model studies find drought legacies about 4 times too short ([Kolus et al., Sci. Rep. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-39373-1)). A surrogate that depends on drought regime, constrained by flux observations, would address both.

**Why us (partial).** The PI's paper "Evaluating ecosystem water use efficiency and recovery dynamics during flash droughts: insights from observations and model simulations" (*Agricultural and Forest Meteorology* 378, 110982, 2026, with ORNL co-authors) and the PI's AGU 2024 talk on flash-drought recovery and WUE at the Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux site. The lab has no emulator publications. Shi Jin lists Earth system models only as an interest.

**Verdict: Revise.** Use it as B1's AI component, or as a standalone only if ORNL co-investigators lead the emulator architecture and Hao Lab leads the observational constraints and the decision use.

##### G1. Beyond soil-moisture recovery: thresholds, transpiration shifts and lagged carbon-water recovery after flash drought in humid Southeastern ecosystems — Pursue

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI); ORNL E3SM collaborators (to be confirmed); a new hydrology graduate student to be budgeted. Gao and Jin work on siting and should not be counted as hydrology personnel. Shi Jin could contribute a scientifically justified ML sub-aim for detecting regime shifts.

**Research question and approach.** H1: humid forests cross from energy-limited to water-limited ET only briefly, and the threshold shifts under concurrent heat (high VPD). H2: T/ET rises under heat during onset and stays perturbed after soil moisture recovers. H3: GPP and ET recovery lag soil-moisture recovery, with the lag set by VPD and refill of deep storage. H4: E3SM's land model reproduces recovery timing only when VPD-stomatal and deep-water processes are represented. Methods: AmeriFlux flux towers plus multi-source remote sensing; an ensemble of Priestley–Taylor and complementary-relationship models merged by BMA to quantify structural ET uncertainty during extremes; benchmarking with ORNL.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Zhang et al. analyzed 151 FLUXNET flash-drought events and found that WUE rises during recovery in forests but not in croplands ([HESS 2020](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/5579/2020/)). Flash droughts with extreme heat recover more slowly ([Nature Geoscience 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-025-01719-y)). A soil-moisture threshold controls coupling during drought-heatwaves ([Commun. Earth Environ. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02977-9)). Stoy's award partitions ET across the US but not specifically during compound extremes ([NSF 2422397](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2422397)). No funded project tests whether recovery follows soil moisture in humid Southeastern forests against ESM process representation.

**Novelty.** It tests a paradigm, which matches the solicitation's "transformative" language. Without the recovery and humid-regime framing, it would overlap 2414601 and 2303458.

**Why us.** The PI's flash-drought WUE and recovery paper (*AFM* 2026) and AGU 2024 Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux talk; the heatwave hydrology paper (*J. Hydrol.* 2024); drought-recovery papers (*J. Hydrol.* 2023, China and Korea); the ET-model portfolio (soil-water-index Priestley–Taylor, *AFM* 2019; complementary relationship + BMA, *AFM* 2019; BMA drivers of ET, *J. Hydrol.* 2022; conductance-based ET partitioning, *J. Hydrol.* 2021); and ORNL E3SM collaborators. Few NSF-funded flash-drought groups pair observed recovery diagnostics with an ESM team.

**Fundability.** It is foundational critical-zone and land-atmosphere science, produces a public event-recovery dataset (a stated measure of success), and benefits from the early-career priority.

**Risks.** Southeastern flux-tower coverage of the 2016 and 2019 flash droughts was not checked. The PI's ISE affiliation may raise questions in EAR, which the hydrology publication record answers. PT, CR and BMA may look like legacy methods unless tied to hypotheses.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Email a one-page summary to WaLCZ@nsf.gov and ask about typical award size.

##### G2. Does flash drought reach the deep critical zone? Vertical propagation into root-zone, groundwater and terrestrial water storage — Revise

**Change.** Replace "TWS in flash vs. conventional drought" with a hypothesis about propagation and legacy: does a surface deficit lasting weeks reach deep storage, and does storage memory predict the recovery lags in G1? The method combines GRACE/GRACE-FO with root-zone soil moisture and well data to bridge the weeks-vs-months scale gap.

**Gap.** A 2025 RSE paper maps global flash-drought hotspots from daily reconstructed GRACE ([RSE 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425725002974)) but does not study propagation mechanisms. The NSF API returned no TWS-plus-drought award.

**Why us.** The PI's co-authorship on long-term terrestrial water storage change (*J. Hydrol.* 2024, with ORNL) and the drought-recovery papers.

**Risks.** GRACE resolution and finding groundwater data.

##### E1. Drought- and heat-robust co-design of data center cooling technology, water sourcing and operations with grid-attributed water — Pursue

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao (PI), Yifan Gao, Shi Jin; Mingzhou Jin as possible optimization collaborator (not confirmed); a thermal-engineering Co-PI is needed (none in the talent pool).

**Research question and approach.** For a fixed set of candidate Tennessee Valley sites, which mix of cooling technologies (evaporative, hybrid or adiabatic, dry, liquid) and water sources minimizes a new **drought-conditioned, stress-weighted total (direct + indirect) water** metric at acceptable cost and reliability? The model is a two-stage distributionally robust optimization. Stage 1 chooses cooling portfolio and water source; stage 2 chooses hourly wet/dry switching and load flexibility. Ambiguity sets come from compound flash-drought and heatwave scenarios. Indirect water is decision-dependent: the cooling choice changes grid load, and indirect water is attributed at generator level across the TVA/SERC fleet, including derating at river-temperature limits. Following the cross-call partition, regional siting and capacity expansion stay in CAREER K1. E3 (reclaimed water) is an aim here, and E2 (SMR) is a case-study module.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Vabson et al. site data centers by county with a deterministic base model ([arXiv 2601.16315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16315)). Xiao, You et al. analyze national scenarios, estimating 731–1,125 million m³/yr for AI servers ([Nature Sustainability](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/536a055b-d8ab-4b86-bd4d-88facfaaf2ae/s41893-025-01681-y.pdf)). "Balancing Bits and Drops" uses static county-month AWARE stress factors to evaluate, not optimize ([arXiv 2607.22617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22617)). Alston et al. found that air cooling halves total water but raises indirect water by a third in Michigan, but they assess rather than optimize ([arXiv 2609.25437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25437)). None treats cooling choice as a decision that moves water between the site and the grid under drought-heat uncertainty.

**Novelty.** Decision-dependent indirect water under hydro-climate ambiguity, plus a new metric. This is a methods contribution, not a relabeling.

**Why us.** The PI's flash-drought and heatwave science supplies the uncertainty model. The PI's submitted electricity-related water screening paper supplies the grid-water attribution base. Gao's robust siting and Jin's tariff-aware Tennessee Valley models supply the optimization base. The ISSE and 104b grants supply data access and a state-agency stakeholder.

**Fundability.** It matches "AI modeling... novel metrics," the early-career priority and "novel connections across scientific domains." A 3-year budget of about $400K fits the observed norm.

**Risks.** The program director may route it to CMMI (operations research) or to CBET Transport Phenomena (cooling hardware). Keep water-resource limits (withdrawal and consumption caps, basin depletion, thermal limits) at the core. Credible cooling performance curves need a thermal Co-PI. The PI's submitted paper may overlap Alston et al. and must be distinguished from it.

**Verdict: Pursue.** Send a one-page summary to cbet-ewre@nsf.gov stressing water-resource management, and run a targeted Scopus search ("distributionally robust" + "data center" + "water") before writing.

##### C1. Siting AI data centers under endogenous rules (AMDO) — Pursue after program-director email

**Team:** Yifan Gao, Shi Jin, Yuefeng Hao; Mingzhou Jin as the only listed optimization collaborator (Co-PI role not confirmed).

**Research question and approach.** How should a planner site and expand capacity when (i) the chance of a moratorium or tariff change in a jurisdiction rises with the MW already sited and water already withdrawn there, spreading spatially; (ii) interconnection-queue information is revealed only by entering the queue; and (iii) substation and basin water headroom tighten with the planner's own decisions? The model class is multistage stochastic or distributionally robust programs with decision-dependent feasible regions. Contributions are decomposition, cuts and bounds on the value of modeling endogeneity, with an empirical hazard model fitted to moratorium and opposition data. C3 is one thrust, and the Tennessee Valley is the testbed.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Data center siting under queue delays treats delay as an exogenous scenario ([Abdelhady et al., ESCAPE 2026](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2606/LAPSE-2026.0251-1v1.pdf); [Shao et al., arXiv 2510.25118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25118); [Kim et al., Nat. Commun. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72324-9)). Decision-dependent facility location makes demand endogenous, not the feasible set ([Başçiftçi et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577); [Pantuso 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09816); [NSF 2534745](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2534745.json)). Stakeholder-approval constraints are static ([NSF 2620205](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2620205.json)). The motivation is strong: at least 75 projects worth about $130B were blocked or delayed in Q1 2026, and statewide moratorium bills appeared in 14 states ([Data Center Watch](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026)).

**Novelty.** Genuinely methodological. No facility-location model with a spatially contagious, decision-dependent regulatory hazard was found (web search only).

**Why us.** Both student papers provide the base models: Gao's robust siting under queue uncertainty and Jin's tariff- and moratorium-aware Tennessee Valley siting. Gao's NLP background can build the event data. The ISSE grant measures the noise, water and land-market drivers of local acceptance.

**Fundability.** It fits AMDO's "analytical methods to aid decision-making" with AI. It is not manufacturing, so the program director must confirm fit.

**Risks.** The team has a thin OR-theory record. The PI's 15 papers are in hydrology, climate and energy reviews, and both students are new. The hazard is hard to identify from few events. Strong theory groups (Minnesota, Pantuso) are competitors. Mitigate with preliminary computational results from the two student papers.

**Verdict: Pursue after a program-director email.** Submit after at least one student paper is out.

##### K1. Siting AI computing infrastructure under hydro-climate extremes: robust planning of coupled compute-power-cooling-water systems (ENG/CMMI-EBE) — Pursue

**Team:** Yuefeng Hao (sole PI). Students Yifan Gao and Shi Jin. Collaborators by single-sentence letters only: ORNL (E3SM scenarios) and Mingzhou Jin (optimization mentoring); none confirmed.

**Research question and approach.** How should multi-decade siting and capacity plans for data centers and co-located power account for nonstationary, spatially correlated compound extremes (heat-drought, low flow) that shrink cooling water and tighten thermal-discharge limits across a shared basin and grid at the same time? There are three thrusts. (i) Generate extreme scenarios from observations, remote sensing and E3SM-class projections. (ii) Build correlated-hazard robust or distributionally robust siting and capacity models with withdrawal and thermal-discharge constraints, extending the Gao and Jin models. (iii) Model moving policy constraints as uncertainty. Thrust (iii) must stay light so it does not duplicate AMDO C1.

**Education.** A "siting under extremes" data-science studio in IE 465/565 Applied Data Science; AI-for-infrastructure cases in IE 591; statistics-of-extremes modules in IE 200. The policy activity is the TDEC decision outputs.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Data center CAREER awards are all operational ([NSF 2541996](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2541996), [NSF 2544321](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2544321), [NSF 2340042](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2340042), [NSF 2045641](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2045641)). Energy-water CAREER precedents predate AI data centers ([NSF 1845931](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=1845931), [NSF 1847404](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=1847404)). **Novelty.** Foundational methods for siting interdependent compute-power-water infrastructure under spatially correlated hydro-climate extremes.

### One theme, six doors: which call should host which idea

All six screens arrived at the same intersection: **AI data center siting and water under compound flash drought and heat extremes**. Every screen also found that this intersection is unoccupied in funded work. No NSF award titled on "data center siting" turned up in the Award API ([NSF API query](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22data%20center%20siting%22)). Every data center CAREER award found is about operations: power flexibility, thermal management and carbon ([NSF 2541996](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2541996), [NSF 2544321](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2544321), [NSF 2340042](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2340042)). The DOE-funded data center energy-water work at PNNL uses state or HUC2 scale through GCAM-USA, without Earth-system drought extremes ([OSTI 2998851](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2998851), [OSTI 2584365](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/2584365)). The opening is real. The danger is that one good project sent to every door becomes a duplicate-submission problem.

**NSF duplicate-submission risk.** NSF returns without review a proposal that duplicates, or is substantially similar to, one already under review from the same submitter ([NSF PAPPG](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg); the section was not reopened in this research, so confirm the current wording). The EWRE, CMMI-EBE and CAREER screens each picked nearly the same project as their lead: robust siting and design of data centers under heat-drought uncertainty, with withdrawal and thermal-discharge constraints. Those three cannot be pending at once. EWRE's own page warns that "buildings and cities" belong in CMMI ([EWRE program page](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/energy-water-resource-engineering)), so the same proposal could even be moved between the two programs.

The partition below keeps one theme across six proposals while giving each a distinct question, decision variable and method:

| Host call | Idea | Distinct question | What it must NOT contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| USGS 104g (2026-11-12) | U1 + U2 (with U3, U4 as data layers) | How much water do Tennessee data centers use now, and how will that change seasonally and under extremes? (observation, estimation, validation) | Siting optimization as a deliverable; SMR thermal discharge (104b scope) |
| DOE BER (before 2027-04-01) | B1 | How predictable is sub-basin water availability for energy loads under compound extremes? (Earth-system science) | Optimization as the headline; boreal ecology |
| NSF GEO Core (rolling) | G1 (+ G2, G3 as aims) | What controls ecosystem ET and carbon recovery after flash drought? (no data centers) | Decision support; FFAR tool deliverables |
| NSF CBET EWRE (rolling, early 2027) | E1 (+ E3, E2 module) | Which cooling technology, water source and operating policy should a facility use under drought-heat uncertainty? Candidate sites are fixed inputs. | Regional siting and capacity expansion (reserved for CAREER) |
| NSF CMMI AMDO (rolling) | C1 (+ C3) | How do you site when rules (moratoria, tariffs, queues) respond to your own siting? (optimization methodology) | Hydro-climate hazard modeling as the core |
| NSF CAREER (2027-07-28) | K1 | Regional, multi-decade siting and capacity planning under spatially correlated compound extremes, with an education plan | Facility cooling co-design (EWRE); policy endogeneity theory (AMDO) |

Under this plan, CMMI-EBE idea C2 is held back. If the PI turns out to be ineligible for CAREER (see the CAREER section), or if program officers prefer EBE over a CAREER, C2 becomes the vehicle for K1's research and K1 is not submitted. Cross-agency submissions (DOE and NSF, USGS and NSF) are allowed, but each must list the others in Current and Pending Support, and the 104g program explicitly screens for "potential overlap or duplication between the proposed project and any other funded or proposed project" ([104g NOFO](https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/2c5fcf26-2264-42ae-9bc9-60f591fd770c/attachments/1931b7a5-8a2e-4007-a158-cbf31be312cd/Foa_Content_of_G27AS00164_100226.pdf)).

**Overlap with the lab's own grants.** Two funded projects sit at the center of this theme. The USGS 104b project, "Decision Support for TDEC to Reduce Freshwater Withdrawals and Thermal Discharges from Data Centers and Small Modular Reactors in Tennessee" ($84K, 1 year from 2027-01, PI), is closest to any idea centered on SMR thermal discharge or TDEC decision tools. That is why U5 is dropped and E2 is only a module. The UT ISSE project, "The Local Footprint of AI Data Centers: Noise, Water, and Land-Market Tradeoffs in Tennessee" ($60K, 1 year from 2026-08, PI), is closest to observatory or footprint ideas. That is why K4 is dropped, and it is the data source behind C1's acceptance-hazard model. Every new proposal should present these two grants as preliminary results, data access and a stakeholder link, with one sentence stating what the new proposal does that they do not. The FFAR flash-drought decision-support project (Co-PI) plays the same role for GEO and CAREER-K3, and the DOE/ORNL vulnerability grant (PI) for BER.
