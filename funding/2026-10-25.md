#### Ideas: USDA NIFA AFRI A1712 Rapid Response to Weather Events

**Key call rules and the qualifying event.** A1712 funds only **Extension or Integrated** projects (Standard or FASE grants), **12 months, at most $300,000 including indirect costs**. An application "must be submitted within 45 calendar days after a weather event or disaster", must "directly address effects associated with a recent weather event", and must include a statement on timing and relevance when the event is prolonged. One or more deliverables must be implemented **within 90 days of award**, the narrative is limited to **7 pages**, adoption potential must be measurable, and the program "limits applications to **one per PD per funding cycle**". Work with a preparedness, recovery, or mitigation focus is pointed to **A1701 CARE** instead. A mentoring plan is required for all FY2026 AFRI applications. After submitting, notify afri-rapidresponse@usda.gov ([NOFO MOD6, section 7g, Table 39](https://files.simpler.grants.gov/opportunities/10a7404c-3e2f-4355-9044-78c8bafc35cd/attachments/1d6366ca-2e3e-48a8-bea7-8303cbbd5500/FY26-AFRI-FAS-NOFO-P-MOD6.pdf)).

The qualifying event is the **2026 Tennessee fall flash drought**. Statewide, D1+ rose from **0.91% (2026-08-25) to 64.22% (2026-10-20)**. On the 2026-10-20 map D2+ is 48.15%, D3+ 18.80%, and the first **D4 (1.16%)** appeared ([USDM TN statistics](https://usdmdataservices.unl.edu/api/StateStatistics/GetDroughtSeverityStatisticsByAreaPercent?aoi=47&startdate=8/1/2026&enddate=10/25/2026&statisticsType=1)). The worst area is **West Tennessee**: D4 covers 64.15% of Lauderdale and 34.31% of Tipton County, D3 covers all of Chester, Fayette, and Hardeman, and 27 counties have some D3. Southern Middle Tennessee is also affected: Lincoln, Giles, Lawrence, Coffee, and Moore are 100% D2, and Marshall (35.69%) and Maury (27.45%) have D3. East Tennessee is largely outside the drought ([USDM TN county statistics](https://usdmdataservices.unl.edu/api/CountyStatistics/GetDroughtSeverityStatisticsByAreaPercent?aoi=TN&startdate=10/20/2026&enddate=10/20/2026&statisticsType=1); [NWS Huntsville statement, valid 2026-09-29](https://www.weather.gov/media/hun/DGT/DGT_HUN_10012026.pdf)). The same year already had a statewide spring drought (about 72% D2 and 27% D3 in May) that cut spring pasture growth ([UT Beef Live.Stock Companion, May 2026](https://utbeef.tennessee.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/127/2026/05/Live.Stock-Companion-Volume-30-May-2026.pdf)), so forage-livestock producers are hit twice before winter feeding.

**Recommended timing:** anchor the event on the 2026-09-29 USDM map (D2+ jumped to 33.75%, D3 first confirmed by NWS) and **submit by 2026-11-13** (9/29 + 45 days). The first D4 on 2026-10-20 gives a fallback anchor of 2026-12-04; the Grants.gov listing closes 2026-12-31. How NIFA dates the start of a prolonged drought was not verified (the A1712 page and FAQ on nifa.usda.gov could not be opened), so the earlier date is the safe one.

Because Hao can be PD on only one A1712 application, the five candidates collapse into **one Hao-led integrated proposal (R1)** with two internal objectives (R2, R3). R4 is absorbed into R1 and R5 is redirected to A1701 CARE. Every version depends on a **UT Extension partner who is not yet confirmed**: the talent-pool files list no extension appointment, livestock or forage specialist, producer network, drone equipment, or Tennessee flux tower.

##### R1. Flash drought forage and stock-water rapid response for Middle and West Tennessee cattle producers — Pursue (conditional on an extension Co-PD)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao (PD; flash drought, ET, remote sensing). Mingzhou Jin (UT ISE/ISSE, decision support; listed collaborator, role unconfirmed). Yifan Gao (geospatial and optimization pipeline). Shi Jin (from Spring 2027; dashboard and ETL). **Needed, unconfirmed:** a UT Extension forage or beef specialist as Co-PD (for example through the UT Beef & Forage Center), county agents in the D3/D4 counties, and the Tennessee Climate Office as a communication partner.

**Research question and approach.** How large are pasture and forage deficits and stock-water stress, by county and pasture, during the 2026 fall flash drought, and which hay-purchase, destocking, early-weaning, and stockpile decisions reduce losses before winter feeding? Adapt the lab's multi-source flash drought approach (ET, soil moisture, vegetation indices) to grazing land to produce a weekly pasture-deficit index relative to normal, and convert it into county hay-balance estimates using UT Extension's drought planning benchmark of 100–150 days of hay ([UT W 1222](https://utbeef.tennessee.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/127/2024/02/W-1222-Strategic-Resource-Management-for-Forage-Livestock-Systems-in-Drought_DWM-Suggestion-020824.pdf), seen only as a search snippet). Deliver through extension meetings, county-agent briefings, and a web dashboard. **90-day deliverable:** a first county-level forage-deficit map and bulletin plus agent training. Adoption is measured with pre/post producer surveys, reported decisions (hay sourcing, early weaning, culling, stockpile use), dashboard use, and agents trained.

**Closest existing projects and the gap.**
- A three-year, **$849,000 NASA-funded project led by UAH** with the Tennessee Climate Office (ETSU) improves flash drought identification, monitoring, prediction, and communication in the Southeast ([ETSU news](https://news.etsu.edu/articles/improving-drought-monitoring-in-the-southeast)). It serves drought monitors and USDM authors, not farm-level forage or water decisions.
- The FFAR project "Creating Decision Support Knowledge for Droughts & Floods" builds a weather-based decision-support tool for **row-crop producers** in the Tennessee River Basin, with East Tennessee watershed analysis, irrigation rates, and SWAT models ([FFAR grant page](https://foundationfar.org/grants-funding/grants/creating-decision-support-knowledge-for-droughts-floods/)).
- USDA Grass-Cast forecasts grassland productivity only for the Great Plains and the Southwest, April–July, with no Southeast coverage ([USDA Climate Hubs](https://www.climatehubs.usda.gov/hubs/southwest/topic/grass-cast-new-experimental-grassland-productivity-forecast-expanding); [UNL repository](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/droughtfacpub/174)).
- Earlier A1712 drought awards went to Idaho (about $290K) and Florida; their topics were not available ([NIFA Idaho, search snippet](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/about-nifa/announcements/nifa-invests-290k-response-drought-idaho); [secondary summary of Florida/Hawaii awards](https://samsearch.co/government-contracting-news/nifa-allocates-600k-for-climate-resilience-research-in-florida-and-hawaii-185515)).
- Gap: no source checked turns real-time ET and soil-moisture flash drought signals into county or pasture-level forage deficits and hay or destocking guidance for Tennessee cattle producers.

**Novelty.** Real but modest. The input data products exist; the contribution is turning flash drought signals into forage and stock-water decisions for humid-Southeast grazing systems, delivered during the event.

**Why us.**
- PI's flash drought papers: "Evaluating ecosystem water use efficiency and recovery dynamics during flash droughts" ([*Agric. For. Meteorol.* 2026, 378:110982](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2025.110982)) and a conference talk on flash drought recovery and WUE at the Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux site.
- PI's drought recovery and drought-index papers ([*J. Hydrol.* 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2023.129766); [*SERRA* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00477-021-02137-3)), satellite ET algorithms ([*AFM* 2019 soil-water-index Priestley–Taylor](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2019.107760); [*AFM* 2019 BMA](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2019.107759)), and heatwave hydrology across CONUS ([*J. Hydrol.* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2024.131368)).
- PI is **Co-PI on the FFAR grant "A multi-source remote sensing-based framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods under climate change" ($434,038, 3 years, from 04/2024)**.
- Mingzhou Jin is a frequent co-author on optimization and decision support; Shi Jin built a Django/ECharts dashboard on a 350K-record ETL and retrieval pipeline.
- *External evidence, not a team asset:* the FFAR grant page shows the same amount ($434,038) under the title "Creating Decision Support Knowledge for Droughts & Floods", with John Schwartz as PD and award year 2023 ([FFAR](https://foundationfar.org/grants-funding/grants/creating-decision-support-knowledge-for-droughts-floods/)). The CV gives a different title and a 04/2024 start; likely the same award, not confirmed.
- Why others have not done this: extension specialists have the producer network but not flash drought ET methods; the NASA/UAH team has monitoring tools but no channel to producer decisions. The lab supplies only the methods half.

**Fundability against A1712.** Addresses a documented, intensifying event; fits emphasis **a.3** (AI, connected sensors, decision tools) and **b.1** (livestock feed and water); integrated project with a 90-day deliverable and measurable adoption. Most of the $300K should go to delivery, because reviewers weigh immediate implementation.

**Risks.**
1. No extension specialist on the team; without a confirmed UT Extension Co-PD the proposal fails the immediate-implementation test.
2. The 45-day clock leaves about 19 days to 2026-11-13.
3. Reuse of the FFAR framework and data for pastures, and the data rights, are not documented; confirm with the FFAR lead.
4. Another UT/UTIA A1712 proposal for the same drought would compete for the same extension staff; coordinate internally.
5. Rain may end the drought before the award, but hay shortages persist through winter; the timing statement should say so.

**Verdict: Pursue** as Hao's single A1712 application, if a UT Extension forage or beef Co-PD confirms within about one week. Scope it to Middle and West Tennessee.

##### R2. Did grazing and soil-health practices limit 2026 flash drought damage? ET-based efficacy assessment and extension guidance — Revise (Objective 2 of R1)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao. **Needed, unconfirmed:** a UT soil-health or cover-crop extension specialist and NRCS Tennessee practice data.

**Approach.** Compare pastures and fields with and without management practices (stockpiled or rotational grazing; cover crops and no-till for crop fields) using satellite ET, water-use efficiency, and greenness drawdown and recovery, with the PI's flash drought WUE and recovery methods; present results at spring 2027 field days.

**Closest existing work and gap.** Satellite evidence on practice efficacy comes from the Corn Belt and is yield-based: across more than 90,000 fields, 3+ years of cover crops reduced maize yields by 5.5% and soybean yields by 3.5% on average, with larger losses in dry springs (Deines et al., *Global Change Biology* 2023; [PNNL record](https://data.pnnl.gov/group/nodes/publication/33662), [Stanford news](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2022/11/cover-crops-can-lower-yields)). Drought-year benefits are self-reported ([SARE 2012 survey](https://www.sare.org/publications/cover-crop-economics/an-in-depth-look-at-management-situations-where-cover-crops-pay-off-faster/when-soil-moisture-is-at-a-deficit-or-irrigation-is-needed)) and field soil-moisture evidence is mixed ([Practical Farmers of Iowa](https://practicalfarmers.org/research/cover-crop-effect-on-soil-water)). No ET- or recovery-based evidence from a documented flash drought in the humid Southeast was found.

**Why us.** The AFM 2026 flash drought WUE and recovery paper and the *J. Hydrol.* 2023 drought-recovery paper. Earlier unfunded proposals on these topics (SE CASC 2026, PI; AFRI A1102 and A1401 2025, Co-PI; 2023 NIFA ranching soil-health, Co-PI) are reusable text, not assets.

**Risks.** Practice-location data may not be available within 12 months; confounding is hard to control; most of the fall drought fell after the row-crop season; the NOFO steers recovery work to A1701. It is also close to GEO Core idea G1 in [2026-10-21.md](2026-10-21.md).

**Verdict: Revise.** Keep only a pasture-practice objective (stockpiling, rotational grazing) inside R1, framed as decision guidance for this winter, or move the full crop-and-pasture version to A1701 CARE.

##### R3. Drone and computer-vision ground truth for satellite forage-deficit estimates — Revise (validation sub-task of R1)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao with Shi Jin (from Spring 2027). A UAS platform, a Part 107 pilot, and farm access are needed; none is listed in the talent pool.

**Approach.** Repeated multispectral drone flights over a few demonstration pastures in D3/D4 counties, with a simple computer-vision biomass model, to calibrate and validate the R1 pasture-deficit index (months 4–12).

**Closest existing work and gap.** Drone pasture-biomass models (structure-from-motion and vegetation indices) exist and are weakest at low biomass, which is the drought condition ([PMC7833225](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7833225/)); Kentucky work uses ground-vehicle height sensing for alfalfa and tall fescue ([UKnowledge IGC 2023](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/igc/XXV_IGC_2023/Sustainability/8), search snippet). Standalone novelty is low.

**Why us.** Shi Jin's YOLO/EfficientNet detection and classification models and SLAM-based infrared + LiDAR registration for a substation inspection robot; no agricultural or drone-flight experience is listed.

**Risks.** Shi Jin starts in Spring 2027, so no 90-day deliverable can depend on him; no drone equipment is listed (the files do not say what the pending NIFA Equipment Grants proposal covers); winter dormancy pushes flights to spring 2027.

**Verdict: Revise.** Keep as a small a.3 technology-validation task inside R1.

##### R4. Flash drought monitoring and decision-support tool deployment (standalone) — Drop (merged into R1)

State-level flash drought monitoring for Tennessee and the Southeast is already funded through the UAH-led NASA project with the Tennessee Climate Office ([ETSU news](https://news.etsu.edu/articles/improving-drought-monitoring-in-the-southeast)), and USDM already reports weekly status. A generic "monitoring tool plus trainings" pitch also overlaps the FFAR project, where Hao is Co-PI, not lead. The lab's flash drought assets add value only when tied to a specific producer decision, which is R1.

##### R5. Stochastic-optimization irrigation and water allocation under flash drought for small and mid-size farms — Drop for A1712 (redirect to A1701 CARE)

The 2026 drought intensified from mid-September to October, after the main Tennessee corn and soybean irrigation season, so an irrigation-planning tool is preparedness for next season, which the NOFO sends to A1701. It also overlaps the FFAR irrigation objectives ([FFAR](https://foundationfar.org/grants-funding/grants/creating-decision-support-knowledge-for-droughts-floods/)) and UT Extension's irrigation scheduling program (Brian Leib is listed as UT Extension irrigation specialist on the [UT FEWSUS bio](https://fewsus.utk.edu/brian-leib); external evidence from a search snippet). **Why us for A1701:** the ongoing paper by Rui Xi, Yuefeng Hao, Brian Leib, and Mingzhou Jin, "A Decision-Support Model for Irrigation Planning under Flash Drought and Flood Risks Using Remote Sensing and Stochastic Optimization", and the lab's stochastic-optimization expertise (Hao, Gao), with Leib as a possible extension lead (unconfirmed).
