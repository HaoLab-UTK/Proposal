# NSF GEO Core (NSF 26-516), Earth Sciences: screening of Hao Lab flash-drought proposal ideas

Current as of 2026-10-22. Award links use NSF Award Search (`https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=<id>`); award facts come from the NSF Awards API (`api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards`). "Why us" claims cite only `talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md` (shortened to "hao-lab.md" below).

## Q1. What does GEO Core / Earth Sciences fund, and how is it reviewed?

### Takeaway
GEO Core funds foundational, hypothesis-driven geoscience. Hydrology goes to the Earth Sciences program under "water, landscape, and critical zone processes" (program officer contact is still the WaLCZ mailbox). Reviews use only the two standard NSF criteria, Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts. The solicitation also lists stated preferences: work that is transformative or challenges paradigms, early-career investigators, and AI that is "scientifically justified rather than incidental". Decision-support or tool-delivery framing is a poor fit.

### Cited Findings
- There are three programs: AGS, Earth Sciences (EAR) and OCE. The Earth Sciences program "supports foundational research that advances understanding of Earth's interior and surface processes... water, landscape, and critical zone processes; and life and environments through time." — [NSF 26-516 solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- The cognizant program officer contact for hydrology-type work is "Water, Landscape, and Critical Zone processes, email: WaLCZ@nsf.gov". — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- Proposals are accepted anytime, with 200-350 awards and $110,000,000 anticipated. Award types are standard grant, continuing grant or cooperative agreement. No LOI or preliminary proposal is required. Voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited. There are no limits on proposals per PI or per organization. No per-award size is stated. — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- NSF's stated priorities:
  - "intellectually ambitious and potentially transformative research; work that creates opportunities for exceptional early-career investigators; and proposals that leverage emerging technologies, including AI... The use of these tools should be scientifically justified rather than incidental."
  - Proposals "need not promise a near-term product, deployment, or commercial outcome."
  - NSF encourages up to 5-year requests, and RAPID, EAGER, RAISE and Planning proposals for exploratory work.
  
  — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- The measures of success are: peer-reviewed publications; "creation, curation, or expansion of publicly accessible data sets"; and "development of new methods, tools, models". — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- The solicitation sets several requirements and review rules:
  - GEO data policy: data, derived products and code go to a public long-lived archive on publication or within 2 years.
  - Off-site work needs a 1-page safety protocol.
  - Review is ad hoc and/or panel, usually with 3 or more external experts.
  - "all else being equal after merit review, NSF may give preference to institutions with lower indirect cost rates."
  - Awards may include termination-for-convenience provisions.
  
  — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)
- The solicitation encourages EPSCoR-jurisdiction submissions. Tennessee appears to be a graduated, non-eligible EPSCoR jurisdiction: [UAH-hosted NSF EPSCoR slides](https://www.uah.edu/images/colleges/science/computer-science/nsf_agendas/epscor_srai_2022.pdf); [U. Iowa EPSCoR page](https://research.uiowa.edu/nsf-epscor). Both are secondary sources; NSF's own eligibility table was not checked.
- The solicitation was published 2026-08-13 and last updated 2026-10-05. — [NSF 26-516](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/geo-core-geosciences-core-research-atmospheric-geospace-earth-ocean/nsf26-516/solicitation)

### Inferences
- Every idea must be framed as a mechanism or hypothesis about how water moves and is stored (critical zone, land-atmosphere exchange), not as monitoring or decision support. The FFAR decision-support work in hao-lab.md (section 2.4) should appear only as prior work and broader impact.
- The early-career emphasis helps: the PI has been an Assistant Professor since 2026-08 (hao-lab.md section 1).
- An AI component must answer a scientific question the physics alone cannot, such as finding regime thresholds. It should not be the headline.
- Because there is no budget cap, a 3-year single-PI project of moderate size is a reasonable assumption. Confirm typical EAR award sizes with WaLCZ@nsf.gov.

### Gaps
- The solicitation gives no typical award size or duration for EAR. A program officer should confirm this.
- Tennessee's current EPSCoR status was not verified on nsf.gov.
- The solicitation does not say whether ecohydrology with a strong carbon component is routed to EAR or co-reviewed with BIO/DEB Ecosystem Science.

## Q2. Which funded NSF projects (2020-2026) and papers already cover each idea?

### Takeaway
Flash drought is a crowded NSF topic. Land-atmosphere feedback during onset, AI/deep-learning forecasting, and high-resolution soil-moisture drought monitoring are all funded, much of it in 2023-2026. NSF funding is much thinner for:
- the recovery phase in humid forests;
- process-resolved ET partitioning during heat plus flash drought;
- how flash-drought deficits propagate into deep storage.

### Cited Findings

**Flash drought onset and mechanism (overlaps Ideas 1 and 5)**
- AWD 2414601, "Land-Atmosphere Interactions in Flash Drought Development" (U. Nebraska-Lincoln, Liang Chen, start 2024-08, AGS). It asks the role of soil moisture and vegetation in flash drought development in CONUS and whether it is distinct from conventional drought. — [NSF 2414601](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2414601)
- AWD 2303458/2303457, "Collaborative Research: Uncovering the Role of Land-Atmosphere Feedbacks on Flash Drought Intensification, Severity, and Expansion" (UW-Madison, Otkin; James Madison U., Gerken; 2023). It builds a database of slow vs. flash drought events and land-atmosphere coupling metrics, and asks "whether flash droughts indeed present a separate class of drought." — [NSF 2303458](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2303458)
- AWD 2144293, "CAS-Climate: CAREER: Analytical methods for understanding and predicting agricultural flash droughts in a changing climate" (Auburn, Di Tian, 2022). It is funded by Hydrologic Sciences/WaLCZ and covers ML causal inference of drivers, deep-learning sub-seasonal forecasts, and CMIP large ensembles. — [NSF 2144293](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2144293)

**AI and remote-sensing flash drought detection or forecasting (overlaps Idea 5 directly)**
- AWD 2616279/2616278, "Collaborative Research: CAIG: Leveraging Artificial Intelligence for understanding and forecasting flash drought development and propagation" (Ohio State, Xueru Zhang; UCLA, Yanlan Liu; start 2026-08-15; obligated $390,000 + $502,520). It proposes a "robust, interpretable, multi-modal, multi-task (RI-M³T) deep learning framework" built on vegetation, atmospheric and soil-moisture inputs. — [NSF 2616279](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2616279)
- AWD 2702406, "CAIG: Data-Centric Geoscience Foundation Model for Extreme Events" (SUNY Stony Brook, 2026-10). It encodes each flood or drought event "as a latent trajectory with spatial footprint" and uses physics-regularized decoding. — [NSF 2702406](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2702406)
- AWD 2544077, "CAREER: DroughtRx" (Rice, Noemi Vergopolan, 2026-09, ESH and WaLCZ). It produces 30-m daily soil moisture and drought products using land surface modeling, satellites and "physics-informed artificial intelligence", delivered through a dashboard. — [NSF 2544077](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2544077)
- AWD 2435046, "EAGER: CHIRRP: Community-driven Tools for Monitoring Flash Droughts" (Texas A&M, Mishra, 2024). — [NSF 2435046](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2435046)

## Q3. What gap remains for each idea, and is the novelty real?

### Takeaway
The most defensible gap is the recovery half of flash drought in humid, energy-limited forests (Southeastern US). The questions are:
- when and why the ecosystem crosses from energy-limited to water-limited ET;
- how T/ET shifts under heat;
- whether carbon-water recovery tracks soil moisture recovery or lags it through VPD and deep storage.

Novelty is real only if framed as testing a recovery paradigm against observations and Earth system model process representation. Novelty is weak for TWS-only, GHG-only, and AI-detection ideas.

### Cited Findings
- Existing NSF flash-drought awards target onset and intensification ([2414601](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2414601), [2303458](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2303458)) and forecasting ([2144293](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2144293), [2616279](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2616279), [2544077](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2544077)).
- Recovery-focused awards look at deep root-zone water in Texas forests ([2549574](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2549574)) and at watershed reconnection and aquatic carbon in the Southeast ([2627600](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2627600)). Neither targets land-atmosphere ET and carbon flux recovery after flash drought.
- Recovery responses differ by biome. Forests showed WUE increases and resistance during recovery while croplands and savannas did not. — [HESS 2020](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/24/5579/2020/)
- Heat changes flash-drought dynamics in a region-dependent way: heat slows onset in subtropical transition zones and slows recovery. — [Nature Geoscience 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-025-01719-y)
- A 2012 flash-drought analysis reports that recovery challenges soil-moisture-threshold recovery paradigms and that the model underestimates VPD's role. — [OSTI 3020938](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3020938) (snippet only)
- A soil-moisture critical threshold controls land-atmosphere coupling during drought-heatwave events. — [Commun. Earth Environ. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02977-9)
- The GRACE flash-drought framework exists at global scale. GRACE's coarse resolution and short record are acknowledged limits. — [RSE 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425725002974); [Haihe GRACE study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4151530/)

### Inferences
**Idea 1 (onset to recovery, SE US): real gap, if the framing moves from onset to recovery.**
- Proposed hypothesis: in humid forests, flash drought pushes ET across a soil-moisture threshold from energy-limited to water-limited for only a short time. Recovery of GPP and ET is then governed by atmospheric demand (VPD) and deep-storage refill, not by surface soil-moisture recovery.
- This tests a paradigm, which fits NSF's "challenge prevailing paradigms" language.

**Idea 2 (ET partitioning under compound heat and flash drought): partial gap.**
- Partitioning in general is covered (2422397).
- What remains:
  - how T/ET evolves through onset and recovery when heat co-occurs;
  - whether different ET model families (complementary relationship vs. Priestley-Taylor) disagree most exactly under these extremes, which would reveal missing physics.
- Too methodological to stand alone. It works best as the process engine inside Idea 1.

**Idea 3 (soil moisture, carbon and GHG): gap exists, but it is weakly testable with the team's assets.**
- No flash-drought-specific CH4/N2O evidence was found, so the gap is real.
- Answering it needs chamber or eddy-covariance GHG measurements, and hao-lab.md lists no field or lab GHG capability.
- Post-flash-drought CO2 (NEE, GPP, Reco from flux towers) can be folded into Idea 1.

**Idea 4 (TWS, flash vs. conventional drought): incremental as written.**
- It becomes novel if reframed as vertical propagation: whether a weeks-long surface deficit reaches deep and groundwater storage in humid basins, and whether depleted deep storage explains lagged ecosystem recovery.
- This complements 2549574 (a different region and method) and connects to the PI's TWS work (hao-lab.md section 2.2).
- The scale mismatch between GRACE (~monthly, coarse) and flash drought (weeks) is the core scientific and methodological challenge.

**Idea 5 (AI/CV detection): no defensible novelty as a standalone GEO Core proposal** given the 2026 CAIG awards and the published CNN, GAN and U-Net literature.
- AI could be justified inside Idea 1, for example data-driven detection of regime shifts and thresholds in multi-source fields, where it "reveals patterns invisible to prior methods" (solicitation language).
- Even then it should be a supporting aim.

### Gaps
- It is unverified whether the Christian et al. 2024 review or the Otkin/Gerken project already quantify humid Southeast recovery. Their full texts were not read.
- How many AmeriFlux forest sites in the Southeast cover recent flash droughts (2016, 2019, and later) was not checked. This is a key feasibility item.

## Q5. Main risks per idea

### Takeaway
The main risks are:
- overlap with recently funded NSF flash-drought awards (all ideas, worst for Idea 5);
- reviewers reading the work as applied monitoring rather than fundamental science;
- data limits (flux-tower coverage in the Southeast, GRACE resolution);

### Inferences: final ideas

**Final Idea A (Pursue; lead). Merges Ideas 1 and 2: "Beyond soil-moisture recovery: energy-to-water limitation thresholds, transpiration shifts, and lagged carbon-water recovery after flash droughts in humid Southeastern US ecosystems."**
- **Hypotheses:**
  - H1: humid forests cross a soil-moisture threshold into water-limited ET only briefly, and the threshold shifts upward under concurrent heat (high VPD).
  - H2: T/ET rises during onset under heat and stays perturbed after soil moisture recovers.
  - H3: GPP and ET recovery lag soil-moisture recovery, with the lag controlled by VPD and deep storage refill, not surface moisture.
  - H4: E3SM's land model reproduces recovery timing only if VPD-stomatal and deep-water processes are represented. This uses the ORNL E3SM link in hao-lab.md section 2.3.
- **Methods:**
  - AmeriFlux and flux towers, plus multi-source remote sensing (hao-lab.md section 2.2).
  - PT/CR ensemble with BMA to quantify structural uncertainty in ET and T/ET during extremes.
  - Model benchmarking with ORNL collaborators.
  - Optional: ML-based breakpoint and threshold detection, if justified.
- **Fit:** foundational land-atmosphere and critical-zone process science; produces a public event-recovery dataset (a stated measure of success).
- **Differentiation:** onset is covered by 2414601 and 2303458, and forecasting by the CAIG awards and 2144293. This idea targets recovery, humid energy-limited regimes, and the model-process test.
- **Why us (hao-lab.md):**
  - the PI's flash-drought, recovery and WUE expertise and ET-model portfolio (section 2.2);
  - the E3SM/CIVE collaboration with ORNL (sections 2.3 and 6);
  - the FFAR multi-source remote-sensing flash-drought project as prior work (section 4).
- **Risks:** Southeast flux-site coverage; reviewer overlap concerns; staffing a hydrology student.

**Final Idea B (Revise and pursue; second proposal or Aim 3 of A). Reframes Idea 4: "Does a flash drought reach the deep critical zone? Vertical propagation of rapid-onset deficits into root-zone, groundwater and terrestrial water storage, and its legacy for ecosystem recovery in humid basins."**
- **Needed changes:**

  - Combine GRACE/GRACE-FO TWS with root-zone soil moisture and groundwater observations to bridge the weeks-vs-months scale gap.

- **Why us (hao-lab.md):** terrestrial water storage and remote sensing (section 2.2) and ORNL ESM collaborators (section 6).
- **Risks:** GRACE resolution, and the need to find groundwater data and a method to bridge scales.
