#### Ideas: USGS WRRA 104(b) FY2027 via TNWRRC

**Call facts that shape the ideas.** Only state institutes apply to USGS, by **2026-12-30**. Faculty compete earlier in TNWRRC's internal fall pre-proposal round, whose FY2027 date is not posted; in 2019 it closed on **October 18**, about ten weeks before the federal date ([TNWRRC e-News Fall 2019](https://tnwrrc.tennessee.edu/news/enews/Fall2019/enewsFall2019.html); [TNWRRC funding page](https://tnwrrc.tennessee.edu/research/funding.html)), so the window may already be closed — the biggest risk. Each institute gets **$202,850 federal** for its whole program, with a 1:1 match and a 2027-03-01 to 2028-02-29 period ([NOFO](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/0c548397-9fef-4631-ae40-2455ff0904a1)); Tennessee's past allocation ran about $97K–$165K ([TNWRRC Flash Talk 2022](https://isse.utk.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2022/09/2-TNWRRC-Flash-Talk_2022.pdf)). A realistic faculty ask is about $30K–$60K federal (an estimate, not a TNWRRC figure; Kentucky caps FY2026 projects at $25K, [KWRI](https://kwri.uky.edu/copy-of-usgs-grant-applications)). TNWRRC also runs graduate student supplemental grants (about $5K in the past). Each project needs a "who wants it and why" statement; at least 25% of the institute's package must support EO14239 (local preparedness) and SO3444 (strategic AI); USGS screens every project for "overlap or duplication". The PI already holds a 104(b) TDEC award on data center and SMR withdrawals (from 01/2027), so the new proposal must stay off data centers. **Bottom line:** submit **I1** as the faculty pre-proposal (with I2 as its test case) and pair it with the student grant **I4** (Yifan Gao).

##### I1. Flash-drought triggers for Tennessee public water supply, tested on the fall 2026 drought — Pursue (lead faculty proposal)

**Team.** Yuefeng Hao (PI: flash drought, ET, drought indices); Yifan Gao (geospatial; leads I4); Shi Jin from Spring 2027 (ML benchmark). To recruit, all unconfirmed: a Duck River-area utility, the Duck River Agency, or a TDEC contact for a letter.

**Research question and approach.** Which sub-monthly indicators — soil moisture (satellite and model), ET and evaporative stress (Priestley–Taylor and BMA ET), or a triple-collocation combination — give the earliest skillful warning of the low flows and groundwater declines that matter at water-supply intakes, and how can they become trigger levels a community water system can adopt? (1) Retrospective analysis of past Southeast fall droughts (e.g., 2007, 2016, 2019, 2023) in the Duck River and one comparison HUC-8; (2) skill and lead-time scoring against USGS streamflow and groundwater percentiles; (3) the fall 2026 drought and its 2027 recovery as an out-of-sample test; (4) a trigger table and an open dataset for TDEC, utilities, and the Duck River planning process. Interpretable ML serves only as a benchmark (the SO3444 element).

**Closest work and the gap.** The fall 2026 event went from late-September dryness to Extreme Drought in Middle Tennessee within weeks ([WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/10/30/drought-worsens-across-middle-tennessee/); [NWS Huntsville 2026-10-20](https://www.weather.gov/media/hun/DGT/DGT_HUN_10202026.pdf)). Tennessee's 2010 drought plan "does not specify monitoring indicators nor the threshold values that would trigger drought declaration", while local systems keep their own triggers ([NewsChannel9/AP](https://newschannel9.com/news/local/noaa-report-tennessee-drought-plan-lacks-triggers-water-oversight-infrastructure-national-oceanic-atmospheric-administration-water-quality-public-health-ecosystems)). A draft 2026 Duck River Regional Drought Management Plan is listed but its PDF could not be opened ([draft URL](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/environment/water/water-public-notices/ppo_water_2026-09-04-draft-2026-duck-river-regional-drought-management-plan.pdf)). Sub-monthly (8–21 day) indicators give earlier warning for Southeast water supply ([Drought.gov 2025](https://www.drought.gov/news/facing-future-droughts-lessons-southeasts-2023-fall-flash-drought-2025-12-02)); soil-moisture indicators alone caught a 2022 Connecticut flash drought before declarations ([EGU26-8499](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-8499.html)). Closest funded work is national AI flash-drought forecasting ([NSF 2616279](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2616279)); NSF searches for "flash drought" + "water supply" returned no awards ([NSF API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22flash%20drought%22%20%22water%20supply%22)); the PI's FFAR tool targets agriculture. **Gap:** no study was found that calibrates flash-drought indicators to Tennessee public-water-supply triggers or tests them on a live event.

**Novelty.** Applied and regional, not a new forecasting method: turning sub-monthly indicators into intake-relevant triggers validated out of sample on 2026 — the kind of applied work 104(b) funds.

**Why us.** Flash-drought WUE and recovery (*AFM* 378, 2026) and the Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux recovery talk (AGU 2024); drought-index comparison for recovery time (*SERRA* 36, 2022, lead author); co-author, "Integration of multiple drought indices using a triple collocation approach" (*SERRA* 36, 2022); heatwave hydrology (*J. Hydrol.* 637, 2024); co-author on terrestrial water storage (*J. Hydrol.* 635, 2024); soil-water-index Priestley–Taylor ET and complementary-relationship BMA ET (*AFM* 279, 2019); Co-PI on the FFAR flash-drought decision-support grant ($434,038, from 04/2024); ongoing Xi–Hao–Leib–M. Jin irrigation decision-support paper under flash drought and flood risk.

**Fundability.** WRRI priorities Water Hazards or Water Scarcity and Availability; focus categories DROUGHT, WATER SUPPLY, HYDROLOGY; matches the NOFO's drought/wet-cycle emphasis and EO14239; a Middle Tennessee "who wants it" story is on public record; feasible in one year on public data with one graduate student.

**Risks.** Internal deadline may have passed; no utility or agency partner documented; trigger skill ideally needs intake or pumpage records (start with USGS gauges and wells; TDEC registration data are not public, [TDEC page](https://www.tn.gov/environment/program-areas/wr-water-resources/water-quality/water-withdrawal-registration-program.html), snippet only); 2026 recovery may be partial by 2028-02; lead with Middle Tennessee (West Tennessee status unverified).

**Verdict: Pursue.**

##### I4. NLP inventory of Tennessee community water system drought triggers (graduate student supplemental grant) — Pursue (linked to I1)

**Team.** Yifan Gao (student lead; full-time PhD student since Fall 2026); Hao (advisor). Shi Jin starts Spring 2027 and should not lead a fall 2026 student application.

**Approach.** Collect publicly posted drought management plans of Tennessee community water systems (required by TDEC), extract trigger indicators, thresholds, source types, and response stages into a source-linked inventory, and compare what utilities use with what I1's indicators signaled in 2026. Plans found online include MLGW's ([2022](https://www.mlgw.com/images/content/files/pdf/MLGW_DroughtManagementPlanFinal9202022.pdf)) and Nashville Metro Water's ([2016](https://filetransfer.nashville.gov/portals/0/sitecontent/WaterServices/docs/reports/Metro%20Water%20Services%2016%20Drought%20Management%20Plan%20-%20%2006-17-2016.pdf)) (search listings only, not opened).

**Gap and why us.** No inventory of Tennessee utility drought triggers was found; USGS harmonizes national withdrawal data, not plan triggers ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273350), snippet). Gao's undergraduate NLP and information-extraction research and source-linked dataset approach, plus the PI's drought-index record. Small, AI-enabled (SO3444), and sized to a student grant.

**Risks.** Coverage depends on how many systems post plans; keep it on water supply, not data centers. The original version (AI audit of TDEC registration records) depends on unconfirmed data access and overlaps 104g **U4** — Revise/Drop that version.

**Verdict: Pursue** as I1's student companion.
