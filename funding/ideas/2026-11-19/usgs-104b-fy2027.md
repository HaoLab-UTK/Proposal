## Q1. Call and TNWRRC facts: fit, award size, deadlines, eligibility

### Takeaway
104(b) is a small, state-run program. Tennessee received about $97K to $165K a year in the early 2020s, and the FY2027 federal amount is $202,850 per institute, shared across all of its projects. Each project is therefore likely in the tens of thousands of dollars and must train students. TNWRRC's internal pre-proposal deadline has historically fallen in mid-October; in 2019 it was October 18. The FY2027 internal call was not found online, so the window may already have closed or be closing. This is the single biggest risk.

### Cited Findings
- The federal NOFO asks for projects that "promote the national mission and objectives of the U.S. Geological Survey which are focused on providing water-quality and -quantity information, understanding water availability, addressing the influence of drought/wet cycles on water resources, and responding to water-related emerging needs". It adds that "specific areas of emphasis are at the discretion of the individual Institute or Center Directors" — [NOFO G27AS00170, listing](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/0c548397-9fef-4631-ae40-2455ff0904a1)
- Each project must name one WRRI Science Priority: Water Scarcity and Availability; Water Hazards; Water Quality; Water Policy, Planning, and Socioeconomics; Watershed Function; Water Technology and Innovation; or Workforce Development and Water Literacy. It must also name up to 3 focus categories from Attachment 2; DROUGHT, WATER SUPPLY, WATER QUANTITY, HYDROLOGY, MODELS, METHODS and MANAGEMENT AND PLANNING are all on the list. Each project also needs a 300-word abstract, a "Statement of regional or State water problem ... who wants it and why", and a "Related research" section with citations — [NOFO](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/0c548397-9fef-4631-ae40-2455ff0904a1)

At least 25% of each institute's submission must directly support Executive and Secretarial Order priorities: EO14303/SO3441 Gold Standard Science, EO14239 hazard and risk assessment for local preparedness, SO3444 strategic use of AI, and EO14313 — [NOFO](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/0c548397-9fef-4631-ae40-2455ff0904a1)

Faculty and affiliates of any accredited Tennessee college are eligible, as are full-time graduate students "in good academic standing". TNWRRC "annually solicits research priorities from governmental agencies in Tennessee including input from: TDEC, West TN River Basin Authority, TVA, TN Dept. of Agriculture, TDOT Environmental Division, and the USGS TN Science Center". Its 104b grants "require funding for graduate students"; prior years gave "$5,000 research support grants to graduate students". ## Q2. Landscape: the fall 2026 drought, Tennessee water-supply drought planning, and existing funded work and literature

### Takeaway
Tennessee is in a fast-onset fall drought for the second year running in Middle Tennessee, and water supply is a live state issue. The issues on record are the Duck River, a proposed Cumberland pipeline, and a statewide drought plan from 2010 that a NOAA-funded review found has no indicator triggers. A draft 2026 Duck River regional drought management plan is out for comment. Federal research on flash drought is national-scale and agricultural or AI forecasting (NSF CAIG 2026; the FFAR project where the PI is Co-PI). None found connects flash-drought indicators to the triggers used in Tennessee community water systems' drought plans.

### Cited Findings
- Fall 2026: "Severe rainfall deficits have plunged Middle Tennessee into Extreme Drought". After normal late-summer rainfall, dry patterns "took hold in mid-to-late September, rapidly expanding from moderate dryness into high-tier drought conditions within a matter of weeks". Nashville had under 1.25 in of rain after October 1, more than 4.5 in below normal, "among the driest stretches ... since 1961" (published 2026-10-29) — [WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/10/30/drought-worsens-across-middle-tennessee/)
- NWS Huntsville drought statement, valid 2026-10-20: "Drought conditions have worsened across the entire area over the last month, especially in southern Middle Tennessee". D3 is shown in northern Moore County, TN, and D2 in Lincoln, Moore and Franklin Counties, TN — [NWS HUN DGT 2026-10-20](https://www.weather.gov/media/hun/DGT/DGT_HUN_10202026.pdf)
- The Duck River Agency said parts of the Duck River watershed are in moderate to severe drought, which "could reduce stream flows, as well as put stress on water supplies ... affect groundwater levels". The river provides "drinking water for communities" (2026-10-02) — [WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/10/02/duck-river-experiencing-abnormally-dry-conditions-amid-ongoing-drought-middle-tn/)
- Earlier in 2026, streamflows were "below to well below normal" across East Tennessee in spring, with soil moisture 10–30% below normal — [NWS Morristown DGT 2026-04-02](https://www.weather.gov/media/mrx/DGT/DGT_MRX_04022026.txt) (search snippet only)
- In a previous summer, extreme drought plus utility withdrawals from growing communities left Duck River mussels stranded. The state proposed a Cumberland River pipeline to the Columbia area and put "$100 million towards seed money" in the Governor's FY2026 budget — [NewsChannel5](https://www.newschannel5.com/news/plan-on-the-table-to-build-pipeline-from-the-cumberland-river-to-help-with-duck-river-water-levels). Lawmakers reportedly approved $65M of the $100M asked — [Tennessee Lookout](https://tennesseelookout.com/briefs/gov-bill-lee-wanted-100m-for-duck-river-projects-lawmakers-approved-65m/) (search snippet only)
- Tennessee's statewide Drought Management Plan dates from 2010. A NOAA- and University of South Carolina-funded report found "It is not an operational plan as it does not specify monitoring indicators nor the threshold values that would trigger drought declaration levels and response actions". Local water systems "are required to have their own drought management plan that includes trigger points" (AP, 2023-02-06) — [NewsChannel9/AP](https://newschannel9.com/news/local/noaa-report-tennessee-drought-plan-lacks-triggers-water-oversight-infrastructure-national-oceanic-atmospheric-administration-water-quality-public-health-ecosystems)
- TDEC requires community water systems to have stand-alone drought management plans — [MLGW Drought Management Plan page](https://mlgw.com/about/DMP) (search snippet only). TDEC posted a "Draft 2026 Duck River Regional Drought Management Plan" (public notice 2026-09-04), and the Duck River Watershed Planning Partnership met on 2026-10-06 on "drought management recommendations". Both are listed in web search, but the tn.gov PDFs returned a 118-byte HTML stub when downloaded with curl and **could not be verified** — [draft plan URL](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/environment/water/water-public-notices/ppo_water_2026-09-04-draft-2026-duck-river-regional-drought-management-plan.pdf); [DRWPP 2026-10-06 URL](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/environment/water/duck-river-watershed-planning-partnership/drwpp_meeting-10-06-26-drought_management_recommendations.pdf)
- Lessons from the Southeast's 2023 fall flash drought: "Standard 30-, 60-, or 90-day drought indices may overlook critical transitions. Sub-monthly (e.g., 8 to 21 days) and inter-monthly (e.g., 45 days) drought indicators ... provide earlier warnings for sectors like agriculture and water supply". "Limited soil moisture sensors, weather stations, and mesonets leave major portions of the Southeast, especially rural areas, with sparse data". Impacts often show up before state task forces declare drought or the USDM degrades. Tennessee was among the largest Livestock Forage Disaster Program recipients in 2023 — [Drought.gov, 2025-12-02](https://www.drought.gov/news/facing-future-droughts-lessons-southeasts-2023-fall-flash-drought-2025-12-02)
- NSF 2616279/2616278 (Ohio State, UCLA; CAIG; $390,000 to OSU; 2026-08-15 to 2029-07-31): "Leveraging Artificial Intelligence for understanding and forecasting flash drought development and propagation". The project builds an interpretable multi-modal deep learning framework for flash drought prediction and vegetation–atmosphere interactions. It is a national research tool, not utility triggers — [NSF Award 2616279](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2616279)
- NSF Award API searches for "flash drought" + "water supply" and "data center" + "water availability" returned no awards. "flash drought" + Southeast returned only the CAIG pair — [NSF Award API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22flash%20drought%22%20%22water%20supply%22)
- Literature: soil-moisture-based indicators were the only ones that captured a 2022 Connecticut flash drought, and "drought declarations came only after intensification" — [Gesualdo & Hadjimichael, EGU26-8499](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-8499.html). A NASA white paper found the 2024 New York City drought revealed the system's vulnerability to flash droughts (87 days) — [NTRS 20260007407](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260007407). A USGS study of a Georgia forested headwater catchment found groundwater became the dominant moisture source during a flash drought, with streamflow approaching levels of past multiyear droughts — [USGS publication](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/ecohydrological-response-a-forested-headwater-catchment-a-flash-drought-southeastern). The September–October 2019 Southeast flash drought was linked to an extreme positive Indian Ocean Dipole — [NOAA repository 54601](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/54601) (search snippet only; authorship not confirmed)
- NOAA PSL runs a "Modernizing Drought Early Warning" pilot whose sectors include water utilities — [NOAA PSL Drought](https://www.psl.noaa.gov/drought/) (search snippet only)
- USGS already has national ML models of public-supply withdrawals for 2000–2020 (Water Resources Research 2025, doi:10.1029/2023WR036632). It also has a harmonized withdrawal dataset in which annual data were obtained for 81% of public-supply service areas and monthly data for 47% — [USGS harmonization paper](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273350); [USGS Water Use](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/water-use) (search snippets)
- Tennessee's Water Resources Information Act requires withdrawals of 10,000 gal/day or more to be registered (agriculture exempt). Public water systems file monthly pumpage reports, and non-public systems file an annual registration by Feb 15 through MyTDEC Forms — [TDEC Water Withdrawal Registration Program](https://www.tn.gov/environment/program-areas/wr-water-resources/water-quality/water-withdrawal-registration-program.html) (search snippet only; the curl download returned a 118-byte stub). No public, downloadable statewide registration dataset was found.

### Inferences
- The strongest "who wants it and why" for a 2027 project is drought and water supply in Middle Tennessee: the Duck River plan, the pipeline debate, community water systems' trigger points, and the fall 2026 drought. It falls under the NOFO's "influence of drought/wet cycles on water resources" and EO14239 (local preparedness), and AI-based indicators can address SO3444.
- The gap that survives the checks is operational: turning sub-monthly soil-moisture and ET flash-drought indicators into triggers for public water supply, tested against Tennessee streamflow, groundwater and utility pumpage. Flash drought science and forecasting are crowded nationally (NSF CAIG, NOAA); Tennessee-specific trigger work for water supply was not found.

### Gaps
- The Duck River draft regional plan and the DRWPP recommendations could not be opened, so their actual triggers and indicators are unknown.
- Whether TDEC shares monthly pumpage or registration records with researchers was not confirmed.
- USGS groundwater-level and streamflow percentiles for West Tennessee in fall 2026 were not checked. Only Middle Tennessee and southern Middle Tennessee are documented above.

## Q3. Idea I1 (merges candidates 2 and 3): Flash-drought triggers for Tennessee public water supply, tested on the fall 2026 drought (faculty research proposal)

### Takeaway
**Verdict: Pursue (lead faculty pre-proposal).** The project would build and test sub-monthly soil-moisture and ET-based flash-drought indicators as early-warning triggers for public water supply in Middle and West Tennessee watersheds. The fall 2026 event serves as the out-of-sample test and recovery case study: streamflow, groundwater and soil moisture, observed through 2027. It matches the 104(b) drought/wet-cycle emphasis, the PI's core published expertise, and a documented state planning need. It does not touch data centers or SMRs.

### Cited Findings
- Need: a statewide plan without indicator thresholds, local plans that must carry trigger points, a draft Duck River regional plan in 2026, and an extreme fall 2026 drought that developed within weeks — [NewsChannel9/AP](https://newschannel9.com/news/local/noaa-report-tennessee-drought-plan-lacks-triggers-water-oversight-infrastructure-national-oceanic-atmospheric-administration-water-quality-public-health-ecosystems); [WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/10/30/drought-worsens-across-middle-tennessee/); [NWS HUN](https://www.weather.gov/media/hun/DGT/DGT_HUN_10202026.pdf)
- Methodological support: sub-monthly indicators give earlier warnings for water supply in the Southeast — [Drought.gov](https://www.drought.gov/news/facing-future-droughts-lessons-southeasts-2023-fall-flash-drought-2025-12-02). Soil-moisture indicators detect flash drought that other indices miss — [EGU26-8499](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-8499.html)
- Closest funded projects: NSF CAIG 2616279/2616278, national AI flash-drought forecasting — [NSF](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2616279). The PI's FFAR Co-PI project, "A multi-source remote sensing-based framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods under climate change" ($434,038, 2024-04, 3 years), aimed at agriculture (talent pool CV). Tracked idea USDA A1712 R1 covers flash drought forage and stock water for cattle producers (funding/summaries/2026-10.md). Tracked idea NSF GEO G1 covers ecosystem carbon-water recovery after flash drought (same file).
- Team facts (talent pool only): the PI's flash-drought WUE and recovery paper (*Agricultural and Forest Meteorology* 378, 110982, 2026); heatwave effects on CONUS hydrological processes (*J. Hydrol.* 637, 131368, 2024); drought recovery papers (*J. Hydrol.* 622, 129766, 2023; *SERRA* 36, 2022, on drought indices and recovery time); triple-collocation drought index integration (*SERRA* 36, 2022, co-author); soil-water-index Priestley–Taylor ET and BMA ET models (*AFM* 2019 ×2); terrestrial water storage (*J. Hydrol.* 635, 2024, co-author); the FFAR flash drought decision-support project (Co-PI); and an ongoing irrigation decision-support paper under flash drought and flood risk (Xi, Hao, Leib, M. Jin). Yifan Gao contributes geospatial analysis, and Shi Jin (from Spring 2027) ML pipelines (PyTorch, LSTM) — [hao-lab.md](../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)

### Inferences
- **Research question and approach.** Which sub-monthly indicators of soil moisture (satellite and model), ET or evaporative stress (the PI's PT/BMA ET approaches) and their combination (triple collocation) give the earliest skillful warning of low flows and groundwater declines that matter for water-supply intakes? How should they be converted into trigger levels a community water system can adopt? Steps: (a) retrospective analysis of 2007, 2016, 2019, 2023 and other Southeast fall droughts (exact event list to be set from data) in selected HUC-8 watersheds, such as the Duck and one West Tennessee basin; (b) skill and lead-time evaluation against USGS streamflow and groundwater percentiles; (c) the fall 2026 drought and its 2027 recovery as an independent test; (d) a short trigger table and an open, FGDC-compliant dataset for TDEC, utilities and the Duck River planning process. Interpretable ML, used only as a benchmark against physically based indices, can carry the SO3444 element.
- **Gap and novelty.** National AI forecasting (CAIG) and agricultural decision support (FFAR) exist. No study was found that calibrates flash-drought indicators to public-water-supply triggers in Tennessee, or that uses a live event (fall 2026) as an out-of-sample test. The novelty is applied and regional, which is what 104(b) funds. It is not a new forecasting method.
- **Why us.** The PI has published on flash-drought recovery, drought-index integration, recovery-time metrics and ET modeling, and is Co-PI of a funded flash-drought decision-support tool (FFAR). The new project reuses that remote-sensing framework for a different sector (public water supply). That reuse is preliminary work, not duplication, as long as the proposal states it.
- **Fundability.** Fits WRRI priority Water Hazards (or Water Scarcity and Availability), focus categories DROUGHT, WATER SUPPLY and HYDROLOGY, the NOFO's drought/wet-cycle emphasis, and EO14239 local preparedness. The scope is feasible in one year at about $30K–$60K federal if it funds one graduate student and uses public data only.
- **Overlap.** No data centers or SMRs, so it is clearly separate from the funded 104b TDEC grant. Keep it separate from FFAR (agricultural decision tool), A1712 R1 (livestock and forage), GEO G1 (ecosystem carbon-water recovery) and NOAA N1 (heat cascades in the energy-water system). List FFAR in current support and state the sector difference.
- **Risks.** (1) The TNWRRC internal deadline may have passed. (2) Without a utility or TDEC partner, "who wants it" rests on public documents; seek a letter from a Duck River utility or the Duck River Agency (none is documented in the talent pool). (3) The trigger skill evaluation needs utility pumpage or intake data, so start with USGS gauges and wells as proxies. (4) Recovery from the 2026 drought may be partial by 2028-02.

### Gaps
- What indicators the draft Duck River plan already uses is unknown (the PDF could not be verified).
- No partner utility or agency letter is documented in the talent pool.

## Q6. Idea I4 (candidate 4, reshaped): AI extraction of drought triggers and supply data from Tennessee community water system drought plans (graduate student supplemental grant, Yifan Gao)

### Takeaway
**Verdict for the reshaped student version: Pursue (student supplemental grant linked to I1). Verdict for the original faculty version (AI audit of the withdrawal registration and permit records): Revise / Drop as standalone.** No public statewide registration dataset was found, so the original version depends on unconfirmed TDEC data access. It also overlaps with tracked 104g idea U4 (NLP disclosure audit) and with existing USGS national withdrawal-harmonization work. A smaller, data-available version uses public community water system drought management plans, which TDEC requires and some utilities post online.
