## Key question 1: Past winners — what has DEB Ecosystem Science (and the new BIO Core / OIE program) funded on drought and heat impacts on fluxes, recovery, ET partitioning, WUE, soil moisture and GHG, flux synthesis, NEON, and eastern forests?

### Takeaway
Ecosystem Science funds ecologist-led, theory-framed questions about *why* ecosystems respond as they do (legacies, memory, diversity, carbon allocation, nutrient feedbacks, VPD effects across scales), usually at $0.4–1.3M over 3–5 years, often as multi-institution collaboratives that pair a field or long-term site with network synthesis (NEON, AmeriFlux, FLUXNET, ForestGEO, LTER) and, increasingly, modeling or AI. Pure flux-partitioning or flash-drought hydrology is funded elsewhere (MacroSystems/NEON, GEO); no Ecosystem Science or OIE award was found on flash drought or on biodiversity as the control of flux recovery after rapid-onset extremes.

### Inferences
- **Common patterns.** (1) An explicit ecological-theory hook: legacies and memory, resistance vs. recovery, diversity–function, community–environment disequilibrium, nutrient oligotrophication. (2) A named site or long-term asset (SCBI ForestGEO, Hubbard Brook, a biodiversity experiment, an ongoing drought) combined with network synthesis (NEON, AmeriFlux, national inventories). (3) Ecologists or ecophysiologists lead; hydrologists, modelers and data scientists appear as co-PIs (Knighton–Wang, Ivanov, Ogle's statisticians). (4) 2026 awards add AI or Bayesian/ML methods when tied to a scientific question (Ogle 2026, Maavara KGML, SCALE-UP). (5) Broader impacts are training plus manager or policy engagement, not decision tools.
- **Why they were likely selected.** Each asks a mechanism question the field cannot yet answer with a plausible test (e.g., "is SCBI biomass decline caused by heat, nutrients or pests?"), uses unique long-term data, and has a team whose prior awards make feasibility obvious. Several frame societal value with forests as carbon sinks.
- **Opening for Hao Lab.** BIO has not funded flash drought or rapid-onset extremes as an ecological perturbation, and no award was found that tests whether biodiversity or structure controls the *recovery* of ecosystem fluxes after such events. The Southeast has only the 2026 RAPID (aquatic carbon reconnection) and the FIRE-MODEL fire-risk awards.
- **Fit risk.** An industrial-and-systems-engineering PI leading a BIO proposal is unusual but not unprecedented in spirit (engineering-hydrology co-PIs in 2243263 and 2111028). A UT ecologist co-PI and an ecology-first framing are needed. The PI's ESA Asian Ecology Section Early Career Award (2026) and ESA Council Representative role (CV) help show standing in the ecology community.

#### Full evaluation BE1 (lead)

**Title.** Does biodiversity buffer ecosystems against rapid-onset extremes? Diversity, canopy structure and the recovery of carbon and water fluxes after flash droughts and heatwaves across NEON.

**Team (talent pool names only).** Yuefeng Hao (PI). External co-PIs are candidates only (see collaborators file: a UT Knoxville ecologist and a NEON structural-diversity ecologist are the priority). Shi Jin could contribute a justified AI/CV sub-aim (BE11). Yifan Gao works on data center siting and should not be counted.

**Research question and approach.** H1 (insurance hypothesis applied to recovery): ecosystems with higher hydraulic-trait and structural diversity recover GPP and ET faster after flash droughts and heatwaves, not only resist them better. H2 (resistance–recovery decoupling): diversity controls recovery more than resistance under rapid-onset events, opposite to the grassland pattern where diversity aids resistance and dominant species shape recovery. H3: canopy structural diversity from NEON AOP lidar is a better predictor of recovery than species richness. Methods: event detection (flash drought, heatwave) at NEON flux sites and collocated AmeriFlux sites; event-scale resistance, recovery-time and WUE metrics from the PI's recovery papers; NEON plant diversity and woody-vegetation data, AOP lidar structure, community-weighted hydraulic traits; hierarchical Bayesian models with site random effects; a small ELM test with ORNL (BE7) and a CV canopy-stress layer (BE11).

**Closest existing projects and papers, and the gap.**

**Novelty.** Moves the diversity–stability debate from biomass and annual productivity to sub-seasonal flux recovery after rapid-onset events, and from species richness to hydraulic and structural diversity measured consistently by NEON.
