## 1. What the SON and BAA actually reward (fundability baseline)

### Takeaway
The SON asks for generalizable, statistically rigorous fusion that does three things: links legacy and emerging data, reconciles scales, and propagates uncertainty for installation-relevant variables. It must benchmark fusion against single-source products on accuracy, coverage, uncertainty, cost and labor, and it expects close work with installation natural resource or range managers. For pre-proposals, technical merit is weighted far above personnel, and personnel above cost.

### Cited Findings
- The SON lists five challenges: (1) integrate emerging and legacy monitoring, "including use of contemporary observations to improve inferences from legacy monitoring records"; (2) combine multiple emerging technologies; (3) statistical approaches that reconcile contrasting spatial and temporal scales; (4) spatiotemporal fusion; (5) characterize and propagate error and uncertainty. — [SERDP SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)
- Proposals "should leverage publicly available data sets, including new and existing earth observation platforms". They must account for each source's sampling and sensing properties, and must "evaluate the performance, efficiency, and limitations of data-fusion approaches relative to single-source approaches, including differences in accuracy, precision, spatial coverage, uncertainty, cost, and labor requirements." — [SERDP SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)
- "Investigators are expected to work closely with natural resource or installation range management communities in project design and execution". Proposers are encouraged to include subject-matter experts in the hazard or resource-management context. The ideal team is described as "remote sensing experts, environmental scientists, natural hazard experts, statisticians, computational scientists, and modelers", particularly effective with "DoW staff, end user communities". — [SERDP SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)
- Variables named in the SON's Benefits text: ecosystem structure and function, habitat availability, and occurrence and abundance of species (including threatened, endangered and at-risk species). The Background also names hazard assessments and "mission relevant environmental conditions". Example technologies: satellite remote sensing, UAS, eDNA, bioacoustics, camera traps, AI-assisted image analysis. — [SERDP SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)
- Limited Scope proposals are for "innovative approaches ... that entail high technical risk or have minimal supporting data", up to $350,000 and about 1 year, with the aim of proof-of-concept leading to a future Standard proposal. The SON separately invites Limited Scope work on "computationally efficient algorithms, and efficient approaches for working with natural infrastructure datasets of large size and high dimensionality." — [SERDP SON](https://serdp-estcp.mil/workwithus/callforproposal?id=c23979a6-37d8-4cb0-bd28-0c6f144eb3ae)
- BAA evaluation: SERDP Relevance is pass/fail. For pre-proposals, "Technical Merit is significantly more important than Personnel, which is more important than Cost". Technical merit stresses "a thorough understanding of the environmental issue", tasks with "milestones, critical paths, go/no-go decision points", and innovation weighed against risk. The transition plan counts for full proposals only. An optional "Existing Support" appendix (half a page per effort) must describe overlapping funded work. The approach should preferably be "structured in hypothesis-driven tasks". — FY28 SERDP Core BAA pre-proposal instructions (baa.txt, from the official BAA package)
- The 5-page pre-proposal is limited to one SON per pre-proposal, but "Proposers may respond to more than one (1) SON with separate pre-proposals." The text does not explicitly forbid two pre-proposals to the same SON. — baa.txt

### Inferences
- The SON is oriented toward ecology and species monitoring (habitat, species abundance, eDNA, bioacoustics), but it explicitly also covers "ecosystem properties, environmental variables" and "hazard assessments". An ecosystem water-stress, drought and fuel-condition product is in scope if it is framed as a natural-infrastructure condition variable that managers use, for example for training-land carrying capacity, prescribed-burn windows, wildfire risk, or habitat condition for at-risk species.
- The single-source vs. fusion benchmark (accuracy, coverage, uncertainty, cost, labor) is a required design element, not optional. Every idea needs a task built around it.
- Lack of a DoW installation partner is the biggest Personnel and Relevance weakness for Hao Lab. Nothing in the talent pool files shows any DoW installation contact.

### Gaps
- The SON does not name specific installations or regions. The webinar on 2026-11-20 may clarify whether proposals centered on drought, fire or condition (rather than species) are welcome.

## 2. Existing funded projects and literature (prior art per idea)

### Takeaway
SERDP has funded installation remote sensing mostly as single-technology demonstrations: TLS, hyperspectral, multi-sensor airborne packages, UAS integration. It has funded one multi-sensor satellite indicator project in Interior Alaska, but I found no funded SERDP/ESTCP project that builds a statistically rigorous, uncertainty-propagating fusion of satellite ET, soil moisture and vegetation records with flux towers and installation records across the MODIS-to-VIIRS/ECOSTRESS/HLS transition. The literature has mature global ET fusion (BMA, triple collocation) and western-U.S. live-fuel-moisture deep learning, but leaves the installation scale, the Southeast, and decision-linked uncertainty open.

### Cited Findings: SERDP/ESTCP projects (closest prior art)
- **RC24-8162**, "Demonstrating Improved Accuracy, Consistency, and Efficiency of DoD Ecosystem Monitoring Using Laser Scanning Technology": pine-dominated installation ecosystems; IntELiMon, which integrates terrestrial laser scanning with automated processing, plus the CruzAssist tablet tool. This covers ground LiDAR for vegetation structure and directly occupies idea 4's space. — [SERDP-ESTCP RC24-8162](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/01612720-0b87-4cb5-8503-eb904fc67862)
- **RC-201667**, "Demonstration of Multi-Sensor Airborne Mapping Tools for Ecosystem Management at Coastal DoD Training and Testing Sites": integrates multiple airborne sensors (search snippet: bathymetric LiDAR with topographic LiDAR, visible, infrared and hyperspectral) to map coastlines from upland to subtidal at reasonable cost. — [SERDP-ESTCP RC-201667](https://serdp-estcp.mil:443/projects/details/8afe3241-365b-4fd7-a93c-bb141491ce8b)
- **RC-1143**, "Application of Hyperspectral Techniques to Monitoring and Management of Invasive Weed Infestation": hyperspectral imaging combined with GIS into a predictive invasion model at military installations. — [SERDP-ESTCP RC-1143](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/858481d4-969f-4070-9e42-027e5f3addbe)
- **RC19-5218** (MCIEAST region-wide UAS integration into natural resource management) and **RC18-5187** (commercial UAS at Camp Lejeune for fire and coastal storm impacts). These show that UAS for installation habitat mapping is already demonstrated and transitioned. Search snippets for RC19-5218 also report that Blue UAS (2020 NDAA) cyber-security requirements caused implementation delays. — [RC19-5218](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/9dd0dd09-5acd-4e88-a1c7-52451272a96d); [RC18-5187](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/49ff1f14-56a2-4fe9-bf10-84c46f34386b)
- **NH24-9046**, "Measurements to Support Proactive Wildland Fire Management": collects real-time micrometeorology, vegetation and fuel-condition data during SERDP/ESTCP prescribed-burn campaigns for multi-scale fire-behavior and smoke modeling. This is a data collection effort, not a satellite fusion product. — [SERDP-ESTCP NH24-9046](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/ace90b68-342a-4c56-8aec-f5dd4cf061db)
- Public fuel-moisture data from SERDP-facilitated burn campaigns exist: Fort Stewart 2022 gravimetric fuel moisture, and 2023 DoD Wildland Fire Science Initiative fuel moisture at Eglin. — [WIFIRE Commons: Fort Stewart 2022](https://wifire-data.sdsc.edu/dataset/2022_fortstewart2022prescribedburncampaignfuelmoisturedata); [WIFIRE Commons: 2023 DoD WFSI Eglin](https://wifire-data.sdsc.edu/dataset/2023_fuelmoisturecontentdatafromthe2023departmentofdefensewildlandfirescienceinitiativeegli)
- 3D fuels work for DoD lands: 3DFuels (RC19-C1-1064) and the FastFuels/FuelsCraft line of work combine inventory, remote sensing and ML into 3D fuels. — [UW FERA 3DFuels](https://depts.washington.edu/fera/3dfuels); [SERDP-ESTCP resource page](https://serdp-estcp.mil/resources/details/adee307c-ed2c-43cd-b2cb-dfaa4c6d43ef)
- Interior Alaska project (one year; project number not visible): integrated VIS-NIR-TIR and microwave satellite data for three Earth System Indicators (terrain thermal state, ecosystem water stress, vegetation state) on DoD training lands. It tested ML downscaling of passive microwave to 30 m and 500 m, and used data through 2017. (Snippet; page not verifiable.) — [SERDP-ESTCP project 358c7356 (now Page Not Found)](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/358c7356-da06-46b9-b58d-653849375eb4)
- Recently funded resource-area projects near our topic: RC25-0189, "Reducing Uncertainty in Vegetation Stress Responses and Stress Tolerance on Ecosystems" (Los Alamos National Laboratory), and RC25-4949 (Princeton), on drone remote sensing plus fine-scale modeling of topography, soil moisture and shallow groundwater controls on vegetation resiliency. (Snippet; page not verifiable.) — [SERDP-ESTCP news item (search result, demo host)](https://demo.serdp-estcp.mil/newsitems/details/af1f2445-9c51-4292-9678-8d40b7562c94)
- Older installation vegetation work: remote-sensing vegetation and soil stratification with cross-scale index methods at Camp Williams, UT and Fort Bliss, TX; Camp Grayling satellite plus field-plot integration (ERDC report). (Snippets.) — [DTIC ADA390993](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA390993.pdf)
- Species data-fusion precedent for installations: RC-1469 (SEED) used monitoring data from all stakeholders for red-cockaded woodpecker modeling at Camp Lejeune. Integrated species distribution models are a mature USGS research line. — [SERDP-ESTCP project 239bd647](https://serdp-estcp.mil/projects/details/239bd647-dcad-4583-b3ec-ca7a79cec2ca); [USGS: Integrating multiple data sources in species distribution modeling](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70193265)

### Cited Findings: literature (2019-2026)
- BMA-ET (ESSD 2025) fused 30 ET datasets with Bayesian Model Averaging into a global product that beats most single datasets against FLUXNET2015. Global BMA ET fusion is therefore published and not novel by itself. — [ESSD 17, 6445 (2025)](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6445/2025/)
- Multi-product ET fusion against 462 flux towers (Agricultural and Forest Meteorology, 2025); triple-collocation-based uncertainty analysis and fusion of ET (2024). — [Xu et al. AFM 2025](https://lee-lab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-05/xu_afm_2025.pdf); [Atmosphere 15(12) 1410](https://mdpi.com/2073-4433/15/12/1410)
- The continuity problem is real and active. PML-V2.2 (ESSD 2026 preprint) builds MODIS (2000-2024), VIIRS (2012-2025) and AVHRR (1982-2020) ET/GPP records and reports high agreement in the overlap period (NSE 0.90 and 0.79 for annual anomalies), but at 500 m or coarser. The ECOSTRESS STARS ET product fuses VIIRS with HLS (30 m), and LP DAAC flags lower-quality periods (Jan-Sep 2023; noisy May-Jul 2025) and VIIRS-dominated artifacts when HLS is sparse. — [ESSD preprint essd-2026-94](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2026-94/essd-2026-94-manuscript-version2.pdf); [NASA Earthdata ECOSTRESS JET/STARS](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-eco-l3g-jet-002)
- Live fuel moisture content (LFMC) remote sensing is dominated by the western U.S. Rao et al. (2020, RSE) used Sentinel-1 plus Landsat-8 with a physics-assisted RNN at 250 m; adding SAR raised R² from 0.44 to 0.63. Miller et al. (2023, IJWF) made CONUS deep-learning LFMC projections from MODIS with a 3-month lead. A 2025 arXiv preprint maps LFMC with a multimodal EO foundation model. — [Miller et al. 2023, IJWF](https://www.publish.csiro.au/wf/Fulltext/WF22188); [arXiv 2506.20132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20132v2); [Stanford Konings Lab](https://koningslab.stanford.edu/research/wildfire-behavior)
- Southeast relevance: 2026 Southern Area Fuels and Fire Behavior Advisories reported persistent drought, D3-D4 drought from north Florida into the Carolinas (May 2026), and reduced live fuel moisture driving active fire behavior. — [SACC advisory 2026-04-30](https://gacc.nifc.gov/sacc/resources/predictive/Fuels_and_Fire_Behavior_Advisory_Southeastern_United_States_043026.pdf); [SACC Coastal Plain advisory 2026-05-14](https://gacc.nifc.gov/sacc/resources/predictive/Fuels_and_Fire_Behavior_Advisory_Southeastern_Coastal_Plain_051426.pdf)
- Field LFMC sampling is sparse (typically once or twice a month, poor spatial coverage), which motivates satellite estimates. Fire danger systems represent soil moisture mainly through simple meteorological drought indices. — [AMS conference paper on ML LFMC](https://ams.confex.com/ams/35AF14F6BG/webprogram/Paper424295.html); [NASA NTRS 20220018738](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220018738/downloads/20220018738.pdf)
- UAS/airborne LiDAR to GEDI to Sentinel/HLS upscaling of canopy structure is a crowded field: global GEDI plus Sentinel-2 canopy height; UAV LiDAR as an intermediate scale to GEDI; Florida statewide GEDI plus Sentinel-1 plus HLS canopy cover covering longleaf and other pines; LiDAR bias correction of GEDI/ICESat-2. UAV LiDAR plus hyperspectral fusion beats either source alone (88% accuracy, USGS). — [arXiv 2204.08322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.08322); [Remote Sensing 18, 1796](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01796/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01796.pdf); [USGS pub 70191185](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70191185)

### S1. Uncertainty-aware fusion of legacy and emerging water-stress observations for continuous ecosystem-condition monitoring on Southeastern installations (merges ideas 1 and 3): Pursue (lead, Standard)
- **Team (talent pool):** Yuefeng Hao (PI: ET models, BMA, drought, remote sensing, engineering statistics); Yifan Gao (geospatial analysis, scenario/robust decision layer); Shi Jin (data pipelines/ETL, ML, computer vision for cloud and quality screening); ORNL collaborators (Jiafu Mao, Xiaoying Shi, Yaoping Wang, Daniel Ricciuto, Forrest Hoffman) as possible advisors, involvement unconfirmed. Missing: a DoW installation natural resources or range partner (required in practice; none in the files); an ecologist or forester for condition thresholds; possibly a spatial statistician. All partners unconfirmed.
- **Research question:** Can a hierarchical Bayesian fusion improve accuracy, 30 m to 1 km coverage and calibrated uncertainty of installation drought-stress and vegetation-condition metrics over any single source, across the 2000-present record? The fusion would use legacy MODIS-era ET, LST and VIs; newer VIIRS, ECOSTRESS, HLS and SMAP; flux towers; and installation field records. Each source would get explicit error models, estimated by triple collocation where no ground truth exists. The project would also test whether contemporary high-resolution observations can retroactively sharpen the legacy record (SON challenge 1).
- **Approach (hypothesis-driven tasks):** T1: source error characterization (triple and extended collocation; flux-tower validation). T2: hierarchical Bayesian or BMA spatiotemporal fusion with sensor-transition terms (MODIS to VIIRS, ECOSTRESS gaps, HLS cadence). T3: back-casting the legacy record with contemporary observations. T4: fusion vs. single-source benchmark on accuracy, precision, coverage, uncertainty, compute cost and field-labor equivalents, as the SON requires. T5: a manager-facing condition and uncertainty product co-designed with installation staff. Go/no-go after T1/T2 (does the fusion beat the best single source on held-out towers or field data?).
- **Closest prior art and gap:** BMA-ET (ESSD 2025) and 462-tower fusion (AFM 2025) are global or coarse and stop at the merged product. PML-V2.2 handles continuity at 500 m or coarser. ECOSTRESS STARS fuses VIIRS+HLS but has flagged quality gaps. The SERDP Alaska project covers different indicators in a different region, through 2017. Gap: an installation-scale, Southeastern, legacy-to-emerging fusion with error models, uncertainty propagated to a decision metric, and a cost/labor benchmark.
- **Novelty:** real but incremental in method. Novelty comes from (i) explicit sensor-transition and back-casting terms, (ii) carrying uncertainty through to a manager-facing metric, and (iii) the required cost/labor benchmark. It must not be pitched as "BMA of ET products", which is published.
- **Why us (facts in talent pool files only):** PI's BMA ET fusion papers (AFM 2019, "Combining generalized complementary relationship models with the BMA method"; J. Hydrology 2022, BMA quantification of hydrological drivers of ET). PI co-authored triple-collocation integration of multiple drought indices (SERRA 2022) and a merged-ET "Simple Taylor Skill Fusion" conference paper (ISRS 2018). Soil-water-index Priestley-Taylor ET (AFM 2019). ET partitioning with flux towers (J. Hydrology 2021). Flash drought WUE and recovery from observations and models, including the Missouri Ozark AmeriFlux site (AFM 2026; AGU 2024). CONUS heatwave hydrology 2003-2022 (J. Hydrology 2024). Co-PI on the FFAR multi-source remote sensing framework and decision-support tool for flash droughts and floods (2024, $434K). ORNL CIVE v1.0 compound-indicator framework (under revision). PI teaches engineering statistics and applied data science. Jin built a 350K-record ETL pipeline with schema and QC checks.
- **Fundability:** hits SON challenges 1, 3, 4 and 5 directly, uses public EO data, and includes the required single-source benchmark. Weak on "work closely with installation management communities" until a partner is confirmed. Fits Standard (3 years suggested).
- **Risks:** (1) No DoW installation partner or installation field records; this is the biggest risk to Relevance and Personnel. (2) Reviewers may see drought/ET as less central than species and habitat. Mitigate by linking metrics to habitat condition for at-risk species, training-land condition, and burn windows, with an ecologist co-I. (3) Overlap with the FFAR grant (multi-source remote sensing flash-drought tool, agricultural focus) and with the October NSF GEO idea G1 (flash drought recovery in the Southeast). This must be declared in the Existing Support appendix and differentiated: installation natural lands, uncertainty to decision, and the legacy-to-emerging continuity method. (4) Flux tower density on or near installations is unknown.
- **Verdict: Pursue.** Change from the drafts: frame it around continuity and uncertainty (idea 3), not "BMA extended". Name 2-3 Southeastern installations only after a partner confirms. Add an ecologist or fire/forest co-I.

### S2. Flash drought to fuel-condition fusion for prescribed-burn windows and wildfire risk on Southeastern training lands (idea 2): Revise (make it the application aim of S1)
- **Team:** Hao (flash drought, soil moisture); Gao (decision and scenario layer for burn-window scheduling); Jin (ML). ORNL co-authors of the wildfire papers (Jiafu Mao, Xiaoying Shi) and Mingzhou Jin as possible collaborators, unconfirmed. Missing: a fire scientist or fuels expert, and an installation fire management office partner.
- **Research question and approach:** Fuse SMAP and in-situ soil moisture, ET/LST-based flash-drought indicators, SAR (Sentinel-1) and optical (HLS) vegetation water signals with the public SERDP burn-campaign fuel-moisture data (Fort Stewart 2022, Eglin 2023) and other field LFMC samples. Produce calibrated LFMC and drought-stress maps with uncertainty, then test whether the fused product improves burn-window and fire-risk decisions over single-source indices.
- **Prior art and gap:** LFMC deep learning is mostly western U.S. (Rao 2020) or MODIS-only CONUS (Miller 2023). NH24-9046 collects burn-campaign fuel data but does not build a satellite fusion product. Fire-danger systems use simple drought indices rather than soil moisture (NASA NTRS). I found no Southeast flash drought plus LFMC fusion study.
- **Novelty:** reasonably strong. Flash-drought dynamics as a predictor of rapid fuel drying in humid Southeastern pine systems, with uncertainty, is an open gap.
- **Why us:** PI's flash drought papers (AFM 2026; J. Hydrology 2023 Korea flash drought), soil moisture review (npj CAS 2025), "Wildfire-Power Grid Interactions" review (RSER 2026), and submitted review "Living with Fire in the Humid Southeastern US" (Earth-Science Reviews, under review).
- **Fundability:** good fit to "hazard assessments" and "mission relevant environmental conditions" and to multi-technology fusion. But as a standalone it competes with established fire-science teams, and Hao Lab has no fire-behavior or field-fuels record in the files.
- **Risks:** the field LFMC sample size in the Southeast may be small; the public campaign data cover burn days only; the team lacks a fire-science co-PI.
- **Verdict: Revise.** Make it Aim 3 (the decision application) of S1. Pursue it as a separate pre-proposal only if a fire-science co-PI and an installation fire-program contact join. In that case S1 and S2 must be clearly non-overlapping, and submitting both to the same SON is not advised.

### S4. Limited Scope ($350K, about 1 year): scalable spatiotemporal fusion with calibrated uncertainty for large natural-infrastructure datasets (idea 5): Revise (fallback to S1, same engine)
- **Team:** Hao (PI, statistics and fusion), Jin (ML, data pipelines/ETL), Gao (ML-assisted optimization, computational efficiency).

The test would run over 30 m multi-decade stacks for one or two installations, and the project would report accuracy, uncertainty calibration and compute cost vs. single-source products.
- **Prior art:** the fusion literature above. I did not find calibrated-uncertainty ML emulators of multi-sensor ET/condition fusion for installations, but this was not exhaustively searched (gap).

- **Why us:** PI's BMA and triple-collocation fusion record (above); Jin's 350K-scale ETL with schema validation and reproducible ML workflows; Gao's ML-for-optimization interest.
- **Risks:** pure-method framing may fail the Relevance pass/fail threshold if it does not tie to an installation variable and a user. Limited Scope output must lead to a Standard proposal.
- **Verdict: Revise.** Do not submit both S1 and S4 to this SON, because they would be near-duplicates. Decision rule: by about 2026-12-15, if an installation partner and an ecology or fire co-I are confirmed, submit S1 (with S2 as Aim 3) as a Standard proposal. If not, submit S4 as Limited Scope, tied to one named installation variable (drought stress or fuel condition) and to public data only, and frame it as the proof-of-concept for S1 in FY2029.
