## Q1. What does the call require of collaborators, and which constraints shape the roles?

### Takeaway
The CLaSH review explicitly rewards "new partnerships" and proposals that "link two or more processes or observatories". Subawards are not allowed, so each partner must either be a co-PI listed on the proposal or a collaborator who sends a letter. This makes CLaSH network members (KGS, USGS, CSDMS, UPRM) the most valuable partners, because they supply observatory data and process expertise at no cost to the budget.

### Cited Findings
- The award size is $20,000-$40,000 including indirect costs, for up to 12 months. The project runs May 1, 2027 to April 30, 2028, and the deadline is January 15, 2027 (11:59 PM PST) — [CLaSH RFP 2026-27](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf); [program page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- The RFP lists "Subcontracts/subawards" and "Participant Support Costs" as unallowable costs — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf)
- Letters of Collaboration are optional and "can confirm any collaborative relationships that are central to the proposed plans". A letter is required when the scope uses NSF or other facility instrumentation or services — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf)
- The review criteria include "Innovation & Collaboration — cross-disciplinary integration or new partnerships". The relevance questions ask "How well does the proposal link two or more processes or observatories, if the focus is not on Alaska or Puerto Rico?" — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf)
- PIs must be at U.S. institutions eligible for NSF funding, and "Early-career scientists and investigators are strongly encouraged to apply" — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf)
- The 2026-27 priorities are: (1) cross-observatory inquiry, with special interest in Puerto Rico or Alaska and in partner engagement and community-engaged research; (2) cascade modeling linked to the CLaSH Modeling Collaboratory and CSDMS (BMI, pyMT, Landlab), with ML/AI "especially welcome"; (3) radar/InSAR, remote sensing and AI at scale, including FAIR, AI-ready datasets; (4) event-driven investigations — [program page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- The existing CLaSH partnerships are EarthScope, NHERI RAPID, DesignSafe and OpenTopography — [program page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/)
- Awards are issued by the University of Michigan as subawards to the grantee institution — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf)

### Inferences
- A co-PI at a second institution cannot receive part of the funds, because CLaSH issues one subaward to the PI's institution and the PI cannot subaward onward. A co-PI outside UTK would therefore be unfunded, or would submit a separate, complementary small grant. Two linked proposals from the same team carry an overlap risk that should be checked against the FAQ, which was not reviewed here.
- The cheapest high-value structure has three parts. Hao Lab is PI (UTK budget). A UTK geomorphologist (Anna Marshall) joins as co-PI, since the same institution means no subaward is needed. KGS/CLaSH Appalachia observatory, USGS and CSDMS staff join as letter collaborators or data partners.

### Gaps
- The Proposal FAQ document was not opened. It may state whether multi-institution co-PIs or paired proposals are allowed.
- No public list of 2025-26 small-grant awardees was found. The archived 2025-26 page lists priorities but no winners, and searches found none. So no candidate below could be confirmed as a past CLaSH small-grant winner.

## Q3. Which partner organizations should be named?

### Takeaway
Five organizations give the proposal observatory access, agency data, model infrastructure and community reach without any subawards.

### Cited Findings
- **Kentucky Geological Survey / CLaSH Appalachia (Kentucky) observatory partners (CLIMBS):** "CLIMBS will provide data, CLaSH personnel will develop modeling software and provide training". The SP&M lab runs statewide landslide inventories, ML susceptibility, stochastic storm transposition and debris-flow integration projects — [UKNow](https://uknow.uky.edu/research/climbs-researchers-go-global-finding-hazard-solutions-through-nsf-s-clash-initiative); [KGS SP&M](https://www.uky.edu/KGS/labs/spam/projects.htm). Role: observatory/data partner (letter). Weight: **High**.
- **USGS Landslide Hazards Program / Geologic Hazards Science Center:** led the multi-agency Helene response through the LASER team. It mapped 2,217 landslides and produced susceptibility-based hazard models — [GSA Today](https://www.geosociety.org/gsa-today/january-2026/insights-into-widespread-landsliding-in-southern-appalachia-from-hurricane); USGS Helene inventory data release ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/data/preliminary-landslide-inventory-landslides-triggered-hurricane-helene-september-2024)). Role: data partner (letter). Weight: **Medium-high** (agency).
- **CSDMS (Community Surface Dynamics Modeling System, CU Boulder):** named in the RFP (BMI, pyMT, Landlab), and a CLaSH senior person sits at INSTAAR/CSDMS — [RFP](https://www.geoclash.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/CLASH-Small-Grant-RFP-2027.pdf); [Team Bios](https://www.geoclash.org/team-bios/). Role: modeling infrastructure (letter if staff support is used). Weight: **High**.
- **North Carolina Geological Survey (Asheville Regional Office):** co-author institution of the Helene synthesis, together with the Virginia Dept. of Energy geology program and the NWS Greenville-Spartanburg office — [GSA Today](https://www.geosociety.org/gsa-today/january-2026/insights-into-widespread-landsliding-in-southern-appalachia-from-hurricane). Role: state-agency data and engagement partner. Weight: **Medium-high**.
- **University of Puerto Rico at Mayagüez (CLaSH Puerto Rico observatory link):** CLaSH senior personnel plus Track I landslide center partner — [Team Bios](https://www.geoclash.org/team-bios/); [NSF 2224973](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2224973). Role: cross-observatory data partner. Weight: **Medium-high to High** (MSI, priority observatory).
- Optional: **OpenTopography**, an existing CLaSH partnership, for lidar and DEM access — [program page](https://www.geoclash.org/small-grant-program-2026-2027/).

### Inferences
- If the proposal uses OpenTopography or EarthScope services, the RFP calls for a letter from that facility.
- The KGS-plus-USGS-plus-UPRM combination makes a "cross-observatory" (Appalachia–Puerto Rico) design possible without new fieldwork, which suits a $40K, 12-month budget.
