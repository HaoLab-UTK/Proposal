## Call scope and fit rules (NSF 26-515, AMDO, EBE)

### Takeaway
NSF 26-515 is a broad CMMI umbrella: proposals accepted anytime, 225-275 awards, $100M, no PI limits. Review uses the standard Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts criteria. AMDO's language centers on manufacturing, services, supply chains, and "operation and design of these systems." EBE explicitly covers infrastructure "systems-of-systems," extreme events, and cascading effects. EBE is the natural home for data-center/grid/water infrastructure work. AMDO is plausible only for decision-methodology work framed as operations and design of service/infrastructure systems. Confirm AMDO fit with a program director.

## Idea 1: Robust data center siting and capacity expansion under interconnection-queue uncertainty

### Takeaway
This space has filled up quickly since 2025. At least six recent papers co-optimize data center siting or interconnection with grid expansion under uncertainty, including a two-stage stochastic siting model with queue delays (ESCAPE 2026) and a Nature Communications 2026 siting framework built around the queue. A "comparative study of optimization approaches" is not a foundational CMMI contribution. Verdict: **Revise / merge** into Idea A below, where the queue is modeled as decision-dependent (endogenous) information and timing uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- Abdelhady, Iakovou, Pistikopoulos (Texas A&M), ESCAPE 36 (2026). The paper presents "a two-stage stochastic optimization framework with binary capacity constraints co-optimizing data center location and BTM energy portfolios" under "interconnection queue delays" and price volatility. Case study: 5 GW in ERCOT; "queue delays drive 2.7 GW bridging investments" — [LAPSE 2026.0251 / Syst Control Trans 5:396-403](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2606/LAPSE-2026.0251-1v1.pdf)
- Shao, Yu, Wong: stochastic 15-year joint planning of data center siting/sizing and power-system expansion in PJM, two-stage stochastic program with enhanced Benders; up to 55 GW data center peak; joint planning cuts investment cost 12.6% (from search summary; published in Int. J. Electrical Power & Energy Systems vol. 173, 2025 per summary) — [arXiv 2510.25118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25118)
- Vabson, Zater, Sajadi, Baker, Hodge (Jan 2026): county-level U.S. data center siting model covering power grid, telecom, climate, water use, and collocated generation — [arXiv 2601.16315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16315)
- Kim, Dong, Xie, "Flexibility-aware framework for efficient planner-initiated siting of data center," Nature Communications (online 2026-05-16). Per the search summary, it screens sites before they enter the "multi-year interconnection queue" using firm/pause/shift flexibility envelopes on a synthetic 2,000-bus Texas system — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72324-9) (title and date verified on page; method details from search summary)
- Chen and Zheng (Apr 2026): capacity expansion model with AI data center spatial/temporal flexibility; they list "a two-stage stochastic programming framework for handling AI load uncertainty" as future work (future-work point from search summary) — [arXiv 2604.05376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05376)
- Butt, Fatima, Li (Aug 2026): ICP-AI, developer-side framework minimizing grid interconnection capacity with PV/BESS and workload flexibility — [arXiv 2608.29359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29359)
- NSF awards on data centers and the grid sit in ECCS/EPCL or CISE, not CMMI. Examples: CAREER 2541996 (Lei Fan, U. Houston, $500,018, 2026-05), co-optimizing power system operations and data center flexibility; 2610262 (Khazaei, Lehigh, $518,881), hyperscale data center stability in inverter-dominated grids; 2624228/2624229 (UW/ASU), grid-data center power electronics; 2450111 FlexDC (Coskun, BU, CISE) — [NSF API 2541996](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2541996.json); [2610262](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2610262.json); [2450111](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2450111.json)
- No NSF award with "data center" plus siting/location in the title was found in CMMI 2020-2026 via the API — [NSF Award API query](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22data%20center%22%20siting)

### Inferences
- **Gap that remains:** existing models treat queue delay as an exogenous scenario. An earlier draft of these notes said it was "a parameter or scenario set" in LAPSE 2026.0251; only the abstract was read, so that wording is not verified. None models the queue as a stochastic process whose information arrives because of the planner's own choices: entering a queue, paying study deposits, or withdrawing. That is decision-dependent information discovery ("Type 2" endogenous uncertainty), and it is a recognized OR methods frontier. This framing is my own; no source names it for data centers.
- **Novelty:** low as currently scoped ("comparative study"). It is moderate to high if reframed around endogenous queue information plus multistage adaptive siting.
- **Hao Lab assets:** Yifan Gao's ongoing paper on this exact topic and dissertation direction (geospatial + optimization for data center siting); PI's ISSE and USGS 104b data center grants; Mingzhou Jin (UT ISE/ISSE) as a frequent optimization co-author — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)
- **Program:** AMDO (decision methods for design and operation of systems), pending PD confirmation.
- **Risks:** crowded field led by power-systems groups with grid-model depth (Texas A&M, Harvard, UCR, Houston). Hao Lab has no listed power-systems modeling or OR-journal track record. Yifan Gao is a first-year PhD student (since 2026 Fall). The PI's 15 papers are in hydrology, climate, and energy-review journals, not OR journals.

### Gaps
- Full text of Shao et al. and Kim et al. was not read; whether either treats queue position as endogenous could not be confirmed from abstracts.
- Status of Yifan Gao's paper (draft or submitted) is not stated in hao-lab.md.

## Idea 2: Siting under moving rules (tariff- and moratorium-aware optimization with NLP-extracted policy rules)

### Takeaway
The policy problem is real and fast-moving: Q1 2026 saw a record 75 projects worth about $130B blocked or delayed, and statewide moratorium bills appeared in 14 states. LLM extraction of siting ordinances already exists for wind and solar (NREL COMPASS, Energy and AI 2024), but I found no data center ordinance database and no optimization model whose constraint set is uncertain because it was extracted from text. Verdict: **Revise.** Drop "NLP as a data tool" as a contribution. Keep a foundational thrust, "optimization with text-derived, uncertain constraints," and fold it into Idea A or present it as standalone Idea C.

### Cited Findings
- Data Center Watch Q1 2026: "At least 75 data center projects worth approximately $130 billion were blocked or delayed in a single quarter". "More than 300 state data center bills were filed in the first six weeks of 2026 alone, with statewide moratorium proposals introduced in 14 states"; "legislative sessions introduced formal regulatory uncertainty"; opposition groups "more than doubled ... across 49 states" — [Data Center Watch Q1 2026](https://datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026)
- Secondary reports: about $200B blocked or delayed in H1 2026 (methodology not verified); Gallup (March 2026) found 7 in 10 Americans oppose local AI data centers; Maine governor vetoed a statewide moratorium in April; Minneapolis adopted a 6-month pause in June — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/nearly-200-billion-worth-of-data-center-projects-have-been-blocked-or-delayed-this-year-2000815054); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/data-center-pushback-hit-record-levels-in-early-2026-report-says.md) (secondary sources; not independently verified)
- Buster et al., Energy and AI (2024): LLMs plus a decision-tree framework extract wind siting ordinances at 85-90% accuracy for NREL's database. The tool became INFRA-COMPASS; NREL released 2025 wind and solar ordinance datasets built with GPT-4.1 and warns "LLMs ... make mistakes. Validate the data" — [NREL publication](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/supporting-energy-policy-research-with-large-language-models-a-ca/); [Solar ordinances 2025, data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/u-s-solar-siting-regulation-and-zoning-ordinances-2025); [COMPASS GitHub](https://github.com/rajeee/compass) (details from search summary)
- A web search for policy- or regulatory-uncertainty-as-endogenous in facility location found no direct match. The closest are a 2018 IISE Transactions review of international facility location under uncertainty and decision-dependent demand models — [UAEU repository](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/modeling-international-facility-location-under-uncertainty-a-revi/)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** Two pieces exist separately: (a) LLM-extracted rule databases (wind/solar only) and (b) siting optimization with fixed constraints. Nobody has (i) built the same thing for data center rules (zoning, moratoria, large-load tariffs) or (ii) propagated extraction error and rule-change risk into the optimization.
- **Methodological novelty (if reframed):** treat each extracted rule as an uncertain constraint with a calibrated error probability, for example by using held-out validation to build chance constraints or ambiguity sets over constraint parameters and structure, and model rule changes as a regime-switching stochastic process. This is a foundational "text-to-constraint uncertainty" contribution that a CMMI reviewer could accept. Whether calibrated NLP error has already been used to construct uncertainty sets elsewhere was not checked exhaustively; do a targeted search before writing.
- **Hao Lab assets:** Shi Jin's ongoing paper on tariff- and moratorium-aware siting in the Tennessee Valley; Yifan Gao's NLP/information-extraction background from undergraduate research; Shi Jin's 350K-paper ETL and retrieval platform; Expertise Index entry "Energy policy, tariffs, moratoria, interconnection queues" (Shi Jin, Yifan Gao) — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)
- **Program:** AMDO (decision methods), or as a thrust inside Idea A.
- **Risks:** reviewers may see the NLP piece as engineering rather than science. Tennessee Valley focus may read as a case study. Shi Jin starts in 2027 Spring, so his paper is pre-enrollment work. Rule data must be collected and validated by the team.

### Gaps
- No verified count of U.S. local data center moratoria or ordinances; Data Center Watch counts blocked projects, not rules.
- No source found on TVA-specific large-load tariffs; not researched here.

## Idea 3: Systems-of-systems resilience of co-located AI data centers, power grid, and water supply under climate extremes (EBE)

### Takeaway
This is the strongest "why us" and the cleanest program fit. EBE asks for systems-of-systems, extreme events, and cascading effects in water and energy services. Existing NSF awards study data-center/grid electrical stability (ECCS/CISE) or interdependent-infrastructure resilience in general (CMMI CAREER, Ceferino). None found couples data center demand, grid, and water supply under compound heatwave and flash drought. Verdict: **Pursue**, with a foundational modeling core rather than a Tennessee case study.

### Cited Findings
- First Street (via E&E News, 2026): 54% of data centers are in areas facing "chronic stress conditions such as extreme heat or water scarcity"; the report warns of periodic shutdowns from power outages or water restrictions — [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/heat-waves-and-drought-imperil-data-center-operations-report-says/) (secondary; First Street report itself not opened)
- During a 2026 U.S. heatwave, grid operators asked data centers to switch to backup generation; nearly 80% of potable water used in data center cooling evaporates — [Al Jazeera, 2026-07-03](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/3/us-heatwave-raises-alarms-over-ai-data-centre-energy-demands) (news; not verified against primary data)
- Siting in drought-prone areas: more than two-thirds of data centers built or in development since 2022 are in drought-prone areas (2025 analysis); nearly two-thirds of 809 planned U.S. data centers are in areas that saw drought in the past year (2026, NOAA drought data) — [KUNC/Mountain West News Bureau](https://www.kunc.org/2025-06-02/ai-data-centers-growing-drought-stricken-mountain-west-analysis); [Fertilizer Daily](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260612-ai-data-center-boom-increasingly-targets-drought-prone-regions-across-the-u-s/) (secondary)
- Indirect water from data center electricity in the U.S. is estimated at over 200 billion gallons/year (AWWA 2025 report, as cited in a preprint; search summary only) — [engrXiv preprint](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/7795/12658/10948)
- CMMI CAREER 2541220 (Ceferino, UC Berkeley, $616,762, 2026-09): computational framework for rare-event resilience planning in interdependent infrastructure (hospitals, transportation, power); uses UQ, stochastic optimization, and surrogate modeling for earthquakes, wildfires, and hurricanes. Not data centers, not drought or heat — [NSF API 2541220](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2541220.json)
- CMMI 2611922 (Garcia, ASU, $455,000, 2027-01): design principles for decentralized power and water infrastructure (prosumers), incentives, and regulations — [NSF API 2611922](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2611922.json)
- CMMI 2622226 et al. (Zaniolo/Duke, UC Davis, UC Riverside, NC State, 2026-09): AI-enabled decision support for water utility rates and investment under drought, with supply-demand-finance feedback — [NSF API 2622226](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2622226.json)
- CISE/OAC 2613408 (Haining Wang, Virginia Tech, $1.2M, 2026-10): power-related vulnerabilities and cascading failures between AI data centers and grids (cyber/power safety focus) — [NSF API 2613408](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2613408.json)
- WaterWise (arXiv 2501.17944) co-optimizes carbon and water footprints in geo-distributed workload scheduling (operations, not infrastructure resilience) — [arXiv 2501.17944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17944)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** no NSF-funded or found published work models the joint failure and adaptation dynamics of (data center cooling load + grid + thermoelectric/SMR cooling water + municipal water supply) under compound heat-drought events, or designs siting and cooling choices for adaptability. The data-center/grid awards are electrical-dynamics or cyber; the interdependent-infrastructure awards omit data centers and hydro-climate drivers.
- **Foundational contribution (needed for EBE):** (1) a coupled hydro-thermal-electric systems-of-systems model in which heat raises cooling load and cuts generator and cooling-water capacity at the same time (a positive-feedback stressor); (2) a compound-event stochastic generator (flash drought plus heatwave) feeding resilience metrics; (3) design-for-adaptability optimization (siting, cooling-technology mix, water-sourcing portfolios) evaluated against those metrics. The contribution is the coupled model, the metrics, and the design theory; Tennessee is the testbed.
- **Hao Lab assets (strongest across all ideas):** PI expertise in flash drought, heatwaves, ET, and soil moisture; remote sensing (MODIS, multi-source); E3SM collaboration with ORNL (Jiafu Mao, Forrest Hoffman, and others). USGS 104b grant (PI, 2027-01, $84K) on decision support for TDEC to reduce freshwater withdrawals and thermal discharges from data centers and SMRs. ISSE grant (PI, 2026-08, $60K) on local footprint (noise, water, land). A submitted paper on water use tied to data center electricity demand. FFAR flash-drought decision-support grant (Co-PI). Wildfire-power grid interaction work. Students cover siting optimization (Gao) and AI/vision/digital-twin skills (Jin: SLAM-based infrared + LiDAR registration for a digital-twin substation robot) — [hao-lab.md](../../../../talent-pool/labs/hao-lab.md)
- **Why nobody else has done it (inference):** the data center groups found are power-systems or computer-systems groups without hydro-climate extremes expertise. Hydrology groups rarely run infrastructure siting optimization. Hao Lab sits at that intersection, and its active state-agency (TDEC) decision-support grant gives it a real-world testbed and stakeholder.
- **Program:** EBE (primary). Possible co-review with AMDO if the design-optimization thrust is strong.
- **Risks:** EBE reviewers (civil/infrastructure) may want explicit engineering-science advances, not impact assessment. Grid modeling depth is missing from the listed team, so consider a power-systems co-PI (no such person is named in hao-lab.md). Overlap with CBET EWRE (energy-water) is possible, so ask the PD which program fits. Avoid being read as a "data center footprint study."

## Idea 5: Decision-dependent uncertainty: siting choices change local grid/water constraints and community acceptance (moratoria feedback)

### Takeaway
Decision-dependent-uncertainty (DDU) facility location is an active theory area: Başçiftçi-Ahmed-Shen 2021, Pantuso 2025, Kang-Bansal 2025, and CMMI/OE awards to Qi Zhang (2021) and Yiling Zhang (2026). All of it treats demand or asset health as endogenous. None found treats the feasible set as endogenous, i.e., cumulative siting decisions raising the probability that a moratorium removes or tightens future sites or that resource limits tighten. That is a real gap with a clear data center motivation. Verdict: **Pursue**, merged with Ideas 1 and 2 as the AMDO lead (Idea A).

### Cited Findings
- Başçiftçi, Ahmed, Shen, EJOR (2021): distributionally robust facility location where demand moments are functions of location decisions; exact MILP reformulation for piecewise-linear moment functions — [arXiv 1912.05577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05577)
- Pantuso (Sep 2025): stochastic facility location with "combinatorially many decision-dependent distributions" (up to 2^17 distributions), solved by a finitely convergent method with new cuts — [arXiv 2509.09816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09816)
- Kang and Bansal (Jan 2025): two-stage stochastic programs where first-stage decisions affect both objective and feasible region of the second stage; handles DRO with decision-dependent ambiguity sets — [arXiv 2501.01081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01081)
- CMMI/OE 2030296 (Qi Zhang, U. Minnesota, $306,746, 2021-2025): adaptive robust optimization with endogenous uncertainty and active learning in smart manufacturing (asset health) — [NSF API 2030296](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2030296.json)
- CMMI/OE 2534745 (Yiling Zhang, U. Minnesota, $334,264, 2026-09 to 2029-08): "Data-Driven Infrastructure Planning for Uncertain and Changing User Demand"; long-term planning when users respond to the services provided (building energy, parcel lockers); advances stochastic optimization — [NSF API 2534745](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2534745.json)
- CMMI/OE 2620205/2620206 (Martinez Mori, Toriello, 2027-01): transit design with "stakeholder approval as an explicit requirement in the optimization process" via cooperative game theory — [NSF API 2620205](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards/2620205.json)
- ECCS 2114100 (Dehghanian, GWU, 2021): stochastic programming under decision-dependent uncertainties for power-grid resilience — [NSF Award API query](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=%22decision-dependent%20uncertainty%22)
- Social acceptance in energy siting: Lohr et al. (Energy, Sustainability and Society, 2024) add endogenous disamenity costs to a German wind model. An MDPI Sustainability study reports that no prior optimization method had incorporated contention risk and the delays it causes for wind siting (from search summary) — [Energy, Sustainability and Society 2024](https://doaj.org/article/61a629733af14d00b1a52ed9e2ae387c); [MDPI Sustainability 15(13):10231](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/13/10231)

### Inferences
- **Gap:** existing DDU work makes demand distributions (Başçiftçi; Pantuso; Y. Zhang) or asset degradation (Q. Zhang) depend on decisions. The data center case needs something different: (a) feasible-set uncertainty that depends on cumulative decisions, where the probability of a moratorium or tariff change in a jurisdiction rises with MW sited and water withdrawn there, which is a threshold or contagion hazard; (b) resource constraints (substation headroom, basin water availability) that tighten endogenously and shift with climate; (c) information revealed by acting (queue studies). Static disamenity costs (Lohr) and approval constraints (Martinez Mori/Toriello) do not capture the stochastic, cumulative, spatially contagious nature of moratoria.
- **Novelty:** genuinely methodological. It needs a new class of multistage stochastic or DRO programs with decision-dependent feasible regions and spatial contagion, plus decomposition and cuts, plus an empirical hazard model estimated from moratorium and opposition data (for example Data Center Watch counts).
